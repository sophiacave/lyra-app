<div class="lesson-section">
  <span class="section-label">The Signal</span>
  <h2 class="section-title">When to scale.</h2>
  <p>Scaling isn't always the right move. But you'll know it's time when three things are simultaneously true:</p>
  <p><strong>1. You're consistently turning down work.</strong> Not occasionally — consistently. If you're saying no to good-fit clients every week, you've hit your capacity ceiling.</p>
  <p><strong>2. Your AI workflows are documented.</strong> You have repeatable processes that someone else could follow. If your entire operation lives in your head, you're not ready to scale.</p>
  <p><strong>3. Your income has plateaued.</strong> You're working at full capacity and can't raise rates further without losing clients. The only way to earn more is to add capacity.</p>
  <p>If only one or two of these are true, focus on optimizing what you have. Raise rates. Improve your AI workflows. Get more selective with clients. Scaling too early is the number one small business killer.</p>
  <div class="tip-box">
    <strong>The overnight test:</strong> If you couldn't work for a week, would your business survive? If yes, you have systems worth scaling. If no, build those systems first.
  </div>
</div>

<div class="lesson-section">
  <span class="section-label">Models</span>
  <h2 class="section-title">Three ways to scale.</h2>
  <p><strong>1. The Subcontractor Model.</strong> Hire other freelancers for specific tasks while you manage client relationships and quality control. You become the project manager. AI handles coordination — generating briefs, reviewing output, tracking progress. Best for: people who love client work and want more capacity.</p>
  <p><strong>2. The Productized Service Model.</strong> Turn your most popular service into a standardized package with fixed pricing and delivery. "SEO blog post package: 8 posts/month for $2,400." AI handles most of the production. You handle quality control and client strategy. Best for: people who want passive-ish income.</p>
  <p><strong>3. The Micro-Agency Model.</strong> Build a small team (2-5 people) with specialized roles. You're the creative director. Others handle production. AI augments everyone's output. Best for: people who want to build something bigger than themselves.</p>
  <div class="callout">
    <strong>Most AI freelancers choose the productized service model.</strong> It scales the best with AI because you can systematize everything. Same inputs, same process, same outputs — AI loves consistency.
  </div>
</div>

<div class="lesson-section">
  <span class="section-label">AI Management</span>
  <h2 class="section-title">Using AI to manage people.</h2>
  <p>Managing subcontractors or a small team is where most solo freelancers struggle. AI makes it dramatically easier.</p>
  <p><strong>Brief generation:</strong> Use AI to create detailed project briefs from client requests. Include context, requirements, style guides, and examples. A good brief prevents 80% of revision requests.</p>
  <p><strong>Quality review:</strong> Before reviewing subcontractor work yourself, run it through AI for a first-pass quality check. Ask AI to compare the deliverable against the brief and flag any gaps.</p>
  <p><strong>Communication:</strong> Use AI to draft update emails to clients and feedback messages to subcontractors. Maintain professionalism across all touchpoints without spending hours on emails.</p>
  <p><strong>SOPs:</strong> Document every process as a Standard Operating Procedure. AI can generate SOPs from your descriptions of how you do things. "Here's how I write a blog post from start to finish..." becomes a step-by-step guide anyone can follow.</p>
  <div class="tip-box">
    <strong>Pay well, expect well.</strong> The cheapest subcontractor is rarely the best value. Pay top rates for reliable talent, and your management overhead drops dramatically.
  </div>
</div>

<div class="lesson-section">
  <span class="section-label">Systems</span>
  <h2 class="section-title">Systems that scale without you.</h2>
  <p>The goal of scaling is to remove yourself from production while maintaining quality. Here are the systems that make that possible:</p>
  <p><strong>Client onboarding pipeline:</strong> Automated intake forms, welcome sequences, and project kickoff templates. A new client should be fully onboarded with minimal hands-on time from you.</p>
  <p><strong>Project management:</strong> Use tools like Notion, Asana, or Linear with AI integrations. Every project follows the same workflow: intake, brief, production, review, delivery, feedback. Automate status updates.</p>
  <p><strong>Financial tracking:</strong> Automate invoicing, expense tracking, and profitability analysis per client. Know your margins. If a client isn't profitable after accounting for management overhead, raise the price or drop them.</p>
  <p><strong>Knowledge base:</strong> Build a living document of FAQs, processes, brand guidelines, and lessons learned. AI can help you maintain and query this knowledge base.</p>
</div>
