<div class="lesson-section">
  <span class="section-label">Non-Negotiable</span>
  <h2 class="section-title">Never work without a contract.</h2>
  <p>A verbal agreement is worth the paper it's not printed on. Every single project needs a written agreement — even small ones. Especially small ones. The $500 project with no contract is exactly where disputes happen.</p>
  <p>The good news: AI makes contract creation fast. You can draft a solid freelance agreement in minutes. But you need to understand what's in it, so here are the clauses that matter.</p>
  <p><strong>Essential contract clauses:</strong></p>
  <p>1. <strong>Scope of work.</strong> Exactly what you're delivering. Be specific. "10 blog posts, 1,500 words each, including one round of revisions" not "content creation."</p>
  <p>2. <strong>Payment terms.</strong> Amount, schedule, and method. Include late payment penalties. Standard: 50% upfront, 50% on delivery for new clients.</p>
  <p>3. <strong>Timeline.</strong> Delivery dates with buffer built in. What happens if the client is late with feedback or materials.</p>
  <p>4. <strong>Revision limits.</strong> How many rounds of revisions are included. Additional revisions billed at what rate.</p>
  <p>5. <strong>Kill clause.</strong> Either party can cancel with 14 days notice. Work completed to date is billed and paid.</p>
  <div class="tip-box">
    <strong>The revision trap:</strong> "Unlimited revisions" is a red flag from either side. Cap revisions at 2-3 rounds. Anything beyond that signals a scope or communication problem that a contract should address upfront.
  </div>
</div>

<div class="lesson-section">
  <span class="section-label">AI & IP</span>
  <h2 class="section-title">AI changes the IP conversation.</h2>
  <p>Intellectual property law around AI-generated content is still evolving. Here's what you need to know right now as a freelancer:</p>
  <p><strong>Disclosure:</strong> Some clients require you to disclose AI tool usage. Be upfront about your process. Most clients don't care how you produce the work — they care about the quality. But hiding AI usage when asked directly is a trust breaker.</p>
  <p><strong>Ownership:</strong> In most jurisdictions, AI-generated content isn't automatically copyrightable. However, content that is substantially edited, curated, and directed by a human likely qualifies for copyright protection. Your editing and creative direction matter legally.</p>
  <p><strong>Client data:</strong> Never paste confidential client information into AI tools without permission. Many AI platforms use inputs for training. If a client shares proprietary data, confirm your AI tool's data policy or use a private/enterprise AI instance.</p>
  <div class="callout">
    <strong>Add an AI clause:</strong> Include a clause in your contracts that addresses AI tool usage. Something like: "Contractor may use AI-assisted tools in the production process. All deliverables are reviewed, edited, and approved by Contractor before delivery. Final work product IP transfers to Client upon full payment."
  </div>
</div>

<div class="lesson-section">
  <span class="section-label">Red Flags</span>
  <h2 class="section-title">Contract red flags that cost you money.</h2>
  <p><strong>"Work for hire" with no clear scope.</strong> This means the client owns everything you create during the engagement — even work they didn't specifically ask for. Make sure "work product" is tightly defined.</p>
  <p><strong>Net-60 or Net-90 payment terms.</strong> You deliver today, get paid in 2-3 months. If a large company insists on this, charge a premium or negotiate milestone payments.</p>
  <p><strong>Non-compete clauses.</strong> Some clients try to prevent you from working with their competitors. For freelancers, this is usually unreasonable. Push back or negotiate a very narrow scope and time limit.</p>
  <p><strong>Unlimited liability.</strong> Your contract should cap your liability at the total contract value. You're not insuring their entire business against loss.</p>
  <p><strong>"All rights in perpetuity."</strong> Reasonable for the specific deliverables. Not reasonable for your underlying methods, templates, or tools. Retain your right to use your processes and frameworks with other clients.</p>
  <div class="tip-box">
    <strong>Use AI to review contracts.</strong> Paste the client's contract into your AI assistant and ask: "Identify any clauses that are unfavorable to the contractor, and explain why." It's not legal advice, but it's an excellent first-pass review.
  </div>
</div>

<div class="lesson-section">
  <span class="section-label">Templates</span>
  <h2 class="section-title">Build your contract template library.</h2>
  <p>Create three contract templates and reuse them:</p>
  <p><strong>1. Single project agreement.</strong> For one-off deliverables. Include scope, payment (50/50), timeline, revisions, and kill clause.</p>
  <p><strong>2. Retainer agreement.</strong> For ongoing monthly work. Include monthly scope, hours or deliverable caps, payment schedule, and 30-day cancellation notice.</p>
  <p><strong>3. NDA / Confidentiality agreement.</strong> For when clients share sensitive information. Keep it mutual — your business information is protected too.</p>
  <p>Use AI to generate first drafts, then have them reviewed by a lawyer once. After that, customize them for each client in minutes.</p>
</div>
