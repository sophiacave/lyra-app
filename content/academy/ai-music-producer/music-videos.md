<div class="lesson-section">
  <span class="section-label">The Visual Revolution</span>
  <h2 class="section-title">A music video used to cost $5,000 minimum. Now it costs $5 and an afternoon.</h2>
  <p class="section-text">Music videos drive streams. Spotify tracks with official videos get 30-50% more plays. YouTube is the world's largest music platform. And until recently, creating a video required cameras, actors, editors, and a budget most indie artists couldn't afford.</p>
  <p class="section-text">AI video generation changed the game entirely. Tools like Kling, Runway, and Pika can generate cinematic footage from text prompts. Combined with AI image generators and free editing software, you can produce a complete music video in an afternoon for under $50.</p>
  <p class="section-text"><strong>The tool landscape:</strong></p>
  <p class="section-text"><strong>Kling AI:</strong> Produces the most cinematic AI video right now. 5-10 second clips at up to 1080p. Excellent for narrative scenes, character shots, and atmospheric footage. Text-to-video and image-to-video modes. ~$0.10-$0.50 per clip depending on quality settings.</p>
  <p class="section-text"><strong>Runway Gen-3:</strong> Strong motion consistency and style control. Best for abstract, artistic, and experimental visuals. Great for electronic and ambient music videos. $15/month for 125 credits (~40 clips).</p>
  <p class="section-text"><strong>Pika:</strong> Fast generation, good for quick iterations. Less cinematic than Kling but more accessible. Free tier available. Works well for lyric video backgrounds and visualizers.</p>
  <p class="section-text"><strong>Midjourney + img2vid:</strong> Generate stunning still images in Midjourney, then animate them with Kling or Runway's image-to-video feature. This gives you maximum control over the visual style because you craft each frame's starting point.</p>
</div>

<div class="lesson-section">
  <span class="section-label">Three Video Styles</span>
  <h2 class="section-title">Pick your style based on genre, budget, and vibe.</h2>
  <p class="section-text"><strong>Style 1 — The Visualizer:</strong> Abstract, mood-driven visuals that pulse and flow with the music. No narrative, no characters — just atmosphere. Perfect for lo-fi, ambient, electronic, and instrumental tracks. Easiest to produce. Generate 8-12 atmospheric clips in Runway or Pika, sync cuts to beat changes. Add color grading for cohesion. Total production time: 2-3 hours.</p>
  <p class="section-text"><strong>Style 2 — The Narrative:</strong> A visual story that complements the lyrics. Characters, settings, emotional arcs. Works for pop, R&B, country, and hip-hop. More complex but more engaging. Plan your shots using a storyboard (even a simple sketch for each scene). Generate character-consistent scenes in Kling using image-to-video with the same reference images. Total production time: 4-8 hours.</p>
  <p class="section-text"><strong>Style 3 — The Lyric Video:</strong> Animated text over visual backgrounds. Professional lyric videos get millions of views — they're not a lesser format. Generate moving backgrounds with AI, overlay lyrics using CapCut (free), Canva Video, or DaVinci Resolve. Sync text appearance to vocal timing. Total production time: 2-4 hours.</p>
  <div class="tip-box">
    <strong>The hybrid approach:</strong> Mix all three styles within one video. Open with a visualizer intro, transition to narrative scenes for verses, and switch to lyric-focused moments for the chorus. This creates visual variety that keeps viewers watching. Retention rate matters for YouTube's algorithm.
  </div>
</div>

<div class="lesson-section">
  <span class="section-label">The Production Pipeline</span>
  <h2 class="section-title">Concept to YouTube in five steps.</h2>
  <p class="section-text"><strong>Step 1 — Concept & Storyboard (30 min):</strong> Listen to your track 3-5 times. Write down the images and scenes that come to mind. Sketch a simple storyboard: what happens in the intro, verse 1, chorus, verse 2, bridge, and outro. You're not planning every frame — just the emotional beats.</p>
  <p class="section-text"><strong>Step 2 — Generate Visual Assets (2-3 hours):</strong> Create your key images in Midjourney or DALL-E. For each scene, generate 3-5 variations and pick the best. Then animate them using Kling's image-to-video or Runway. Generate 15-25 clips of 5-10 seconds each — you'll use about 60-70% of them.</p>
  <p class="section-text"><strong>Step 3 — Edit & Sync (1-2 hours):</strong> Import all clips and your music into a free editor: DaVinci Resolve (professional-grade, free), CapCut (simpler, free), or iMovie (Mac, free). Cut clips to match the beat. Sync visual transitions to rhythmic hits — kick drums, snare hits, chord changes. This sync is what makes a music video feel intentional versus random.</p>
  <p class="section-text"><strong>Step 4 — Color Grade & Effects (30 min):</strong> Apply a consistent color grade across all clips for visual cohesion. Most editors have LUT (color preset) options. Add subtle effects: film grain for vintage feel, light leaks for warmth, glitch effects for electronic genres. Less is more — one consistent grade beats twelve different filters.</p>
  <p class="section-text"><strong>Step 5 — Export & Upload (15 min):</strong> Export at 1080p minimum, 4K if your clips support it. H.264 codec, high bitrate (15-25 Mbps). Upload to YouTube with optimized title, description (include lyrics), tags, and a custom thumbnail. Schedule to go live simultaneously with your music release on streaming platforms.</p>
</div>

<div class="lesson-section">
  <span class="section-label">YouTube Optimization</span>
  <h2 class="section-title">Your video is only as good as its discoverability.</h2>
  <p class="section-text"><strong>Title format:</strong> "[Artist Name] - [Song Title] (Official Music Video)" or "(Official Lyric Video)." This is what people search for.</p>
  <p class="section-text"><strong>Description:</strong> First 2 lines should hook searchers. Include full lyrics (this makes your video searchable for lyric queries). Add links to streaming platforms, social media, and credits.</p>
  <p class="section-text"><strong>Thumbnail:</strong> Generate a striking still from your video or create a custom image in Midjourney. Add the song title in bold text. High contrast, readable at small sizes. The thumbnail is responsible for 80% of click-through decisions.</p>
  <p class="section-text"><strong>Tags:</strong> Include your artist name, song title, genre, "official music video," and related artist names. YouTube's algorithm uses these for recommendation matching.</p>
</div>
