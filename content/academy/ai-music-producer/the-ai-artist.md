<div class="lesson-section">
  <span class="section-label">Artist Branding</span>
  <h2 class="section-title">The music is the product. The brand is the reason people choose your product.</h2>
  <p class="section-text">In an era where anyone can generate a song, your brand is your moat. It's not about the AI — it's about you. Your perspective, your aesthetic, your story. That's what builds fans, not algorithms.</p>
  <p class="section-text"><strong>Choose your artist identity:</strong> Are you the producer behind the curtain (like a DJ or beatmaker) or the artist in front (singer, rapper, persona)? Both work. The producer path lets you release across genres under one name. The artist path builds deeper fan connections but requires more personal branding.</p>
  <p class="section-text"><strong>Visual identity:</strong> Consistent colors, fonts, and visual style across all platforms. Generate your brand assets with Midjourney — artist photos (if using an AI persona), cover art templates, social media graphics. Your visual identity should be recognizable in a 50px thumbnail on Spotify.</p>
  <p class="section-text"><strong>The AI transparency question:</strong> Should you disclose AI usage? Our answer: yes, proactively. "AI-assisted production" is becoming a respected creative approach. Artists who are transparent build trust. Artists who hide it and get caught lose credibility permanently. Lead with your creative process as a feature, not a secret.</p>
  <div class="callout">
    <p><strong>Brand example:</strong> An AI producer who brands as "I use AI to make the music I hear in my dreams, then I shape it until it's real" is infinitely more compelling than one who says "I type prompts into Suno." The story of your relationship with AI is part of your artist narrative.</p>
  </div>
</div>

<div class="lesson-section">
  <span class="section-label">Rights & Legal</span>
  <h2 class="section-title">If you don't understand the business, the business will eat you.</h2>
  <p class="section-text"><strong>Copyright basics:</strong> In the US, copyright protects original works of authorship. For AI-assisted music, the Copyright Office has indicated that portions created with significant human creative input are protectable. Document your creative process — keep notes on what you directed, edited, arranged, and decided. This documentation is your evidence of authorship.</p>
  <p class="section-text"><strong>Publishing:</strong> When your song is played on radio, streamed, used in a TV show, or performed live, publishing royalties are generated. Register your songs with a Performance Rights Organization (PRO): ASCAP, BMI, or SESAC in the US. Registration is free. This ensures you collect royalties that distributors alone don't capture.</p>
  <p class="section-text"><strong>Mechanical royalties:</strong> Generated every time your song is reproduced (streaming, downloads, physical copies). Register with the Mechanical Licensing Collective (MLC) at themlc.com. Free registration. Without this, you're leaving money on the table from every stream.</p>
  <p class="section-text"><strong>Sync licensing:</strong> When your music is used in TV, film, ads, or games. This is the biggest single-payment revenue source for independent artists. Register with sync libraries: Musicbed, Artlist, Epidemic Sound, Pond5, AudioJungle. Upload your best tracks and wait. A single TV placement can earn $1,000-$50,000.</p>
  <div class="tip-box">
    <strong>Protect yourself:</strong> Register with a PRO (ASCAP/BMI), the MLC, and SoundExchange (for digital performance royalties). These three registrations take 30 minutes total and ensure you collect every dollar your music generates. Most independent artists miss 20-40% of their earnings by skipping these registrations.
  </div>
</div>

<div class="lesson-section">
  <span class="section-label">Revenue Streams</span>
  <h2 class="section-title">Don't rely on streaming alone. Stack every revenue stream available.</h2>
  <p class="section-text"><strong>1. Streaming ($0.003-$0.005/stream):</strong> Spotify, Apple Music, Amazon, YouTube Music. Low per-stream but compounds with volume. 100 tracks averaging 1,000 streams/month each = $300-$500/month. Consistency is everything.</p>
  <p class="section-text"><strong>2. Sync Licensing ($50-$50,000/placement):</strong> Upload to sync libraries. Volume matters — 50+ tracks across multiple genres maximizes placement chances. Income is sporadic but high per event.</p>
  <p class="section-text"><strong>3. Beat Sales ($20-$500/license):</strong> Sell beats on BeatStars or Airbit. Non-exclusive licenses ($20-$50) can be sold unlimited times. Exclusive licenses ($200-$500+) transfer full rights. A catalog of 100+ beats generates $500-$3,000/month for active sellers.</p>
  <p class="section-text"><strong>4. YouTube Ad Revenue ($2-$7 per 1,000 views):</strong> Music videos, visualizers, beat showcases. Monetize through YouTube Partner Program (500 subscribers + 3,000 watch hours). Content ID catches others using your tracks and shares their ad revenue with you.</p>
  <p class="section-text"><strong>5. Direct Sales ($1-$50/purchase):</strong> Sell albums, stems, sample packs, and project files on Bandcamp, Gumroad, or your own site. Fans who buy directly support you at 10-20x the rate of streaming.</p>
  <p class="section-text"><strong>6. Custom Production ($200-$5,000/project):</strong> Commission work for YouTubers, podcasters, indie game devs, and businesses. Advertise on Fiverr, social media, and music production forums. Higher per-project revenue than any other stream.</p>
</div>

<div class="lesson-section">
  <span class="section-label">The 90-Day Plan</span>
  <h2 class="section-title">From first track to sustainable income in three months.</h2>
  <p class="section-text"><strong>Month 1 — Build the Foundation:</strong> Produce 15-20 tracks using everything from this course. Release your best 5 as singles (one per week). Set up DistroKid, claim all artist profiles, register with ASCAP/BMI and the MLC. Create your visual brand. Build basic social presence on Instagram and TikTok with behind-the-scenes content.</p>
  <p class="section-text"><strong>Month 2 — Scale & Diversify:</strong> Release your first EP or album (8-10 tracks). Upload 20+ tracks to sync libraries. Open a BeatStars store with your best instrumentals. Start pitching to independent playlist curators. Create your first music video. Post consistently on social media — 3-5 times per week minimum.</p>
  <p class="section-text"><strong>Month 3 — Monetize & Compound:</strong> Launch a Bandcamp page for direct sales. Offer custom production services on Fiverr or through social media. Continue weekly single releases. Analyze your streaming data — double down on genres and styles that perform best. By month 3, you should have 30+ tracks released, 50+ tracks in sync libraries, and multiple revenue streams active.</p>
  <p class="section-text"><strong>Realistic month 3 income:</strong> Streaming: $50-$200. Beat sales: $100-$500. Sync placements: $0-$2,000 (unpredictable). Custom production: $200-$1,000. YouTube: $20-$100. Direct sales: $50-$200. Total range: $420-$4,000/month, with the variance driven by sync placements and custom work. By month 6, streaming and beat sales compound significantly as your catalog grows.</p>
</div>
