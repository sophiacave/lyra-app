<div class="lesson-section">
  <span class="section-label">The State of Play</span>
  <h2 class="section-title">AI music went from a joke to a studio tool in 18 months.</h2>
  <p class="section-text">In early 2024, AI-generated music sounded like a MIDI file having a panic attack. By late 2025, Suno and Udio were producing tracks that fooled professional engineers in blind tests. The leap was that dramatic.</p>
  <p class="section-text">Here's where the major tools stand right now:</p>
  <p class="section-text"><strong>Suno (v4):</strong> The market leader for full song generation. Feed it a text prompt or lyrics and it produces a complete track — vocals, instruments, arrangement, mixing. Quality ranges from "impressive demo" to "release-ready" depending on genre and prompting skill. Free tier: 50 credits/day (~10 songs). Pro: $10/month.</p>
  <p class="section-text"><strong>Udio:</strong> Suno's main competitor. Slightly better at complex arrangements and genre authenticity, especially in electronic, hip-hop, and classical. More granular control over structure. Similar pricing model.</p>
  <p class="section-text"><strong>Stable Audio (by Stability AI):</strong> Best for instrumental loops, sound design, and background music. Less capable for full songs with vocals but excellent for production elements. Open-source model available for local use.</p>
  <p class="section-text"><strong>AIVA:</strong> Specialized in orchestral and cinematic scores. Used by game studios and filmmakers. Generates sheet music alongside audio. Great for composers who need a starting point.</p>
  <p class="section-text"><strong>Timbre (by Like One):</strong> Full pipeline tool — generate, separate stems, mix, master, and distribute from one interface. Designed for producers who want end-to-end control without bouncing between 8 different apps.</p>
</div>

<div class="lesson-section">
  <span class="section-label">What AI Can (and Can't) Do</span>
  <h2 class="section-title">It's a collaborator, not a replacement. Know the difference.</h2>
  <p class="section-text"><strong>AI excels at:</strong> Generating song structures, chord progressions, and melodies. Producing full instrumentals in any genre. Writing serviceable lyrics with proper rhyme schemes. Creating multiple variations quickly for A/B testing. Sound design and texture generation. Stem separation from existing tracks.</p>
  <p class="section-text"><strong>AI struggles with:</strong> Genuine emotional depth and storytelling. Complex time signature changes. Perfectly replicating a specific artist's style (legally and technically). Live instrument feel and human imperfection that makes music breathe. Mixing and mastering to professional release standards (it's getting close but not there yet).</p>
  <p class="section-text"><strong>The sweet spot:</strong> Use AI for the 80% that's technical execution. Add your 20% of creative direction, emotional intent, and quality control. That combination produces music faster than a traditional producer and more human than pure AI. This is the hybrid approach that defines the next generation of music makers.</p>
  <div class="callout">
    <p><strong>Reality check:</strong> AI won't make you a hitmaker overnight. But it will collapse the learning curve from "years of music theory" to "weeks of prompt engineering." If you have taste, vision, and persistence, AI is the most powerful instrument ever created.</p>
  </div>
</div>

<div class="lesson-section">
  <span class="section-label">The Money Map</span>
  <h2 class="section-title">Four ways AI producers are earning real revenue right now.</h2>
  <p class="section-text"><strong>1. Sync Licensing ($50-$5,000 per placement):</strong> TV shows, ads, YouTube channels, and podcasts all need music. AI lets you produce 10-20 tracks per week across multiple genres. Upload to sync libraries like Musicbed, Artlist, or Epidemic Sound. Volume is key — more tracks = more chances for placement.</p>
  <p class="section-text"><strong>2. Streaming Revenue ($0.003-$0.005 per stream):</strong> Yes, the per-stream rate is tiny. But AI lets you release an album per month instead of per year. Artists with 100+ tracks across platforms report $500-$3,000/month from streaming alone. Consistency and volume win.</p>
  <p class="section-text"><strong>3. Custom Production ($200-$2,000 per track):</strong> Small businesses need jingles. YouTubers need intros. Indie game developers need soundtracks. Offer custom AI-assisted production with human polish. Deliver in days instead of weeks.</p>
  <p class="section-text"><strong>4. Beat Sales ($20-$500 per lease):</strong> Produce beats using AI, sell exclusive and non-exclusive licenses on BeatStars, Airbit, or your own site. Rappers and R&B artists are the biggest buyers. A catalog of 200+ beats generates consistent passive income.</p>
</div>

<div class="lesson-section">
  <span class="section-label">Ethics & Legal</span>
  <h2 class="section-title">The rules are still being written. Here's what we know.</h2>
  <p class="section-text"><strong>Copyright:</strong> As of 2026, AI-generated music exists in a legal gray area in the US. The Copyright Office has indicated that purely AI-generated works without meaningful human authorship may not be copyrightable. The workaround: ensure significant human creative input — arrangement decisions, lyric writing, editing, mixing. Document your creative process.</p>
  <p class="section-text"><strong>Platform policies:</strong> Spotify, Apple Music, and YouTube allow AI-assisted music but prohibit "artificial streaming" (botting plays). Distributors like DistroKid and TuneCore accept AI-assisted tracks but require you to disclose AI involvement. Read the terms carefully.</p>
  <p class="section-text"><strong>Voice cloning:</strong> Using AI to clone a real person's voice without consent is legally risky and ethically wrong. Tennessee's ELVIS Act and similar laws are spreading. Use AI voices, your own voice, or licensed voice models only.</p>
  <p class="section-text"><strong>Best practice:</strong> Be transparent about your process. "AI-assisted production" is becoming a respected category, not a dirty secret. The producers who build trust now will own the market later.</p>
</div>
