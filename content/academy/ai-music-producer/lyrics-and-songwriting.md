<div class="lesson-section">
  <span class="section-label">AI as Co-Writer</span>
  <h2 class="section-title">The best songs are conversations. Now you can have one with an AI.</h2>
  <p class="section-text">The biggest misconception about AI songwriting: that you type "write me a hit song" and get one. You don't. What you get is mediocre poetry set to a rhyme scheme. The magic happens when you treat AI as a co-writer, not a replacement.</p>
  <p class="section-text">Here's the workflow that professional AI-assisted songwriters use:</p>
  <p class="section-text"><strong>Step 1 — Define the emotional core.</strong> Before you touch any AI tool, answer this: What is this song about? Not the topic — the feeling. "Heartbreak" is a topic. "The moment you realize they already moved on while you're still crying in the parking lot" is a feeling. Specificity creates emotion.</p>
  <p class="section-text"><strong>Step 2 — Feed context to Claude or ChatGPT.</strong> Give it the emotional core, the genre, the audience, and any specific images or metaphors you want. Example prompt: "Write lyrics for an indie folk song about the specific loneliness of moving to a new city. Use imagery of empty kitchens, unfamiliar streets, and calling someone who doesn't pick up. Conversational tone, not poetic."</p>
  <p class="section-text"><strong>Step 3 — Cherry-pick and rewrite.</strong> AI will give you 10-20 lines. Maybe 3-4 are gold. Take those, discard the rest, and write the gaps yourself. Or prompt for alternatives: "Give me 5 different ways to say 'I miss you but I won't admit it.'" The best line wins.</p>
</div>

<div class="lesson-section">
  <span class="section-label">Hook Engineering</span>
  <h2 class="section-title">A great hook is 5 words that someone can't stop humming.</h2>
  <p class="section-text">The hook is the most important part of any song. It's the melody and lyric that sticks in your head at 3 AM. AI is surprisingly good at generating hook candidates — you just need to know how to ask.</p>
  <p class="section-text"><strong>The Hook Prompt Formula:</strong> "Generate 10 short, catchy hook phrases for a [genre] song about [theme]. Each hook should be under 8 words, use simple language, and have a natural rhythm when spoken aloud. Think [reference artist] meets [reference artist]."</p>
  <p class="section-text">From 10 candidates, 2-3 will have potential. Test them by saying them out loud — if you can imagine singing it in the shower, it works. The best hooks are conversational phrases turned melodic: "I can't feel my face," "Bad guy," "Stay with me."</p>
  <div class="tip-box">
    <strong>Pro technique — the melody-first approach:</strong> Hum a melody into your phone's voice recorder. Transcribe the rhythm (even as nonsense syllables). Then ask AI: "Write lyrics that match this syllable pattern: da-DA-da-da-DA-DA-da. Theme: [your theme]." This ensures the lyrics fit the melody naturally instead of forcing a melody onto words.
  </div>
</div>

<div class="lesson-section">
  <span class="section-label">Writing Real Lyrics</span>
  <h2 class="section-title">Generic lyrics kill great beats. Here's how to make them personal.</h2>
  <p class="section-text">AI lyrics default to cliches. "Dancing in the moonlight," "hearts on fire," "we'll make it through the night." Your job is to push past the defaults into specificity.</p>
  <p class="section-text"><strong>The Specificity Trick:</strong> Replace every general noun with a specific one. "Car" becomes "your mom's Corolla." "Drink" becomes "lukewarm coffee from the gas station." "Home" becomes "the apartment with the broken heater." Specific details make fictional stories feel autobiographical.</p>
  <p class="section-text"><strong>The Constraint Method:</strong> Give AI creative constraints. "Write a verse where every line starts with 'I remember.'" Or "Write a chorus that never uses the word 'love' but is clearly about love." Constraints force creativity in both you and the AI.</p>
  <p class="section-text"><strong>The Perspective Shift:</strong> Write the same verse from three perspectives — first person ("I"), second person ("You"), third person ("She/He"). Each version reveals different emotional angles. Pick the one that hits hardest.</p>
  <p class="section-text"><strong>The Edit Pass:</strong> After assembling your lyrics, read them aloud. Every line that makes you cringe, flag it. Every line that makes you feel something, keep it. Be ruthless. A 3-minute song is roughly 200 words — every word carries weight. If a line doesn't earn its place, cut it.</p>
</div>

<div class="lesson-section">
  <span class="section-label">Lyrics to Track</span>
  <h2 class="section-title">Feeding custom lyrics into AI music generators.</h2>
  <p class="section-text">Both Suno and Udio accept custom lyrics. This is where you get control over what the AI sings. Here's how to format them:</p>
  <p class="section-text"><strong>Suno format:</strong> Switch to "Custom" mode. Paste your lyrics in the lyrics field. Use section tags in brackets: [Verse 1], [Chorus], [Bridge], [Outro]. Add style/genre in the style field. The AI will generate music around your exact words.</p>
  <p class="section-text"><strong>Vocal style tags:</strong> Include delivery cues in your lyrics. "(whispered)" before a line for intimacy. "(belted)" for power. "(spoken word)" for rap-adjacent delivery. "(falsetto)" for high, airy vocals. These cues don't always work perfectly, but they influence the output more than you'd expect.</p>
  <p class="section-text"><strong>Structure tags that work:</strong> [Intro], [Verse], [Pre-Chorus], [Chorus], [Post-Chorus], [Bridge], [Breakdown], [Outro], [Instrumental Break], [Ad-lib]. The more structural guidance you give, the more professional the arrangement sounds.</p>
  <p class="section-text"><strong>Language mixing:</strong> Suno handles multilingual lyrics surprisingly well. You can write a verse in English and a chorus in Spanish, Japanese, or French. The AI will match pronunciation and singing style to the language. This opens up global market appeal.</p>
</div>
