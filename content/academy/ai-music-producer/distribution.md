<div class="lesson-section">
  <span class="section-label">How Distribution Works</span>
  <h2 class="section-title">You don't upload directly to Spotify. A distributor does it for you.</h2>
  <p class="section-text">Spotify, Apple Music, Amazon Music, Tidal, YouTube Music — none of them let you upload music directly. You need a distributor: a service that delivers your music to all platforms, handles royalty collection, and provides your ISRC codes (the ISBN of the music world).</p>
  <p class="section-text"><strong>DistroKid ($22.99/year):</strong> The most popular for independents. Unlimited uploads, keeps 100% of royalties, fastest delivery (1-2 days to most stores). Best for prolific AI producers who release frequently. Important: they accept AI-assisted music but require disclosure.</p>
  <p class="section-text"><strong>TuneCore ($29.99/year per album):</strong> Slightly more expensive but includes more detailed analytics and sync licensing opportunities. Good for artists focused on fewer, higher-quality releases. Also requires AI disclosure.</p>
  <p class="section-text"><strong>CD Baby ($9.95 per single, $29 per album, one-time):</strong> No annual fee — pay once, distributed forever. Takes a 9% commission on royalties. Best for artists who don't release often and don't want recurring charges.</p>
  <p class="section-text"><strong>Amuse (Free tier available):</strong> Free distribution with limited features. Pro tier at $5.99/month adds faster delivery and more stores. Good for testing the waters before committing to a paid distributor.</p>
  <div class="callout">
    <p><strong>For AI producers releasing frequently:</strong> DistroKid is the clear winner. Unlimited uploads at a flat rate means you can release a single every week without extra cost. The speed advantage (1-2 days vs. 5-7 for competitors) also matters when you're riding trends.</p>
  </div>
</div>

<div class="lesson-section">
  <span class="section-label">The Release Process</span>
  <h2 class="section-title">Step by step: from finished track to live on all platforms.</h2>
  <p class="section-text"><strong>Step 1 — Prepare your files.</strong> WAV format, 44.1kHz sample rate, 16-bit depth (or 24-bit). Stereo. No clipping (peaks should be below 0dB). Most distributors reject MP3s. Your mastering service (LANDR, eMastered) exports in the correct format.</p>
  <p class="section-text"><strong>Step 2 — Create your artwork.</strong> 3000x3000 pixels, JPG or PNG, RGB color mode. No blurry images, no copyrighted material, no social media handles or URLs (Spotify rejects these). Use Midjourney or DALL-E to generate unique cover art. Prompt: "[mood/aesthetic] album cover, [style], no text, high resolution, square format." Add text (artist name, title) in Canva.</p>
  <p class="section-text"><strong>Step 3 — Fill in metadata.</strong> Track title, artist name, album name, genre, release date, songwriter credits, producer credits. Metadata is how platforms categorize and recommend your music. Incorrect metadata = invisible music. Use your real artist name consistently across all releases.</p>
  <p class="section-text"><strong>Step 4 — Set your release date.</strong> Schedule at least 2-3 weeks out. This gives you time to pitch to playlists (Spotify requires 7 days minimum for editorial playlist consideration). Friday releases align with Spotify's New Music Friday playlists.</p>
  <p class="section-text"><strong>Step 5 — Upload and submit.</strong> Upload your files and artwork to your distributor. Double-check everything — once it's live, fixing errors requires re-uploading. Your distributor sends it to all platforms automatically.</p>
  <p class="section-text"><strong>Step 6 — Claim your profiles.</strong> Claim your Spotify for Artists profile (artists.spotify.com), Apple Music for Artists, and YouTube Music artist channel. These dashboards give you analytics, playlist pitching tools, and profile customization.</p>
</div>

<div class="lesson-section">
  <span class="section-label">Playlist Strategy</span>
  <h2 class="section-title">Playlists are the new radio. Getting on one can make or break a release.</h2>
  <p class="section-text"><strong>Spotify Editorial Playlists:</strong> Curated by Spotify's team. Massive reach (100K-10M followers per playlist). Submit through Spotify for Artists at least 7 days before release. Fill out the pitch form thoroughly — describe the mood, inspiration, and story. Acceptance rate is low (~5%) but impact is huge.</p>
  <p class="section-text"><strong>Algorithmic Playlists:</strong> Discover Weekly, Release Radar, and Daily Mix. You can't pitch to these — Spotify's algorithm adds your music based on listener behavior. To trigger the algorithm: get early streams (first 24-48 hours matter most), encourage saves over streams (saves signal quality), and maintain consistent release frequency.</p>
  <p class="section-text"><strong>Independent/User Playlists:</strong> Curated by regular users and playlist curators. Find relevant playlists using PlaylistSupply, SpotOnTrack, or manual searching. Contact curators via their social media or submission links. Many curators accept submissions for free — avoid pay-for-play schemes (they violate Spotify's terms).</p>
  <div class="tip-box">
    <strong>The Release Day Playbook:</strong> Day of release: share across all social platforms with direct Spotify/Apple links (use a smartlink from Linkfire or ToneDen so one URL works for all platforms). Ask your network to save the track (not just stream it). Post lyrics, behind-the-scenes content, and the story behind the song. The first 48 hours of engagement signal to algorithms that your music deserves wider distribution.
  </div>
</div>

<div class="lesson-section">
  <span class="section-label">YouTube & Beyond</span>
  <h2 class="section-title">YouTube is the world's biggest music platform. Treat it that way.</h2>
  <p class="section-text"><strong>YouTube Music:</strong> Your distributor sends your music to YouTube Music automatically. But you should also upload a music video or visualizer to regular YouTube — this captures search traffic and ad revenue that YouTube Music alone doesn't.</p>
  <p class="section-text"><strong>YouTube Content ID:</strong> Register your music with Content ID through your distributor or a service like IdentifyMusic. When someone uses your track in their video, you earn ad revenue from their views. This passive income stream can exceed streaming revenue for popular tracks.</p>
  <p class="section-text"><strong>SoundCloud:</strong> Still relevant for hip-hop, electronic, and lo-fi. Free uploads, direct listener engagement, repost chains. Not a revenue driver but a community builder. Post there alongside streaming platforms.</p>
  <p class="section-text"><strong>Bandcamp:</strong> The musician's friend. Fans can name their price or buy at your set price. You keep 85% (Bandcamp takes 15%). Bandcamp Fridays (first Friday of each month) waive their cut entirely. Best for building a dedicated fanbase that supports you directly.</p>
