<div class="lesson-section">
  <span class="section-label">Album Thinking</span>
  <h2 class="section-title">An album isn't 10 random songs. It's a journey with a beginning, middle, and end.</h2>
  <p class="section-text">The temptation with AI music production is to generate a hundred tracks and dump them all onto Spotify. Don't do this. Listeners can tell the difference between a collection of songs and an album with intention. Albums with a cohesive identity get added to playlists, shared with friends, and listened to front-to-back.</p>
  <p class="section-text"><strong>The concept comes first.</strong> Before you generate a single track, define your album's identity:</p>
  <p class="section-text"><strong>Theme:</strong> What is this album about? A breakup, a city, a season, a feeling, a story. "Songs about being alone at night in a new city" is a concept. "Various genres" is not.</p>
  <p class="section-text"><strong>Sonic palette:</strong> What instruments, textures, and production choices will unify the album? If track 1 is lo-fi piano and track 2 is death metal, listeners get whiplash. Pick 5-8 core sonic elements that appear across most tracks.</p>
  <p class="section-text"><strong>Mood arc:</strong> Plan the emotional journey. Most great albums follow a pattern: start accessible, go deeper in the middle, hit a climax around track 7-8, then resolve. Think of it like a movie — you need rising action, a peak, and a denouement.</p>
</div>

<div class="lesson-section">
  <span class="section-label">The Production Pipeline</span>
  <h2 class="section-title">Batch your workflow. Generate first, select second, polish third.</h2>
  <p class="section-text">Here's the pipeline that lets you complete a 10-track album in 2-3 weeks:</p>
  <p class="section-text"><strong>Phase 1 — Ideation Sprint (Days 1-3):</strong> Generate 30-50 track ideas using your concept as a guide. Spend 2-3 hours per day in Suno or Udio, varying your prompts within your sonic palette. Don't stop to polish anything. Just generate, save, and move on. You're mining for gold, not crafting jewelry yet.</p>
  <p class="section-text"><strong>Phase 2 — Curation (Day 4):</strong> Listen to everything with fresh ears. Rate each track 1-5. Anything below a 4 gets cut. You should have 12-18 strong candidates. Pick the 10-12 that tell the most complete story together. Kill your darlings — a great track that doesn't fit the album concept gets saved for a future project.</p>
  <p class="section-text"><strong>Phase 3 — Refinement (Days 5-10):</strong> Work through each selected track systematically. Separate stems. Fix issues. Add human touches — a live guitar overdub, a vocal re-recording, subtle mixing adjustments. Spend 1-2 hours per track. This is where "AI-generated" becomes "AI-assisted."</p>
  <p class="section-text"><strong>Phase 4 — Sequencing & Mastering (Days 11-14):</strong> Arrange track order (covered below). Master each track, then master the full sequence for consistent volume and tonal balance across the album. Export final files.</p>
  <div class="tip-box">
    <strong>Batch processing saves hours:</strong> Do all your stem separations in one session. All your pitch corrections in another. All your mastering in another. Context-switching between different tasks kills efficiency. Batching the same task across all tracks keeps you in flow state.
  </div>
</div>

<div class="lesson-section">
  <span class="section-label">Track Sequencing</span>
  <h2 class="section-title">The order of your tracks determines how people experience your album.</h2>
  <p class="section-text"><strong>Track 1 — The Statement:</strong> Sets the tone. Should be accessible, hooky, and representative of the album's sound. Not necessarily your best song — your most inviting one. This is your first impression.</p>
  <p class="section-text"><strong>Track 2 — The Escalation:</strong> Builds on track 1's energy. Often your strongest song because listeners who've made it to track 2 are committed. Hit them with your best shot.</p>
  <p class="section-text"><strong>Tracks 3-4 — The Exploration:</strong> Introduce variety within your concept. Show different facets of your sonic palette. This is where you can take creative risks.</p>
  <p class="section-text"><strong>Track 5 — The Breather:</strong> A slower, more reflective moment. Every great album needs a breath in the middle. An acoustic version, a stripped-down interlude, or a ballad works perfectly here.</p>
  <p class="section-text"><strong>Tracks 6-8 — The Deep Cut:</strong> The emotional core of the album. Your most personal, experimental, or intense tracks live here. Listeners who've made it this far are invested.</p>
  <p class="section-text"><strong>Track 9 — The Climax:</strong> Your emotional peak. The biggest, most powerful track on the album. Everything has been building to this moment.</p>
  <p class="section-text"><strong>Track 10 — The Resolution:</strong> Bring it home. Echo elements from track 1 for bookend symmetry. Leave the listener satisfied but wanting more. A quiet ending after a loud climax is devastatingly effective.</p>
</div>

<div class="lesson-section">
  <span class="section-label">Quality Control</span>
  <h2 class="section-title">Every track must pass three tests before it makes the cut.</h2>
  <p class="section-text"><strong>Test 1 — The Car Test:</strong> Listen in your car (or on cheap earbuds). If the mix sounds good on bad speakers, it'll sound great on good ones. Most streaming listeners use phone speakers and $20 earbuds. Mix for reality, not for studio monitors.</p>
  <p class="section-text"><strong>Test 2 — The Fresh Ears Test:</strong> After finishing a track, don't listen for 24 hours. Come back with fresh perspective. Issues you missed in the flow state become obvious with distance. If it still hits after 24 hours, it's real.</p>
  <p class="section-text"><strong>Test 3 — The Skip Test:</strong> Play your album front-to-back. If you instinctively want to skip a track, cut it. Replace it with something from your reserves. A 9-track album with no skips beats a 12-track album with 3 filler tracks every time.</p>
  <p class="section-text"><strong>Consistency checks:</strong> Volume levels should be similar across tracks (mastering handles this, but verify). Sonic palette should feel unified — if one track sounds completely different, it might not belong. Transitions between tracks should feel natural — crossfades, complementary keys, or matched energy levels help.</p>
</div>
