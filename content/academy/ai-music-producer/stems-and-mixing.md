<div class="lesson-section">
  <span class="section-label">Stem Separation</span>
  <h2 class="section-title">AI can now pull a finished track apart into individual instruments. This changes everything.</h2>
  <p class="section-text">Stem separation is the process of isolating individual elements — vocals, drums, bass, melody — from a mixed audio file. Two years ago, this required expensive software and produced mediocre results. Today, AI does it in seconds at near-studio quality.</p>
  <p class="section-text"><strong>Why it matters:</strong> When Suno or Udio generates a track you love but one element is off — maybe the drums are too busy or the bass is muddy — you can separate the stems, fix or replace the problematic element, and reassemble a perfect track.</p>
  <p class="section-text"><strong>The tools:</strong></p>
  <p class="section-text"><strong>Demucs (by Meta):</strong> The gold standard for open-source stem separation. Splits into 4 stems (vocals, drums, bass, other) or 6 stems (adding guitar and piano). Free, runs locally. Quality is excellent for most use cases.</p>
  <p class="section-text"><strong>LALAL.AI:</strong> Cloud-based, commercial option. Better vocal isolation than Demucs in many cases. 10 free minutes, then $15-$50/month depending on usage.</p>
  <p class="section-text"><strong>Timbre Separate:</strong> Built into the Timbre pipeline. Separate, process, and recombine without leaving the tool. Ideal if you're already in the Timbre ecosystem.</p>
  <div class="tip-box">
    <strong>Power use case:</strong> Generate 5 variations of the same song in Suno. Separate stems from each. Take the drums from version 2, the bass from version 4, the melody from version 1, and the vocals from version 5. Reassemble in a DAW. You just created a track that sounds nothing like a single AI generation.
  </div>
</div>

<div class="lesson-section">
  <span class="section-label">Mixing Fundamentals</span>
  <h2 class="section-title">Mixing is making every element sit perfectly in the sonic space.</h2>
  <p class="section-text">Even if you've never touched a mixing console, understanding four core concepts will dramatically improve your tracks:</p>
  <p class="section-text"><strong>1. Levels (Volume Balance):</strong> Every element needs its own space in the volume spectrum. Kick and bass sit loudest. Vocals sit just above the instrumental bed. Hi-hats and ambient textures sit lower. A good mix sounds balanced at any volume. Start with everything low, bring up the kick first, then bass, then vocals, then everything else.</p>
  <p class="section-text"><strong>2. EQ (Frequency Balance):</strong> Every sound occupies a frequency range. Bass lives below 250Hz. Vocals live at 200Hz-4kHz. Hi-hats live above 8kHz. When two elements compete in the same range, the mix sounds muddy. Solution: cut conflicting frequencies. If the bass and kick are clashing, cut some bass at 60-80Hz and boost the kick there instead.</p>
  <p class="section-text"><strong>3. Compression (Dynamic Control):</strong> Compression makes quiet parts louder and loud parts quieter, creating a more consistent sound. Apply gently to vocals (3-6dB reduction) and drums (2-4dB). Over-compression sounds lifeless — if the track feels "flat," you've gone too far.</p>
  <p class="section-text"><strong>4. Spatial Effects (Reverb & Delay):</strong> Reverb creates a sense of space — a small room, a cathedral, an open field. Delay creates rhythmic echoes. Use reverb on vocals and synths for depth. Use delay on guitars and vocals for width. Keep kick and bass dry (no reverb) to maintain punch.</p>
</div>

<div class="lesson-section">
  <span class="section-label">AI Mixing Tools</span>
  <h2 class="section-title">Don't have a $50K studio? These tools close the gap.</h2>
  <p class="section-text"><strong>iZotope Neutron:</strong> AI-powered mixing assistant. Drop it on any track and it analyzes the audio, suggests EQ curves, compression settings, and width adjustments. Not a magic bullet, but it gets you 80% of the way. $149 or included in the Music Production Suite.</p>
  <p class="section-text"><strong>LANDR Mix:</strong> Cloud-based AI mixing. Upload your stems, it mixes them for you. Results are surprisingly decent for straightforward genres. Free trial, then $9-$25/month.</p>
  <p class="section-text"><strong>Free DAW options:</strong> GarageBand (Mac, free, great for beginners), Audacity (all platforms, free, basic but functional), Cakewalk by BandLab (Windows, free, full professional DAW). You don't need Pro Tools or Ableton to start. Any DAW that lets you import stems, adjust levels, and apply effects is enough.</p>
</div>

<div class="lesson-section">
  <span class="section-label">AI Mastering</span>
  <h2 class="section-title">Mastering is the final polish. AI does it better than most humans now.</h2>
  <p class="section-text">Mastering is the process of optimizing a final mix for distribution — ensuring it sounds great on earbuds, car speakers, studio monitors, and phone speakers. Traditionally, this costs $50-$200 per track with a human engineer. AI mastering tools do it for $2-$10.</p>
  <p class="section-text"><strong>LANDR:</strong> The most popular AI mastering service. Upload your mix, choose intensity (low/medium/high), download mastered file. $4-$9 per track or $12-$25/month unlimited. Quality is genuinely good for 90% of music.</p>
  <p class="section-text"><strong>eMastered:</strong> Founded by Grammy-winning engineers. More reference-track-based — upload a song you want yours to sound like, and it matches the tonal profile. $9/month unlimited mastering.</p>
  <p class="section-text"><strong>CloudBounce:</strong> Budget option at $2-$5 per track. Quality is a step below LANDR and eMastered but perfectly fine for beat tapes, SoundCloud releases, and demos.</p>
  <p class="section-text"><strong>The full workflow:</strong> AI generation (Suno/Udio) &rarr; Stem separation (Demucs) &rarr; Mixing (GarageBand/free DAW + iZotope) &rarr; Mastering (LANDR/eMastered) &rarr; Export as WAV 44.1kHz/16-bit for streaming or 24-bit for highest quality. Total cost: $0-$10. Total time: 30-60 minutes per track.</p>
</div>
