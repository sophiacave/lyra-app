<div class="lesson-section">
  <span class="section-label">Separation</span>
  <h2 class="section-title">Step one: separate your money.</h2>
  <p>The single most important thing you can do when earning side income is separate it from your personal finances. Mixing business and personal money creates a mess that's expensive to untangle later — especially at tax time.</p>
  <p><strong>Separate bank account:</strong> Open a dedicated checking account for your business income. All business revenue goes in. All business expenses come out. Your personal money stays personal.</p>
  <p><strong>Separate tracking:</strong> Even before you open a separate account, use AI to separate business transactions from personal ones in your existing statements. Ask AI to categorize each transaction as business or personal and keep separate running totals.</p>
  <p><strong>Pay yourself:</strong> Decide on a regular transfer from your business account to your personal account. This is your "paycheck." It forces you to know your business profit because you can only pay yourself from what's actually earned after expenses.</p>
  <div class="tip-box">
    <strong>The tax set-aside:</strong> Immediately set aside 25-30% of every business payment for taxes. Move it to a separate savings account and don't touch it. Self-employment taxes catch people off guard — this prevents the surprise.
  </div>
</div>

<div class="lesson-section">
  <span class="section-label">Bookkeeping</span>
  <h2 class="section-title">AI-powered bookkeeping that takes 15 minutes a week.</h2>
  <p>Bookkeeping sounds intimidating. It's really just tracking money in and money out with categories. AI makes it simple.</p>
  <p><strong>Weekly income log:</strong> Every week, log payments received. Client name, amount, date, and service provided. AI can create a spreadsheet template with the right columns and formulas.</p>
  <p><strong>Weekly expense log:</strong> Log business expenses. Amount, date, category, and purpose. Categories: software/tools, marketing, supplies, education, travel, meals (business), and professional services.</p>
  <p><strong>Monthly reconciliation:</strong> At month-end, export your business bank statement and compare it to your logs. AI can flag discrepancies: "You have a $49.99 charge on the 15th that isn't in your expense log. What is it?"</p>
  <p><strong>Quarterly review:</strong> Every quarter, ask AI to generate a profit & loss summary. Revenue minus expenses equals profit. Track this over time. Is your business becoming more or less profitable?</p>
  <div class="callout">
    <strong>AI bookkeeping prompt:</strong> "Here are my business transactions this month [paste them]. Categorize each as income or expense, assign categories, and generate a simple profit & loss statement."
  </div>
</div>

<div class="lesson-section">
  <span class="section-label">Margins & Cash Flow</span>
  <h2 class="section-title">Understanding profit margins and cash flow.</h2>
  <p><strong>Revenue is not profit.</strong> If you earned $5,000 but spent $2,000 on tools, marketing, and supplies, your profit is $3,000. Your profit margin is 60%. This is the number that matters — not the top line.</p>
  <p>Ask AI to calculate your profit margin monthly. Healthy margins for service-based side businesses are typically 50-80%. If yours is below 40%, you're either undercharging or overspending.</p>
  <p><strong>Cash flow timing:</strong> Cash flow is about when money arrives vs. when bills are due. You might be profitable on paper but cash-poor because clients pay Net-30 while your tools charge immediately. AI can map your cash flow timing and identify gaps.</p>
  <p><strong>The runway number:</strong> How many months can your business survive with zero new income? Take your business savings and divide by monthly expenses. Under 2 months is risky. Over 3 months gives you breathing room.</p>
  <div class="tip-box">
    <strong>Track your effective hourly rate.</strong> Total profit divided by total hours worked. If your side hustle earns $3,000/month profit but takes 60 hours, your effective rate is $50/hr. Is that worth it? AI can calculate this and compare to alternatives.
  </div>
</div>

<div class="lesson-section">
  <span class="section-label">Going Legit</span>
  <h2 class="section-title">When side income becomes a real business.</h2>
  <p>At some point, your side income may warrant formal business structure. Here are the signals:</p>
  <p><strong>Consistent income:</strong> If you're earning regularly for more than a few months, you have a business — even if it's small.</p>
  <p><strong>Tax complexity:</strong> Once you're tracking deductions, paying quarterly taxes, and separating expenses, the structure of a formal business entity can simplify things.</p>
  <p><strong>Liability concerns:</strong> If your work could create liability (client disputes, contracts), a formal business entity can provide personal asset protection.</p>
  <p>AI can explain the differences between sole proprietorship, LLC, and S-Corp structures. But talk to a business attorney or CPA before making this decision — the right choice depends on your specific situation, state laws, and income level.</p>
</div>
