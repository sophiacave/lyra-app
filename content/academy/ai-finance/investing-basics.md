<div class="lesson-section">
  <span class="section-label">Foundations</span>
  <h2 class="section-title">Investing concepts everyone should know.</h2>
  <p>Before AI can help you research, you need to understand the basics. Here are the concepts that matter most:</p>
  <p><strong>Compound interest:</strong> Earnings on your earnings. If you earn 7% on $1,000, you have $1,070. Next year you earn 7% on $1,070 — $1,144.90. Over decades, this snowball effect is massive. AI can model compound growth for any amount, rate, and time period.</p>
  <p><strong>Diversification:</strong> Don't put all your eggs in one basket. Spreading money across different types of investments reduces risk. If one goes down, others may hold steady or go up.</p>
  <p><strong>Risk and return:</strong> Higher potential returns generally come with higher risk. Lower-risk investments tend to grow more slowly. Your tolerance for risk depends on your timeline, goals, and how well you sleep at night when markets drop.</p>
  <p><strong>Time in the market:</strong> Time is the most powerful investing factor. Starting early — even with small amounts — generally outperforms starting late with large amounts because of compound growth.</p>
  <div class="tip-box">
    <strong>Ask AI:</strong> "Explain compound interest using a $200/month contribution over 30 years at a 7% average annual return. Show me year-by-year growth." Seeing your own numbers makes abstract concepts real.
  </div>
</div>

<div class="lesson-section">
  <span class="section-label">AI Research</span>
  <h2 class="section-title">Using AI for investment education.</h2>
  <p>AI is an exceptional learning tool for investing. Here's how to use it responsibly:</p>
  <p><strong>Concept explanations:</strong> "Explain what an index fund is like I'm 25 and just starting to invest." AI can break down any concept at your level. No jargon, no condescension.</p>
  <p><strong>Comparison analysis:</strong> "What are the general differences between stocks, bonds, index funds, and real estate as investment categories? What are the typical risk-return profiles?" AI can lay out objective comparisons without pushing you toward any option.</p>
  <p><strong>Historical context:</strong> "How has the S&P 500 performed historically during recessions? What's the typical recovery timeline?" Understanding history helps you make calmer decisions during volatility.</p>
  <p><strong>Fee analysis:</strong> "Explain expense ratios. What's the difference between a 0.03% and a 1.5% expense ratio over 30 years on a $500/month investment?" Fees compound too — and AI can show you exactly how much they cost over time.</p>
  <div class="callout">
    <strong>Critical boundary:</strong> AI can educate you about investment categories, explain concepts, and run hypothetical calculations. It should NOT tell you what to buy, when to buy, or how much to invest. Those decisions belong to you and your financial advisor.
  </div>
</div>

<div class="lesson-section">
  <span class="section-label">Smart Questions</span>
  <h2 class="section-title">Questions to ask AI vs. questions for a professional.</h2>
  <p><strong>Ask AI:</strong></p>
  <p>- "What does dollar-cost averaging mean and how does it work?"</p>
  <p>- "What's the historical average return of broad market index funds?"</p>
  <p>- "Explain the difference between a Roth IRA and a Traditional IRA."</p>
  <p>- "If I invest $300/month starting at age 28, what could that look like at 65?"</p>
  <p><strong>Ask a professional:</strong></p>
  <p>- "Should I invest or pay off debt first given my situation?"</p>
  <p>- "What specific investments are right for my risk tolerance and goals?"</p>
  <p>- "How should I allocate my 401(k) contributions?"</p>
  <p>- "What tax implications does this investment have for my specific situation?"</p>
  <p>The line is clear: AI teaches concepts and runs calculations. Professionals give personalized advice considering your full financial picture.</p>
  <div class="tip-box">
    <strong>The preparation advantage:</strong> Use AI to learn before meeting with a financial advisor. The better educated you are, the more productive the conversation. You'll ask better questions and understand the answers.
  </div>
</div>

<div class="lesson-section">
  <span class="section-label">Common Traps</span>
  <h2 class="section-title">What AI won't protect you from.</h2>
  <p><strong>Emotional investing:</strong> AI can tell you that panic-selling during a downturn historically leads to losses. It can show you data. But it can't stop you from clicking "sell" at 2 AM when the market drops 5%. Build your plan when you're calm.</p>
  <p><strong>Confirmation bias:</strong> If you ask AI "Why is [specific stock] a good buy?" it will find reasons. That doesn't make them valid. Always ask for the bear case too: "What are the biggest risks of investing in [category]?"</p>
  <p><strong>Overconfidence:</strong> AI-powered research can make you feel like an expert when you're still learning. Feeling knowledgeable and being knowledgeable aren't the same thing. Stay humble with your money.</p>
  <p><strong>Social media hype:</strong> AI can help you fact-check investment claims you see on social media. "Is this claim about [investment] accurate? What's the historical data?" Most hype doesn't survive contact with real data.</p>
</div>
