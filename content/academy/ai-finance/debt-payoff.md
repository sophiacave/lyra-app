<div class="lesson-section">
  <span class="section-label">Clarity First</span>
  <h2 class="section-title">You can't fight what you can't see.</h2>
  <p>Most people know they have debt. Few people know exactly how much, at what rates, and what it's actually costing them. That's where AI comes in first — not with a plan, but with clarity.</p>
  <p><strong>The debt inventory:</strong> List every debt. Balance, interest rate, minimum payment, and due date. Credit cards, student loans, car loans, personal loans, medical bills — everything. Don't leave anything out because it's embarrassing. The numbers don't judge you.</p>
  <p>Feed this inventory to your AI assistant. Ask it to calculate: total debt, total monthly minimums, total interest you'll pay if you only make minimums, and how long it will take at current payments.</p>
  <p>This moment of clarity is uncomfortable. It's also the most valuable financial step you can take. You can't build a real plan on vague feelings about debt.</p>
  <div class="tip-box">
    <strong>AI prompt:</strong> "Here are my debts [list them]. Calculate: total owed, total monthly minimums, total interest paid if I only make minimums, and payoff date for each. Then tell me which debt is costing me the most in interest per month."
  </div>
</div>

<div class="lesson-section">
  <span class="section-label">Strategies</span>
  <h2 class="section-title">Avalanche vs. Snowball: pick your weapon.</h2>
  <p><strong>The Avalanche Method:</strong> Pay minimums on everything, then throw all extra money at the highest-interest debt first. When it's paid off, roll that payment into the next highest-interest debt. This saves the most money mathematically.</p>
  <p><strong>The Snowball Method:</strong> Pay minimums on everything, then throw all extra money at the smallest balance first. When it's paid off, roll that payment into the next smallest. This gives you quick wins that build momentum.</p>
  <p>Here's the truth: the avalanche method saves more money. The snowball method has higher completion rates because of the psychological wins. The best method is the one you'll actually stick with.</p>
  <p>Use AI to model both. Ask: "Show me both avalanche and snowball payoff schedules with my debts. How much do I save with avalanche? How quickly do I get my first win with snowball?" Compare the numbers and pick what feels right for you.</p>
  <div class="callout">
    <strong>The hybrid approach:</strong> Some people start with snowball to get quick wins and build confidence, then switch to avalanche once they've built the habit. AI can model this hybrid timeline too.
  </div>
</div>

<div class="lesson-section">
  <span class="section-label">Hidden Costs</span>
  <h2 class="section-title">What debt is really costing you.</h2>
  <p>AI can reveal costs you never calculated:</p>
  <p><strong>Opportunity cost:</strong> Ask AI: "If I invested the money I'm paying in interest instead, what would it be worth in 10 years?" This isn't meant to depress you — it's meant to motivate. Every dollar of interest is a dollar that could be building your future.</p>
  <p><strong>Minimum payment trap:</strong> A $5,000 credit card balance at 22% APR with minimum payments takes over 14 years to pay off. You'd pay roughly $7,000 in interest — more than the original balance. AI can calculate this for every one of your debts.</p>
  <p><strong>Rate comparison:</strong> Some debts are cheap (3-5% student loans) and some are expensive (20-30% credit cards). AI can rank your debts by true cost and show you where an extra $100/month makes the biggest impact.</p>
  <p><strong>Balance transfer math:</strong> Thinking about a balance transfer? AI can calculate whether the transfer fee is worth it given the temporary lower rate. Sometimes it saves thousands. Sometimes it's a wash. Let the math decide.</p>
  <div class="tip-box">
    <strong>The extra payment calculator:</strong> Ask AI: "If I add $200/month to my debt payments, how much faster do I pay off everything and how much interest do I save?" The answer is usually motivating enough to find that extra $200.
  </div>
</div>

<div class="lesson-section">
  <span class="section-label">Staying On Track</span>
  <h2 class="section-title">Motivation when the numbers feel heavy.</h2>
  <p>Debt payoff is a marathon. AI can help you stay motivated.</p>
  <p><strong>Monthly progress reports:</strong> Every month, update your debt inventory and ask AI to show your progress. Total reduced, interest saved, timeline shortened. Celebrate the trend, not just the number.</p>
  <p><strong>Milestone tracking:</strong> Set milestones and let AI calculate when you'll hit each one. "When will I be below $10,000? When will my first card be paid off?" Having specific dates creates accountability.</p>
  <p><strong>Scenario modeling:</strong> Got a tax refund or bonus? Before spending it, ask AI: "If I put $1,500 toward my highest-rate debt, how does it change my payoff timeline?" Seeing the impact in real numbers makes the choice easier.</p>
  <p><strong>Keep perspective:</strong> Debt is a math problem, not a character flaw. AI helps you treat it that way — with calculations, not emotions. You're not bad with money. You're learning to use better tools.</p>
</div>
