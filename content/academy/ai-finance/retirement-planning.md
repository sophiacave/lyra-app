<div class="lesson-section">
  <span class="section-label">Account Types</span>
  <h2 class="section-title">Retirement accounts explained simply.</h2>
  <p>Retirement accounts are just containers for your money with special tax treatment. The money inside them can be invested in various ways. Here are the main types:</p>
  <p><strong>401(k) / 403(b):</strong> Employer-sponsored accounts. Contributions come from your paycheck before taxes. Many employers match a percentage of your contributions — that's free money. There are annual contribution limits set by the IRS.</p>
  <p><strong>Traditional IRA:</strong> Individual retirement account. Contributions may be tax-deductible. You pay taxes when you withdraw in retirement. Available to anyone with earned income, with income-based limits on deductibility.</p>
  <p><strong>Roth IRA:</strong> Contributions are made with after-tax money. The advantage: withdrawals in retirement are generally tax-free. Great if you expect to be in a higher tax bracket later. Income limits apply to contributions.</p>
  <p><strong>SEP IRA / Solo 401(k):</strong> Designed for self-employed individuals and small business owners. Higher contribution limits than traditional or Roth IRAs.</p>
  <p>Ask AI to explain the differences using your specific income and situation (without sharing sensitive info). It can clarify contribution limits, income phase-outs, and tax implications in plain language.</p>
  <div class="tip-box">
    <strong>AI prompt:</strong> "I'm [age], earning approximately [income range], and my employer offers a 401(k) with a [X]% match. Explain my retirement account options and how the employer match works."
  </div>
</div>

<div class="lesson-section">
  <span class="section-label">The Numbers</span>
  <h2 class="section-title">How much do you actually need?</h2>
  <p>The common rule of thumb is that you'll need 70-80% of your pre-retirement income each year in retirement. But rules of thumb are just starting points. AI can help you build a more realistic estimate.</p>
  <p><strong>Estimating expenses:</strong> Ask AI to help you project your retirement expenses. Some costs decrease (commuting, work clothes) and some increase (healthcare, leisure). Walk through each category.</p>
  <p><strong>The 4% rule:</strong> A general guideline suggesting you can withdraw about 4% of your retirement savings per year without running out over a 30-year retirement. So if you need $40,000/year, you'd need about $1,000,000 saved. AI can model this with different assumptions.</p>
  <p><strong>Social Security:</strong> If you're eligible, Social Security provides a foundation. But it typically covers only a portion of retirement expenses. AI can explain how benefits are estimated, though actual amounts depend on your work history.</p>
  <p><strong>Inflation:</strong> $40,000 today won't buy $40,000 worth of goods in 30 years. AI can adjust your retirement needs for inflation, showing you the future-dollar amount you'll actually need.</p>
  <div class="callout">
    <strong>The power of compound growth:</strong> Ask AI: "Show me the difference between starting to save $300/month at age 25 vs. age 35, assuming a 7% average annual return until age 65." The difference is staggering — and it's not because of the extra $36,000 contributed. It's because of the extra decade of compound growth.
  </div>
</div>

<div class="lesson-section">
  <span class="section-label">Starting Now</span>
  <h2 class="section-title">Starting late is better than not starting.</h2>
  <p>If you're in your 30s, 40s, or beyond and haven't started saving for retirement, the worst thing you can do is nothing. The second worst thing is panic. Here's the practical approach:</p>
  <p><strong>Step 1: Start with your employer match.</strong> If your employer offers a match, contribute at least enough to get the full match. It's the highest guaranteed return available to you.</p>
  <p><strong>Step 2: Calculate your gap.</strong> Use AI to estimate where you'd be at retirement age with your current savings and contributions. Then ask what increasing contributions by $100, $200, or $500/month would do.</p>
  <p><strong>Step 3: Automate.</strong> Set up automatic contributions so saving happens without decisions. Increase the amount whenever your income goes up.</p>
  <p><strong>Step 4: Review annually.</strong> Once a year, revisit your retirement plan with AI. Update your numbers, model scenarios, and adjust your contributions.</p>
  <div class="tip-box">
    <strong>Don't let perfect be the enemy of started.</strong> Saving $50/month is better than $0/month while you figure out the "optimal" strategy. Start, then optimize.
  </div>
</div>

<div class="lesson-section">
  <span class="section-label">AI Planning</span>
  <h2 class="section-title">Using AI for retirement scenario modeling.</h2>
  <p>One of AI's greatest strengths is running "what if" scenarios instantly:</p>
  <p>"What if I retire at 62 instead of 65?"</p>
  <p>"What if I increase contributions by 2% every year?"</p>
  <p>"What if inflation averages 4% instead of 3%?"</p>
  <p>"What if I have a major expense at age 55?"</p>
  <p>Each scenario takes AI seconds to model. Use this power to stress-test your plan and make decisions with data instead of anxiety. The more scenarios you explore now, the fewer surprises you'll face later.</p>
</div>
