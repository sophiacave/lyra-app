<div class="lesson-section">
  <span class="section-label">How It Works</span>
  <h2 class="section-title">Credit scoring is a formula, not a mystery.</h2>
  <p>Your credit score isn't a judgment of your character. It's a number generated by an algorithm based on five factors. Understanding these factors lets you optimize strategically instead of guessing.</p>
  <p><strong>Payment history (35%):</strong> The biggest factor. Have you paid on time? Late payments, collections, and bankruptcies hurt here. On-time payments build it steadily.</p>
  <p><strong>Credit utilization (30%):</strong> How much of your available credit are you using? Using $3,000 of a $10,000 limit = 30% utilization. Lower is generally better. Under 30% is the common guideline. Under 10% is optimal.</p>
  <p><strong>Length of history (15%):</strong> How long have your accounts been open? Longer history generally helps. This is why closing old accounts can sometimes lower your score.</p>
  <p><strong>Credit mix (10%):</strong> Having different types of credit (credit cards, installment loans, etc.) can help. But don't take on debt just to diversify your mix.</p>
  <p><strong>New credit inquiries (10%):</strong> Applying for lots of credit in a short period can lower your score. Each "hard inquiry" has a small, temporary impact.</p>
  <div class="tip-box">
    <strong>AI analysis:</strong> Pull your free credit report and share the summary (not your SSN or account numbers) with AI. Ask it to identify which of the five factors is your weakest area and suggest improvement strategies.
  </div>
</div>

<div class="lesson-section">
  <span class="section-label">Improvement</span>
  <h2 class="section-title">AI-powered credit improvement strategies.</h2>
  <p>AI can help you build a personalized credit improvement plan. Here's the framework:</p>
  <p><strong>Utilization optimization:</strong> Tell AI your credit limits and current balances. Ask it to calculate your per-card and overall utilization rates. If any card is over 30%, AI can suggest a payment priority order to bring utilization down fastest.</p>
  <p><strong>Payment scheduling:</strong> AI can help you set up a payment calendar that ensures you never miss a due date. It can also suggest optimal payment timing — paying before the statement closing date can lower your reported utilization.</p>
  <p><strong>Error detection:</strong> Review your credit report with AI. Ask it to flag anything that looks incorrect: accounts you don't recognize, wrong balances, duplicate entries. Errors on credit reports are common, and disputing them can boost your score.</p>
  <p><strong>Score simulation:</strong> Ask AI: "If I pay down my highest-utilization card by $500, roughly how much might my score improve?" AI can estimate based on the scoring factors, though actual results vary by scoring model.</p>
  <div class="callout">
    <strong>The fastest credit score boost:</strong> Pay down your highest-utilization credit card below 10% of its limit. This single action can improve scores quickly because utilization updates monthly and has immediate impact.
  </div>
</div>

<div class="lesson-section">
  <span class="section-label">Myths</span>
  <h2 class="section-title">Credit myths that keep people stuck.</h2>
  <p><strong>Myth: Checking your own credit hurts your score.</strong> False. Checking your own credit is a "soft inquiry" and has zero impact on your score. Check it regularly.</p>
  <p><strong>Myth: You need to carry a balance to build credit.</strong> False. You build credit by using your card and paying it off. Carrying a balance just costs you interest. Pay in full every month if you can.</p>
  <p><strong>Myth: Closing old cards improves your score.</strong> Usually false. Closing an old card reduces your total available credit (increasing utilization) and shortens your average account age. Keep old accounts open unless there's an annual fee you can't justify.</p>
  <p><strong>Myth: All debt is bad for your score.</strong> Not exactly. Having some credit accounts that you manage well actually builds your score. The key is responsible use, not avoidance.</p>
  <p><strong>Myth: Income affects your credit score.</strong> False. Your income doesn't appear in your credit score calculation at all. A person earning $30K can have a higher score than someone earning $300K.</p>
  <div class="tip-box">
    <strong>AI fact-check:</strong> Whenever you hear credit advice from friends, family, or social media, ask AI to verify it. "Is it true that [claim about credit]?" AI can explain the actual mechanics and cite the scoring factors.
  </div>
</div>

<div class="lesson-section">
  <span class="section-label">Monitoring</span>
  <h2 class="section-title">Build a credit monitoring habit.</h2>
  <p><strong>Monthly:</strong> Check your credit score through your bank or a free monitoring service. Look for unexpected changes. If your score dropped, ask AI to help you figure out why based on recent activity.</p>
  <p><strong>Quarterly:</strong> Pull your free credit report and review it in detail. Use AI to scan for errors, new accounts, and changes in your history.</p>
  <p><strong>Annually:</strong> Review your full credit strategy. Ask AI: "Based on my current score, utilization, account ages, and history, what's the most impactful thing I can do this year to improve?"</p>
  <p>Credit building is a long game. Most improvements take 3-6 months to show up. Be patient, be consistent, and let AI help you track progress.</p>
</div>
