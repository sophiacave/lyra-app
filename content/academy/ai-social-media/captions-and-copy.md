<div class="lesson-section">
  <span class="section-label">Caption Psychology</span>
  <h2 class="section-title">Your caption isn't a description. It's a second chance to stop the scroll.</h2>
  <p class="section-text">On Instagram, the first line of your caption appears before the "more" button. That line determines whether anyone reads the rest. On Twitter/X, you get one shot in 280 characters. On LinkedIn, the first 3 lines are visible before the fold. Every platform gives you a tiny window, and the rest is hidden. Treat that visible portion like a billboard: it needs to deliver a complete reason to engage.</p>
  <p class="section-text">Captions that drive engagement do one of four things: <strong>spark debate</strong> ("Hot take: [controversial opinion]"), <strong>invite participation</strong> ("Drop your [X] in the comments"), <strong>provide immediate value</strong> ("Save this for later: [useful information]"), or <strong>create emotional resonance</strong> ("Nobody talks about the [real thing] behind [common thing]"). Every caption should have a clear job. If you can't name the job, rewrite it.</p>
</div>

<div class="lesson-section">
  <span class="section-label">Hook Formulas</span>
  <h2 class="section-title">11 hook formulas that work on every platform. Memorize them.</h2>
  <p class="section-text">1. <strong>The Contrarian:</strong> "Everything you've been told about [X] is wrong." 2. <strong>The Specific Number:</strong> "I spent 437 hours testing [X]. Here's what I found." 3. <strong>The Mistake:</strong> "Stop doing [common thing]. Do [better thing] instead." 4. <strong>The Story:</strong> "Last Tuesday, I [unexpected event]. Here's what happened." 5. <strong>The Question:</strong> "Why does nobody talk about [obvious thing]?"</p>
  <p class="section-text">6. <strong>The List:</strong> "5 [things] that will [desirable outcome]." 7. <strong>The Result:</strong> "I went from [bad state] to [good state] in [timeframe]." 8. <strong>The Secret:</strong> "[Authority figure] doesn't want you to know about [X]." 9. <strong>The Challenge:</strong> "I bet you can't [do X] without [Y]." 10. <strong>The Confession:</strong> "I've been [doing X] for [Y years] and I was wrong about [Z]." 11. <strong>The Bridge:</strong> "Most people do [X]. Top performers do [Y]."</p>
  <p class="section-text">AI prompt: "I'm posting about [topic] on [platform]. Using these 11 hook formulas, generate 2 hooks for each formula. Then rank the top 5 overall by likely engagement rate for my audience of [describe audience]. Explain why each top pick works."</p>
  <div class="callout">
    <strong>The specificity principle:</strong> "I tested 437 hours" beats "I spent a long time testing." "She made $12,847 in 6 weeks" beats "She made a lot of money quickly." Specific numbers trigger credibility signals in the reader's brain. AI can help you find and emphasize specific data points in your content.
  </div>
</div>

<div class="lesson-section">
  <span class="section-label">Copywriting Frameworks</span>
  <h2 class="section-title">Steal these frameworks from the $400 billion advertising industry.</h2>
  <p class="section-text"><strong>PAS (Problem-Agitate-Solve):</strong> Name the problem your audience has. Agitate it by describing how it feels. Offer your solution. Example: "Spending 3 hours making one Reel? (Problem) While other creators pump out 5 a day and you wonder what you're doing wrong? (Agitate) Here's the 30-minute workflow I use to batch a week's content. (Solve)"</p>
  <p class="section-text"><strong>AIDA (Attention-Interest-Desire-Action):</strong> Grab attention with a hook. Build interest with a surprising fact or story. Create desire by showing the outcome. Close with a clear action. This works perfectly for Instagram carousel captions and LinkedIn posts.</p>
  <p class="section-text"><strong>BAB (Before-After-Bridge):</strong> Show the "before" state your audience is in. Paint the "after" they want. Build the bridge — your content — that gets them there. "Before: you're posting randomly and hoping something sticks. After: you have a system that generates a month of content in one afternoon. Bridge: this carousel breaks down the exact workflow."</p>
  <p class="section-text">AI prompt: "Rewrite this caption using the PAS framework. Keep my voice but structure it as: Problem (first line), Agitate (2-3 lines), Solve (2-3 lines), CTA (final line). The first line must work as a standalone hook above the fold."</p>
</div>

<div class="lesson-section">
  <span class="section-label">Voice Development</span>
  <h2 class="section-title">AI helps you write faster. Your voice makes people read.</h2>
  <p class="section-text">The biggest danger of using AI for captions: everything sounds the same. Generic, polished, forgettable. Your job is to develop a written voice that's so distinct, people recognize your posts without seeing your name. This takes time and intentionality.</p>
  <p class="section-text">Start by analyzing what you already have. Paste 10 of your best-performing captions into AI: "Analyze my writing style. What patterns do you see in sentence length, word choice, tone, humor, and structure? Describe my voice in 3 adjectives. Then, when I ask you to write captions, match this voice profile instead of your default style."</p>
  <p class="section-text">Then iterate. Every time AI writes a caption for you, rewrite at least 30% of it in your own words. Change the phrases that feel generic. Add your specific references, jokes, and opinions. Over time, you'll train AI to approximate your voice more closely — and you'll develop a stronger voice of your own through the editing process.</p>
  <div class="tip-box">
    <strong>The signature move:</strong> Develop one consistent element across all your captions. Maybe it's how you start (always with a question). Maybe it's how you end (always with a one-word sentence). Maybe it's a catchphrase or a recurring character. This becomes your brand's written fingerprint.
  </div>
</div>
