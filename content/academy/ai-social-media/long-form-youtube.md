<div class="lesson-section">
  <span class="section-label">The Algorithm</span>
  <h2 class="section-title">YouTube doesn't promote videos. It matches viewers with content they'll watch.</h2>
  <p class="section-text">YouTube's recommendation engine has one job: keep people on the platform. It does this by tracking two key metrics for every video: <strong>click-through rate (CTR)</strong> — the percentage of people who click when they see your thumbnail — and <strong>average view duration (AVD)</strong> — how long they actually watch. A video with a 10% CTR and 50% AVD will outperform a video with a 15% CTR and 20% AVD every time, because the second video is clickbait: people click but don't stay.</p>
  <p class="section-text">This means your job is two-fold: create packaging (title + thumbnail) that earns clicks, and create content that delivers on the promise so people watch. AI helps with both. But the order matters: never create a video and then figure out the title. Create the title and thumbnail FIRST, then make the video that delivers on that promise. This is how every major YouTuber works.</p>
</div>

<div class="lesson-section">
  <span class="section-label">Research & Scripting</span>
  <h2 class="section-title">AI turns 10 hours of research into 30 minutes.</h2>
  <p class="section-text">Before you shoot anything, validate the idea. Prompt: "I want to make a YouTube video about [topic]. Research the top 10 performing videos on this topic from the last 12 months. What angles have been covered? What angles are missing? What titles and thumbnails got the most views? What comment themes suggest viewers want more on specific subtopics?"</p>
  <p class="section-text">For scripting, use the HIVE structure: <strong>Hook</strong> (first 30 seconds — tease the payoff, create stakes), <strong>Intro</strong> (establish credibility and preview what they'll learn), <strong>Value sections</strong> (3-5 main points, each with a mini-hook, explanation, example, and transition), <strong>End</strong> (deliver the promised payoff, CTA for likes/subscribes, tease next video).</p>
  <p class="section-text">AI scripting prompt: "Write a 10-minute YouTube script using HIVE structure on [topic]. Each value section should have a re-hook to prevent drop-off. Include [B-ROLL] and [GRAPHIC] cues for editing. Write in a conversational tone — this will be spoken, not read. Include one 'open loop' in the first minute that doesn't get resolved until the end."</p>
  <div class="tip-box">
    <strong>The open loop technique:</strong> In your first 60 seconds, mention something fascinating that you'll explain later. "And there's one technique that 90% of creators get wrong — I'll show you exactly what it is in a minute." This creates psychological tension that keeps viewers watching to get the resolution.
  </div>
</div>

<div class="lesson-section">
  <span class="section-label">Packaging</span>
  <h2 class="section-title">Your title and thumbnail are more important than your content.</h2>
  <p class="section-text">That sounds extreme, but it's mathematically true. A great video with a bad thumbnail gets 0 views. A decent video with a great thumbnail gets 100,000. The content determines whether those viewers come back — but the packaging determines whether they show up in the first place.</p>
  <p class="section-text"><strong>Titles:</strong> Under 60 characters. Create curiosity or promise transformation. Use numbers when possible. Avoid clickbait that your video can't deliver on. AI prompt: "Generate 20 title options for a video about [topic]. Each title should be under 60 characters, create a curiosity gap or promise a clear transformation, and feel urgent enough to click. Rank the top 5 by expected CTR."</p>
  <p class="section-text"><strong>Thumbnails:</strong> One face with a strong emotion, 3-4 words of text maximum, high contrast colors, and a visual element that tells a story. AI image generators can create thumbnail concepts: "Design a YouTube thumbnail concept for a video titled '[title].' Include a face showing [emotion], text reading '[2-3 words],' and a background that suggests [context]. Use contrasting colors that pop on a small mobile screen."</p>
  <p class="section-text"><strong>SEO:</strong> Your description and tags still matter for search discovery. Ask AI to generate an SEO-optimized description: "Write a YouTube description for a video titled '[title]' about [topic]. Include the primary keyword in the first 2 lines. Add timestamps for each section. Include 3 relevant links. End with 10 relevant tags."</p>
</div>

<div class="lesson-section">
  <span class="section-label">Repurposing</span>
  <h2 class="section-title">One 10-minute video becomes 15 pieces of content. Here's the math.</h2>
  <p class="section-text">After you upload your video, AI transforms it into a content empire: 3-5 YouTube Shorts (pulled from the best moments using Opus Clip or manually), 3-5 TikToks/Reels (re-edited from Shorts with platform-specific hooks), a blog post (AI transcribes and reformats the script), a newsletter (AI pulls key insights into email format), a tweet thread (5-7 tweets summarizing main points), and 2-3 quote graphics (standout lines from the video).</p>
  <p class="section-text">Prompt: "Here's the transcript of my 10-minute YouTube video [paste]. Create: 1) 5 timestamps for the best 30-second clips to turn into Shorts, 2) a 500-word blog post version, 3) a tweet thread of the key points, 4) a newsletter paragraph summarizing the video for email subscribers, and 5) 3 standout quotes for graphic cards."</p>
  <p class="section-text">This is how creators who post "everywhere" actually do it. They don't create unique content for 6 platforms. They create one hero piece and let AI handle the rest. Your YouTube video is the engine; everything else is distribution.</p>
</div>
