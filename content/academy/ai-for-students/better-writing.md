<div class="lesson-section">
  <span class="section-label">The Line</span>
  <h2 class="section-title">There's a massive difference between AI-assisted writing and AI-generated writing.</h2>
  <p class="section-text">AI-generated writing: you type a prompt, AI writes the essay, you submit it. That's plagiarism. AI-assisted writing: you write the essay, then use AI to identify weak spots, sharpen arguments, and catch errors you missed. That's what professional writers, journalists, and academics already do — and it's the skill that will define your career.</p>
  <p class="section-text">Think of AI as a writing coach who's available 24/7. A coach doesn't play the game for you. They watch you play, point out what you're doing wrong, and suggest specific improvements. The work — the actual writing — stays yours. Your ideas, your voice, your arguments. Just... better.</p>
</div>

<div class="lesson-section">
  <span class="section-label">Structural Editing</span>
  <h2 class="section-title">The reverse outline: see your essay's skeleton before you fix it.</h2>
  <p class="section-text">After you've written a full draft, paste it into AI with this prompt: "Create a reverse outline of this essay. For each paragraph, write one sentence summarizing the main point. Then tell me: Does each paragraph have a clear topic sentence? Does the order of ideas flow logically? Are there any paragraphs that repeat the same point? Where are the logical gaps?"</p>
  <p class="section-text">This technique is taught in every college writing center, but most students never do it because it's tedious by hand. AI does it in seconds. The reverse outline shows you the architecture of your essay stripped of all the words. If the skeleton doesn't make sense, no amount of beautiful sentences will save it.</p>
  <p class="section-text">Once you see the structure, reorganize before you wordsmith. Move paragraphs. Cut repetition. Add transitions where the logical jumps are too large. This is where B+ papers become A papers — not in the sentences, but in the structure.</p>
  <div class="callout">
    <strong>The fix order matters:</strong> Structure first, then arguments, then paragraphs, then sentences, then grammar. Most students do this backward — they obsess over commas while their essay has no thesis. AI helps you work in the right order.
  </div>
</div>

<div class="lesson-section">
  <span class="section-label">Argument Strengthening</span>
  <h2 class="section-title">Make AI poke holes in your argument so your professor doesn't have to.</h2>
  <p class="section-text">The most powerful writing prompt isn't "make this better." It's: "Read this essay and identify the 3 weakest arguments. For each one, explain why it's weak and suggest what evidence or reasoning would make it stronger. Don't rewrite anything — just diagnose the problems."</p>
  <p class="section-text">This is what teaching assistants do when they grade your paper. They find the spots where your evidence is thin, your logic skips a step, or your claim is bigger than your support. AI does the same analysis, but you get it BEFORE submission instead of in red ink after.</p>
  <p class="section-text">Another killer prompt: "What would a professor who disagrees with my thesis say about this essay? What's the strongest counterargument I haven't addressed?" Engaging with opposition is the hallmark of sophisticated academic writing. It's also the thing most undergrads skip entirely.</p>
</div>

<div class="lesson-section">
  <span class="section-label">Voice Development</span>
  <h2 class="section-title">Your voice is the one thing AI can't create. Protect it.</h2>
  <p class="section-text">AI prose has a telltale flatness. It's competent but generic — like a well-dressed mannequin. Your writing has rhythm, quirks, opinions, and personality that no model can replicate. The goal is to keep your voice while eliminating the weaknesses around it.</p>
  <p class="section-text">Try this prompt: "Read this paragraph and identify my writing patterns — sentence length variation, word choice tendencies, tone. Then suggest improvements that maintain my style but fix any awkward phrasing, unclear sentences, or grammatical errors. Show the suggested changes inline so I can accept or reject each one."</p>
  <p class="section-text">The accept/reject part is crucial. Don't let AI batch-rewrite your paragraphs. Look at each suggestion individually. Some will be perfect catches. Others will smooth out the exact quirks that make your writing interesting. Reject those. Over time, you'll internalize the patterns AI catches, and your first drafts will get stronger on their own. That's the real goal: not better essays, but becoming a better writer.</p>
  <div class="tip-box">
    <strong>One skill at a time:</strong> Instead of asking AI to "improve everything," focus each revision on one specific skill. This week: transitions. Next week: evidence integration. The week after: conciseness. Targeted practice builds permanent skills faster than trying to fix everything at once.
  </div>
</div>
