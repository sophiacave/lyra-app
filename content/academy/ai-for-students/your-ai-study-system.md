<div class="lesson-section">
  <span class="section-label">The System</span>
  <h2 class="section-title">Individual techniques are useful. A system is transformative.</h2>
  <p class="section-text">Over the last 9 lessons, you've learned techniques for research, studying, writing, math, presentations, time management, collaboration, and applications. Each one works on its own. But the students who genuinely outperform aren't using isolated tricks — they have a system where every piece connects and reinforces the others.</p>
  <p class="section-text">Your AI study system has three layers: <strong>Daily habits</strong> (5-10 minutes), <strong>Weekly rituals</strong> (30-60 minutes), and <strong>Semester-level strategy</strong> (2-3 hours at the start of each semester). Each layer feeds the next. Daily habits create data. Weekly rituals analyze that data. Semester strategy sets the direction. Let's build each layer.</p>
</div>

<div class="lesson-section">
  <span class="section-label">Daily Layer</span>
  <h2 class="section-title">5 minutes a day that change everything.</h2>
  <p class="section-text"><strong>Morning (2 min):</strong> Check your AI-generated daily schedule. What are today's priorities? What's due this week? One glance at your plan eliminates the "what should I work on?" anxiety that wastes the first 30 minutes of every study session.</p>
  <p class="section-text"><strong>Study sessions:</strong> Use the techniques from this course as they come up. Flashcard review with spaced repetition (Lesson 3). Hint ladder for math problems (Lesson 5). Reverse outline for writing assignments (Lesson 4). You don't need to use AI in every session — just when it accelerates the specific task you're doing.</p>
  <p class="section-text"><strong>Evening (3 min):</strong> Quick brain dump to AI. "Today I studied [topics]. I struggled with [concepts]. I completed [tasks]." This creates a learning log that AI can analyze over time to identify your patterns: which subjects need more attention, when you're most productive, and which study methods work best for you specifically.</p>
  <div class="tip-box">
    <strong>The 3-min journal prompt:</strong> "Here's what I studied today and what I struggled with [paste your notes]. Give me 3 flashcard questions targeting my weak spots and one study suggestion for tomorrow." This turns daily reflection into tomorrow's action plan.
  </div>
</div>

<div class="lesson-section">
  <span class="section-label">Weekly Layer</span>
  <h2 class="section-title">The Sunday Session: 30 minutes that run your whole week.</h2>
  <p class="section-text"><strong>Review (10 min):</strong> Share your week's learning log with AI. "Based on my daily logs this week, what patterns do you see? Which subjects am I improving in? Where am I still struggling? Am I spending time proportional to each course's importance and difficulty?"</p>
  <p class="section-text"><strong>Plan (10 min):</strong> Build next week's schedule using the Eisenhower Matrix from Lesson 7. Paste upcoming deadlines, weight them by grade percentage, and let AI create a day-by-day plan. Block deep work sessions for high-priority items. Schedule flashcard review for courses with upcoming exams.</p>
  <p class="section-text"><strong>Prepare (10 min):</strong> Generate study materials for the coming week. Practice exam questions for courses with tests. Flashcards from last week's lectures. Reading summaries for upcoming classes so you can participate actively. Front-loading preparation means you're never scrambling the night before.</p>
</div>

<div class="lesson-section">
  <span class="section-label">Semester Layer</span>
  <h2 class="section-title">Start the semester with a strategic advantage.</h2>
  <p class="section-text">In the first week of each semester, spend 2-3 hours building your foundation: Feed all syllabi to AI and extract every deadline, exam date, and major assignment into one master timeline. Identify the highest-weighted assignments in each course — these are where your effort has the most impact on your GPA. Map out "crunch weeks" where multiple deadlines overlap and plan lighter weeks to front-load work for those periods.</p>
  <p class="section-text">Ask AI: "Here are my 5 courses this semester with their syllabi [paste]. Create a semester-at-a-glance timeline showing all major deadlines. Highlight the 3 weeks with the most overlapping deadlines. Suggest where I should start working on major projects to avoid crunch." This strategic view is worth more than 100 hours of reactive scrambling.</p>
</div>

<div class="lesson-section">
  <span class="section-label">Tool Selection</span>
  <h2 class="section-title">The best tool is the one you'll actually use.</h2>
  <p class="section-text"><strong>For general AI:</strong> Claude and ChatGPT are both excellent for writing, research, and study help. Pick the one you prefer and learn it deeply. Having one go-to tool you know well beats switching between five tools you barely understand.</p>
  <p class="section-text"><strong>For specific tasks:</strong> Anki for spaced repetition. Wolfram Alpha for math verification. Semantic Scholar or Elicit for academic research. Notion or Google Docs for note organization. Otter.ai for lecture transcription. Gamma or Canva for presentations. You don't need all of these — start with 2-3 and add more as needed.</p>
  <p class="section-text"><strong>Future-proofing principle:</strong> AI tools change fast. The skills you've built in this course — knowing WHEN to use AI, how to write effective prompts, how to verify AI output, and how to maintain your own learning — transfer to any tool. When a new AI comes out next year, you'll know exactly how to use it because you understand the principles, not just the buttons.</p>
  <div class="callout">
    <strong>The meta-skill:</strong> The most valuable thing you've learned isn't any single technique. It's the ability to think about learning itself — to understand how your brain works, where AI fits in, and how to design systems that make you smarter over time. That's a skill that compounds for the rest of your life, regardless of what AI looks like in 5 or 10 years.
  </div>
</div>
