<div class="lesson-section">
  <span class="section-label">The Real Problem</span>
  <h2 class="section-title">You don't have a time problem. You have a priority problem.</h2>
  <p class="section-text">Every student has the same 168 hours per week. The students who crush it aren't working more hours — they're spending their hours on the right things. A 2024 UCLA study found that students who planned their week in advance scored 12% higher on average than students who "figured it out as they went." Not because planning is magic — but because planning forces you to confront reality: you can't do everything, so you have to choose what matters most.</p>
  <p class="section-text">AI is the best planning assistant you'll ever have. It doesn't get tired, it doesn't forget your deadlines, and it can process your entire semester's worth of syllabi in 30 seconds to build a priority map you'd never create manually.</p>
</div>

<div class="lesson-section">
  <span class="section-label">The Weekly Build</span>
  <h2 class="section-title">Build your week on Sunday. Own it by Friday.</h2>
  <p class="section-text">Here's the prompt that changes your semester: "Here are my courses this semester with their syllabi [paste or describe]. It's currently Week [X]. List every deadline, exam, and project due in the next 2 weeks. Categorize each by: urgency (due soon vs. later) and weight (percentage of final grade). Then suggest a day-by-day study schedule that prioritizes high-weight, high-urgency items first."</p>
  <p class="section-text">The Eisenhower Matrix makes this visual. Four quadrants: <strong>Urgent + Important</strong> (do first — exam tomorrow, paper due tonight). <strong>Important + Not Urgent</strong> (schedule it — research paper due in 3 weeks, grad school applications). <strong>Urgent + Not Important</strong> (delegate or minimize — group chat drama, club meeting prep). <strong>Not Urgent + Not Important</strong> (eliminate — social media scrolling, Netflix binges during midterms).</p>
  <p class="section-text">Ask AI to sort your to-do list into these quadrants. Then build your week around Quadrant 2 — Important but Not Urgent. That's where the life-changing work happens: the projects, relationships, and skills that compound over time but never feel pressing until it's too late.</p>
  <div class="callout">
    <strong>The Sunday Ritual:</strong> Every Sunday, spend 15 minutes with AI reviewing the coming week. Update deadlines, adjust priorities, and block study time. Students who do this report 40% less stress about academics — not because the work changes, but because the uncertainty disappears.
  </div>
</div>

<div class="lesson-section">
  <span class="section-label">Focus Science</span>
  <h2 class="section-title">Your brain can focus for 25 minutes. Design around that.</h2>
  <p class="section-text"><strong>The Pomodoro Technique:</strong> Work for 25 minutes with zero distractions. Take a 5-minute break. After 4 rounds, take a 15-30 minute break. This works because your prefrontal cortex — the focus center — fatigues like a muscle. Short bursts with recovery maintain peak performance. There are dozens of free Pomodoro apps; pick one and start today.</p>
  <p class="section-text"><strong>Time blocking:</strong> Instead of a to-do list, assign every task to a specific time slot on your calendar. "Study biology" becomes "Study biology Chapter 7, Tuesday 2-3:30pm, library." The specificity eliminates decision fatigue — you never have to think about what to work on because you already decided.</p>
  <p class="section-text"><strong>Deep work protocol:</strong> For high-stakes tasks (essays, problem sets, exam prep), you need 90+ minutes of uninterrupted focus. Phone in another room. Notifications off. Door closed. AI can help here too: before a deep work session, ask AI to break your task into a clear sequence of steps so you spend zero time during the session figuring out what to do next.</p>
</div>

<div class="lesson-section">
  <span class="section-label">Automation</span>
  <h2 class="section-title">Automate the boring stuff so you can focus on the hard stuff.</h2>
  <p class="section-text">AI can handle the administrative overhead that eats your time: formatting citations, converting notes between formats, generating email drafts to professors, creating to-do lists from syllabi, summarizing readings before class so you can participate actively. None of this is cheating — it's efficiency.</p>
  <p class="section-text">Set up these automations once and save hours every week: Have AI extract all deadlines from your syllabi into one master calendar. Use AI to convert lecture recordings (via transcription tools like Otter.ai) into organized study notes. Ask AI to generate weekly review questions from your notes every Friday. Each automation removes a friction point that was stopping you from doing the real work.</p>
  <div class="tip-box">
    <strong>The 2-minute rule:</strong> If a task takes less than 2 minutes, do it now. If it takes longer, schedule it. If AI can do it, let AI do it. This simple framework eliminates task anxiety and keeps your to-do list from becoming overwhelming.
  </div>
</div>
