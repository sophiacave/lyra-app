<div class="lesson-section">
  <span class="section-label">The Real Issue</span>
  <h2 class="section-title">Group projects don't fail because of the work. They fail because of the coordination.</h2>
  <p class="section-text">You've lived this: four people in a group, one does 60% of the work, two do 30% combined, and one ghosts until the night before. A 2024 survey of 5,000 college students found that 78% said group projects were their most stressful assignment — not because the work was hard, but because coordinating humans is hard. AI can't fix your lazy groupmate, but it can make the coordination so frictionless that there's nowhere to hide.</p>
  <p class="section-text">The key insight: most group project friction comes from ambiguity. Who's doing what? When is it due? What does "done" look like? AI eliminates ambiguity by creating clear structures at the very beginning, when everyone's still motivated.</p>
</div>

<div class="lesson-section">
  <span class="section-label">The RACI Framework</span>
  <h2 class="section-title">Define who does what before anyone does anything.</h2>
  <p class="section-text">RACI stands for: <strong>Responsible</strong> (who does the work), <strong>Accountable</strong> (who makes sure it gets done), <strong>Consulted</strong> (who gives input), <strong>Informed</strong> (who needs to know it's done). For every task in your project, each person gets exactly one letter. This eliminates "I thought YOU were doing that" disasters.</p>
  <p class="section-text">Feed your project requirements to AI: "We have a group project on [topic] due [date]. There are [N] people in our group. The deliverables are [list]. Create a RACI matrix that divides work equally based on these deliverables, with weekly milestones and specific due dates for each person's contribution."</p>
  <p class="section-text">Share the AI-generated RACI matrix in your first group meeting. Adjust it together. Now everyone has signed off on their responsibilities — and there's a written record. When someone drops the ball in Week 3, you have documentation, not drama.</p>
  <div class="tip-box">
    <strong>Equal doesn't mean identical:</strong> Ask AI to divide work based on each person's strengths. "Alex is good at data analysis, Jordan excels at writing, Sam is strongest at design, and Taylor is the best presenter. Redistribute the RACI matrix to play to these strengths." This gets better output AND keeps everyone engaged.
  </div>
</div>

<div class="lesson-section">
  <span class="section-label">Coordination Tools</span>
  <h2 class="section-title">Set up your system once. Let it run the project for you.</h2>
  <p class="section-text"><strong>Shared docs with AI integration:</strong> Google Docs + Notion AI or Microsoft Loop give your whole team a shared workspace where AI can help with drafting, editing, and organizing. Create one master document with sections for each person. Everyone works in their section, AI helps merge and harmonize the final product.</p>
  <p class="section-text"><strong>Progress tracking:</strong> Use a simple Kanban board (Trello, Notion, or even a shared Google Sheet) with three columns: To Do, In Progress, Done. Ask AI to create the initial board from your RACI matrix. Every week, each person moves their tasks. At a glance, you can see who's on track and who's falling behind.</p>
  <p class="section-text"><strong>Meeting efficiency:</strong> Before each group meeting, ask AI to generate an agenda from your progress board: "Based on our project timeline and current progress, create a 30-minute meeting agenda that covers: what's been completed, what's behind schedule, decisions we need to make, and next actions." This turns vague catch-up sessions into productive work meetings.</p>
</div>

<div class="lesson-section">
  <span class="section-label">Handling Conflict</span>
  <h2 class="section-title">When things go wrong — and they will — AI helps you respond, not react.</h2>
  <p class="section-text">Groupmate not pulling their weight? Before sending an angry text, try this: "I'm in a group project and one member hasn't submitted their section. The deadline is in [X] days. Help me draft a message that's direct but respectful, acknowledges they might have stuff going on, and clearly states what needs to happen and by when." AI drafts better conflict messages than you will when you're frustrated at midnight.</p>
  <p class="section-text">If someone's work quality isn't up to standard: don't rewrite it yourself (that's how you end up doing 80% of the project). Instead, use AI to create specific, actionable feedback: "This section on [topic] needs improvement. Here are 3 specific changes: [1] add a citation supporting the claim in paragraph 2, [2] the transition between sections 2 and 3 doesn't flow — connect them with a topic sentence, [3] the conclusion restates the intro instead of synthesizing." Specific feedback is easier to act on than "this needs work."</p>
  <div class="callout">
    <strong>The nuclear option (documented):</strong> If someone truly ghosts the project, document everything: the RACI matrix, your messages, the progress board showing their tasks untouched. Email your professor with this documentation before the deadline. "Professor, here's our project plan, the assignment of responsibilities everyone agreed to, and the current status. [Name] has not completed their assigned sections despite multiple attempts to reach them." Documentation turns a he-said-she-said into an open-and-shut case.
  </div>
</div>
