<div class="lesson-section">
  <span class="section-label">The Stakes</span>
  <h2 class="section-title">Admissions officers read 30-50 essays a day. Yours has 90 seconds to matter.</h2>
  <p class="section-text">College admissions has fundamentally changed. In 2025, the average selective university received over 100,000 applications. Admissions officers spend an average of 7-8 minutes per application — and your essay gets maybe 90 seconds of that. Every word needs to earn its place. AI can't write your story (and admissions officers can detect AI-generated essays with alarming accuracy), but it can help you find the story worth telling and tell it with precision.</p>
  <p class="section-text">The biggest mistake: writing about what you think they want to hear. Admissions officers have read 10,000 "volunteering in Costa Rica changed my life" essays. They haven't read YOUR specific, weird, deeply personal story. That's what gets remembered. AI helps you dig past the cliches to find it.</p>
</div>

<div class="lesson-section">
  <span class="section-label">Finding Your Story</span>
  <h2 class="section-title">The best essays come from the smallest moments.</h2>
  <p class="section-text">Try this brainstorming prompt: "I need to write a college admissions essay (650 words). Instead of asking me what my accomplishments are, ask me 10 questions about small, specific moments in my life — times I was confused, surprised, frustrated, or changed my mind about something. Help me find a story that reveals who I am, not what I've done."</p>
  <p class="section-text">AI excels at this because it asks questions you wouldn't ask yourself. "What's something you believed at age 14 that you no longer believe?" "What's a rule you broke that you don't regret?" "When did you realize you were wrong about something important?" These questions surface the raw material for essays that feel authentic — because they are.</p>
  <p class="section-text">Once you have a moment, apply the "show don't tell" framework: <strong>Scene</strong> (put the reader in a specific place and time), <strong>Struggle</strong> (what was hard, confusing, or at stake), <strong>Shift</strong> (how your thinking or behavior changed), <strong>Significance</strong> (what this means about who you are now). This four-part structure creates narrative tension that keeps readers engaged.</p>
  <div class="callout">
    <strong>The authenticity test:</strong> After drafting, ask AI: "Could any other applicant have written this essay? Or is it specific enough that only I could have written it?" If the answer is "anyone could have written this," dig deeper into the details that make your experience unique.
  </div>
</div>

<div class="lesson-section">
  <span class="section-label">Essay Refinement</span>
  <h2 class="section-title">Draft it yourself. Then make AI your toughest editor.</h2>
  <p class="section-text">Write your first draft without AI. Seriously. The authenticity of your voice matters more than polish, and AI-assisted first drafts inevitably sound like AI. Get your raw thoughts on paper. Then bring AI in for editing.</p>
  <p class="section-text">Editing prompts that work: "Read my essay and highlight any sentences that feel generic — things any applicant might say. Suggest how I can replace them with specific details from my experience." And: "My essay is [X] words. I need to cut it to 650. Identify the 3 weakest sentences that could be removed without losing meaning."</p>
  <p class="section-text">For supplemental essays ("Why this school?"): research is everything. Ask AI "What are [University]'s signature programs, research centers, and unique opportunities in [your field]?" Then write about specific connections between those opportunities and your goals. "Your nanotechnology lab run by Dr. Chen aligns with my interest in..." beats "I love your diverse community" every single time.</p>
</div>

<div class="lesson-section">
  <span class="section-label">Resumes & Beyond</span>
  <h2 class="section-title">Your resume is a marketing document, not a biography.</h2>
  <p class="section-text">Most student resumes list responsibilities. Winning resumes quantify impact. Not "Tutored students in math" but "Tutored 12 students in AP Calculus, with 10 scoring 4 or 5 on the AP exam." AI transforms your experience descriptions with this prompt: "Here are my activities and jobs [list]. For each one, rewrite the description using the XYZ formula: Accomplished [X] as measured by [Y] by doing [Z]. Keep my voice but add specificity and impact."</p>
  <p class="section-text">For formatting: use a clean, single-column layout. ATS (Applicant Tracking Systems) scan resumes before humans see them, and they choke on fancy formatting, graphics, and multi-column layouts. AI can optimize your resume for both machines and humans: "Review my resume for ATS compatibility. Flag any formatting that might cause parsing errors. Then review it as a hiring manager and flag anything that's unclear, generic, or missing impact metrics."</p>
  <p class="section-text">For scholarship essays: the strategy shifts. Scholarship committees want to see need, merit, and alignment with their mission. Ask AI to analyze the scholarship's website and past recipients, then help you frame your story to match what they're looking for — without being dishonest. It's about emphasis, not fabrication.</p>
  <div class="tip-box">
    <strong>LinkedIn for students:</strong> Even in high school, a LinkedIn profile helps. Ask AI to write a student-appropriate summary: "I'm a [year] at [school] interested in [fields]. Write a LinkedIn summary that highlights my interests and goals without overstating my experience. Keep it under 100 words and genuine."
  </div>
