<div class="lesson-section">
  <span class="section-label">Name It to Tame It</span>
  <h2 class="section-title">Step one is always identification.</h2>
  <p>You can't regulate what you can't name. And most of us have a shockingly limited emotional vocabulary. We say "I feel bad" when we might actually feel disappointed, resentful, overwhelmed, lonely, or ashamed. Each of those feelings calls for a different response.</p>
  <p>AI is surprisingly good at helping you get specific. Try this: describe your physical sensations and situation to AI without using emotion words. "My chest is tight, I keep replaying a conversation from today, I don't want to eat, and I feel like staying in bed." AI can help you identify what emotion might match those signals.</p>
  <p>Research shows that simply naming an emotion accurately reduces its intensity. Neuroscientists call this "affect labeling" — your prefrontal cortex comes online and your amygdala calms down. The label itself is the first regulation tool.</p>
  <div class="tip-box">
    <strong>Prompt to try:</strong> "I'm going to describe how I feel physically and situationally. Help me identify the specific emotion or emotions I might be experiencing. Don't assume — ask clarifying questions."
  </div>
</div>

<div class="lesson-section">
  <span class="section-label">Regulation vs. Suppression</span>
  <h2 class="section-title">Feel it, don't fight it.</h2>
  <p>Emotional regulation is NOT about making feelings go away. It's about experiencing them without being controlled by them. There's a massive difference:</p>
  <p><strong>Suppression:</strong> "I shouldn't feel this way. Push it down. Ignore it." This backfires — suppressed emotions come back harder, often as anxiety, anger outbursts, or physical symptoms.</p>
  <p><strong>Regulation:</strong> "I feel angry. That's valid. I'm going to feel this anger without yelling at someone or breaking something." You acknowledge the emotion, allow it, and choose your response instead of reacting automatically.</p>
  <p>AI can guide you through this process in real time. When you're flooded with emotion, tell AI what's happening and ask for help processing — not eliminating — the feeling.</p>
</div>

<div class="lesson-section">
  <span class="section-label">Your Toolkit</span>
  <h2 class="section-title">Four AI-guided regulation techniques.</h2>
  <p><strong>1. The RAIN Method:</strong> Ask AI to walk you through RAIN — Recognize (what am I feeling?), Allow (let it be there), Investigate (where do I feel it in my body? what triggered it?), Nurture (what would I say to a friend feeling this?). This is a gold-standard mindfulness technique.</p>
  <p><strong>2. Cognitive Reappraisal:</strong> Tell AI the situation and your interpretation. Ask: "Help me find alternative explanations for what happened." Maybe your friend didn't text back because they're busy, not because they hate you. AI helps you generate interpretations your emotional brain missed.</p>
  <p><strong>3. The Opposite Action:</strong> When emotion is pushing you toward a behavior that won't help (isolating when lonely, lashing out when angry), tell AI and ask: "What's the opposite action here?" AI can help you identify and plan the healthy counter-move.</p>
  <p><strong>4. The 5-4-3-2-1 Grounding:</strong> For overwhelm or dissociation, ask AI to guide you through 5-4-3-2-1: name 5 things you see, 4 you hear, 3 you can touch, 2 you smell, 1 you taste. This pulls you back into your body and the present moment.</p>
  <div class="callout">
    <strong>Build your go-to list:</strong> Different emotions respond to different tools. AI can help you create a personal cheat sheet: "When I feel anxious, I use breathing. When I feel angry, I use opposite action. When I feel sad, I use RAIN." Having the plan before the moment is everything.
  </div>
</div>
