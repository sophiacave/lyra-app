<div class="lesson-section">
  <span class="section-label">Your Energy Map</span>
  <h2 class="section-title">Stop fighting your biology.</h2>
  <p>Most productivity advice assumes everyone peaks at 6 AM. That's wrong. Your energy follows a unique circadian pattern, and fighting it wastes willpower on something you can't change.</p>
  <p>AI can help you map your actual energy. Try tracking for one week: every two hours, rate your energy 1-10 and note what you're doing. Then feed this data to AI and ask: "Based on this log, when am I most energized, when do I crash, and what patterns do you see?"</p>
  <p>The insights are often surprising. Maybe you crash at 2 PM every day — that's not a character flaw, it's your cortisol dip. Maybe you get a second wind at 8 PM. Once you see the pattern, you can design your day around it instead of against it.</p>
  <div class="tip-box">
    <strong>Prompt:</strong> "I'm going to share a week of energy ratings. Analyze my patterns and suggest how to restructure my day so I'm doing demanding work during peak energy and routine tasks during dips."
  </div>
</div>

<div class="lesson-section">
  <span class="section-label">Sleep Optimization</span>
  <h2 class="section-title">AI-personalized sleep hygiene.</h2>
  <p>You already know the basics: dark room, cool temperature, no screens. But generic sleep advice misses the specifics of your life. AI can get personal:</p>
  <p><strong>The sleep audit:</strong> Tell AI everything — what time you go to bed, when you actually fall asleep, how often you wake up, what your evening looks like, caffeine intake, exercise timing, stress levels, and what your mind does when you lie down. AI identifies the specific factors disrupting your sleep.</p>
  <p><strong>The wind-down protocol:</strong> Ask AI to design a 30-minute pre-sleep routine based on your triggers. If racing thoughts keep you up, it might emphasize a brain dump journal. If physical tension is the issue, progressive muscle relaxation. If screen addiction is the culprit, a structured screen-off ritual.</p>
  <p><strong>The wake-up design:</strong> How you wake up matters as much as how you sleep. AI can help you design a morning that doesn't start with cortisol-spiking alarm stress. Gradual light, gentle alarms, and a first-30-minutes routine that eases you in.</p>
</div>

<div class="lesson-section">
  <span class="section-label">Routine Design</span>
  <h2 class="section-title">Build a day that works for your brain.</h2>
  <p>AI excels at helping you design daily routines because it can hold all the variables at once — your energy patterns, obligations, preferences, and constraints. Here's the process:</p>
  <ol>
    <li><strong>List your non-negotiables.</strong> Work hours, childcare, meals, medication, appointments. These are fixed anchors.</li>
    <li><strong>Map your energy peaks and dips.</strong> From your energy audit above.</li>
    <li><strong>Assign tasks to energy levels.</strong> Creative work at peaks. Admin at dips. Exercise when you have moderate energy.</li>
    <li><strong>Build in transitions.</strong> Most people underestimate the time between tasks. AI can add realistic buffer time.</li>
    <li><strong>Include recovery.</strong> Rest is not laziness. AI can schedule genuine downtime, not just leftover time after everything else.</li>
  </ol>
  <div class="callout">
    <strong>Watch for the optimization trap:</strong> Optimizing your routine should reduce stress, not create it. If you're anxious about sticking to a perfect schedule, you've gone too far. Tell AI: "Make this routine flexible enough that missing one thing doesn't ruin my day."
  </div>
</div>
