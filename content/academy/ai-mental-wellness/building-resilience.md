<div class="lesson-section">
  <span class="section-label">What Resilience Really Is</span>
  <h2 class="section-title">It's not about being tough. It's about being flexible.</h2>
  <p>Resilience has been hijacked by hustle culture. "Just be resilient" often means "suffer without complaining." That's not resilience. That's suppression with a marketing budget.</p>
  <p>Real resilience is the ability to adapt to adversity, recover from setbacks, and grow from difficult experiences. It doesn't mean you don't feel pain. It means pain doesn't permanently define you. Resilient people cry, struggle, ask for help, and have bad days. They just keep moving — sometimes slowly, sometimes on hands and knees.</p>
  <p>And here's what research consistently shows: resilience isn't a fixed trait. It's a set of skills and practices you can build. Which makes it the perfect thing to train with AI.</p>
  <div class="callout">
    <strong>Toxic positivity vs. resilience:</strong> "Everything happens for a reason" is toxic positivity. "This is really hard, and I'm going to figure out how to get through it" is resilience. The difference is honesty about the pain.
  </div>
</div>

<div class="lesson-section">
  <span class="section-label">Four Practices</span>
  <h2 class="section-title">AI-guided resilience building.</h2>
  <p><strong>1. The Adversity Audit:</strong> Tell AI about a past challenge you survived. Ask: "Help me identify what I did that worked, what strengths I showed, and what I learned." Most people never do this — they just move on. But explicitly naming your survival skills makes them available for next time.</p>
  <p><strong>2. Pre-Adversity Planning:</strong> Ask AI: "What's a realistic challenge I might face in the next 6 months?" Then plan your response in advance. "If I lose my job, my first three steps would be..." This isn't pessimism — it's preparation. It reduces panic when hard things happen because your brain has already rehearsed the response.</p>
  <p><strong>3. The Meaning-Making Conversation:</strong> After something difficult, use AI to process: "I went through [experience]. I'm not looking for silver linings. I want to understand what this experience taught me about myself." AI guides you toward post-traumatic growth without forcing fake positivity.</p>
  <p><strong>4. The Strengths Inventory:</strong> Ask AI to help you list your coping strengths based on your life history. "Based on everything I've told you about my life, what are my top resilience strengths?" Seeing them listed out builds genuine confidence — not the fake kind, but the kind rooted in evidence of what you've already survived.</p>
  <div class="tip-box">
    <strong>Key insight:</strong> Resilience doesn't mean going it alone. Asking for help — from humans, from AI, from professionals — is one of the strongest resilience behaviors there is.
  </div>
</div>

<div class="lesson-section">
  <span class="section-label">Daily Resilience Habits</span>
  <h2 class="section-title">Small practices that compound over time.</h2>
  <p>Resilience isn't built during crises. It's built in the quiet moments between them. These daily micro-practices add up:</p>
  <ul>
    <li><strong>The daily win:</strong> Every evening, tell AI one thing you did well today. It doesn't have to be big. "I made it through a hard meeting without shutting down." AI reflects it back and helps you see the pattern of competence you're building.</li>
    <li><strong>The reframe practice:</strong> Once a day, take one negative thought and ask AI to help you find an alternative perspective. Not a positive spin — an accurate alternative. This builds cognitive flexibility, which is the core of resilience.</li>
    <li><strong>The gratitude specificity:</strong> Instead of generic gratitude, get specific with AI. Not "I'm grateful for my family" but "I'm grateful that my sister texted to check on me today because it reminded me I'm not invisible." Specific gratitude activates different brain pathways than generic gratitude.</li>
  </ul>
</div>
