<div class="lesson-section">
  <span class="section-label">Important Disclaimer</span>
  <h2 class="section-title">Let's be crystal clear about this.</h2>
  <div class="callout">
    <strong>AI is NOT a therapist, counselor, or mental health professional.</strong> It cannot diagnose conditions, prescribe treatment, or replace professional care. Nothing in this course is medical advice. If you are in crisis, skip to Lesson 8 for real crisis resources, or call the 988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline right now. AI is a wellness tool — like a journal, a meditation app, or a fitness tracker. It supports your wellbeing. It does not treat mental illness.
  </div>
</div>

<div class="lesson-section">
  <span class="section-label">What AI Can Do</span>
  <h2 class="section-title">The real, practical value of AI for wellness.</h2>
  <p>Now that we've drawn the line, let's talk about what AI genuinely does well for mental wellness. Because it does a lot:</p>
  <p><strong>It's available 24/7.</strong> Your therapist has office hours. Your friends are asleep at 3 AM. AI is always there when you need to process a thought, vent, or work through something. That accessibility fills a real gap.</p>
  <p><strong>It doesn't judge.</strong> Many people struggle to talk openly about their feelings with other humans. AI removes the fear of judgment entirely. You can be completely honest without social consequences.</p>
  <p><strong>It's infinitely patient.</strong> You can ask the same question 15 times. You can explain the same problem from 15 angles. AI won't get frustrated, roll its eyes, or tell you to move on.</p>
  <p><strong>It can guide practices.</strong> AI can walk you through breathing exercises, help you journal, suggest coping strategies, and create personalized wellness routines. It's like having a wellness coach in your pocket.</p>
  <div class="tip-box">
    <strong>Think of it this way:</strong> AI is like a really good self-help book that talks back. It can provide frameworks, strategies, and support — but it can't provide a therapeutic relationship, clinical assessment, or emergency intervention.
  </div>
</div>

<div class="lesson-section">
  <span class="section-label">The Hard Limits</span>
  <h2 class="section-title">Where AI stops and humans must start.</h2>
  <p>This matters. Please take it seriously:</p>
  <ul>
    <li><strong>AI cannot diagnose you.</strong> If you ask it "do I have depression?" it might give you a checklist, but it cannot assess your actual condition. Only a licensed professional can do that.</li>
    <li><strong>AI cannot handle crises.</strong> If you're in danger, thinking about self-harm, or experiencing a psychiatric emergency, AI is the wrong tool. Call 988 or text HOME to 741741.</li>
    <li><strong>AI doesn't actually care about you.</strong> It generates supportive-sounding text because that's what patterns predict. It's useful — but it's not empathy. Don't mistake pattern-matching for love.</li>
    <li><strong>AI can reinforce unhealthy patterns.</strong> If you're seeking validation for harmful behaviors, AI might provide it. It generally agrees with you. A therapist pushes back when needed.</li>
    <li><strong>AI conversations aren't confidential.</strong> Unlike therapy, your AI conversations may be stored, reviewed, and used for training. Don't share anything you wouldn't want a company to see.</li>
  </ul>
</div>

<div class="lesson-section">
  <span class="section-label">Your Wellness Stack</span>
  <h2 class="section-title">AI works best as part of a team.</h2>
  <p>The healthiest approach is layered. Think of your wellness support as a stack:</p>
  <ul>
    <li><strong>Foundation:</strong> Professional care (therapy, psychiatry, medical providers) if you have access.</li>
    <li><strong>Community:</strong> Friends, family, support groups, peer connections.</li>
    <li><strong>Daily practice:</strong> Exercise, sleep, nutrition, mindfulness.</li>
    <li><strong>Tools:</strong> AI, journals, meditation apps, mood trackers.</li>
  </ul>
  <p>AI lives in the tools layer. It's valuable there. But it should never be your only layer, and it should never replace the foundation. If you can't access professional care right now, AI can help you cope — but keep working toward getting human support.</p>
</div>
