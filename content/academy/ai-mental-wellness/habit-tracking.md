<div class="lesson-section">
  <span class="section-label">Why AI Changes Habit Building</span>
  <h2 class="section-title">Generic advice doesn't work. Personalized systems do.</h2>
  <p>You've read the articles. Wake up at 5 AM. Drink water. Meditate. Exercise. Journal. The advice isn't wrong — it's just generic. And generic doesn't account for your actual life: your schedule, your energy levels, your obstacles, your brain.</p>
  <p>AI can build a habit system around your reality. Tell it your schedule, your struggles, your goals, and your past failures. It creates something that fits your life instead of an idealized version of it. That's the difference between a habit that sticks and a habit that lasts three days.</p>
  <div class="tip-box">
    <strong>Try this prompt:</strong> "I want to build a daily meditation habit, but I have ADHD and can't sit still for 10 minutes. I have the most energy at lunch. I've failed at this three times before. Help me design something that actually works for my brain."
  </div>
</div>

<div class="lesson-section">
  <span class="section-label">The System</span>
  <h2 class="section-title">Build your habit stack with AI.</h2>
  <p>Here's a step-by-step process for using AI to build habits that stick:</p>
  <p><strong>Step 1: Audit.</strong> Tell AI about your current daily routine. Be honest — include the Netflix, the doomscrolling, the skipped meals. AI can't help with a fictional version of your life.</p>
  <p><strong>Step 2: Prioritize.</strong> Ask AI: "Based on what I told you, what's the ONE habit that would create the most positive ripple effect?" Start with one. Not five. One.</p>
  <p><strong>Step 3: Design.</strong> Work with AI to make the habit tiny, specific, and attached to an existing behavior. "Meditate for 2 minutes after I pour my coffee" is better than "meditate daily."</p>
  <p><strong>Step 4: Troubleshoot.</strong> Tell AI every reason you think you'll fail. Let it help you design around those obstacles before they happen. This is called "implementation intention" and it doubles your success rate.</p>
  <p><strong>Step 5: Check in.</strong> Use AI as your daily check-in partner. "Hey, I did my meditation today" or "I skipped it, here's why." AI provides consistent, non-judgmental accountability.</p>
</div>

<div class="lesson-section">
  <span class="section-label">Compassionate Accountability</span>
  <h2 class="section-title">AI won't shame you — and that's the point.</h2>
  <p>Most accountability systems are built on guilt. You missed a day, the streak breaks, you feel terrible, and you quit. AI offers something different: accountability without shame.</p>
  <p>When you tell AI you skipped your habit, it doesn't guilt you. It asks what happened, helps you understand the obstacle, and adjusts the plan. This mirrors what good therapists and coaches do — they get curious about failures instead of punishing them.</p>
  <p><strong>Prompts for tough days:</strong></p>
  <ul>
    <li>"I didn't do my habit today and I feel bad about it. Help me reframe this."</li>
    <li>"I've missed three days in a row. Should I adjust my habit or my expectations?"</li>
    <li>"I'm losing motivation. Remind me why I started this and help me find a spark."</li>
  </ul>
  <div class="callout">
    <strong>The truth about streaks:</strong> A broken streak is not a failure. It's data. What happened on the day you missed? What was different? That information makes your system stronger. AI helps you see missed days as information, not indictments.
  </div>
</div>
