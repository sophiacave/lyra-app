<div class="lesson-section">
  <span class="section-label">The Automation Gap</span>
  <h2 class="section-title">Small businesses are drowning in manual work they don't need to do.</h2>
  <p class="section-text">Every small business has processes that look like this: someone receives an email, copies data into a spreadsheet, sends a notification to a team member, updates a CRM, and triggers a follow-up task. Every step is manual. Every step is a point of failure. And every step can be automated.</p>
  <p class="section-text">The average small business wastes 20-30% of employee time on tasks that could be fully automated. That's $15,000-$40,000 per employee per year in wasted labor. When you walk in and offer to eliminate that waste for a one-time fee of $2,000, the ROI is so obvious it sells itself.</p>
  <p class="section-text">You don't need to write code. Modern automation platforms are visual, drag-and-drop, and increasingly AI-powered. If you can draw a flowchart, you can build an automation.</p>
</div>

<div class="lesson-section">
  <span class="section-label">The Platform Stack</span>
  <h2 class="section-title">Three tools. Infinite combinations. Master these and you can automate anything.</h2>
  <p class="section-text"><strong>Make.com (formerly Integromat):</strong> The most powerful visual automation tool. Connects 1,500+ apps. The visual builder is intuitive — you literally draw the workflow. Free tier: 1,000 operations/month. Pro: $9/month. This is your primary tool for complex, multi-step automations.</p>
  <p class="section-text"><strong>Zapier:</strong> The market leader with 6,000+ app integrations. Simpler than Make but less flexible. Best for straightforward "when X happens, do Y" automations. Free tier: 100 tasks/month. Pro: $19.99/month. Use this when clients already have a Zapier account.</p>
  <p class="section-text"><strong>n8n:</strong> The open-source alternative. Self-hosted, no per-operation limits, highly customizable. More technical but zero recurring costs after setup. Use this for clients who need heavy-volume automations or want to own their infrastructure.</p>
  <div class="tip-box">
    <strong>AI Integration Layer:</strong> All three platforms now support AI nodes — you can plug ChatGPT or Claude directly into automations. Example: incoming customer email &rarr; AI categorizes urgency &rarr; drafts a personalized response &rarr; routes to the right team member &rarr; logs in CRM. That's a $2,000 automation that takes 2 hours to build.
  </div>
</div>

<div class="lesson-section">
  <span class="section-label">Finding Opportunities</span>
  <h2 class="section-title">The Automation Audit: find $5K worth of automations in any business in 30 minutes.</h2>
  <p class="section-text">Here's the exact script you use in a discovery call or meeting:</p>
  <p class="section-text"><strong>"Walk me through what happens when [trigger event]."</strong> Trigger events: a new lead comes in, an order is placed, an employee requests time off, a customer complains, a new team member starts. Each trigger reveals a chain of manual steps.</p>
  <p class="section-text"><strong>"How many times per day/week does this happen?"</strong> Frequency determines ROI. An automation that runs 5 times/day is worth 10x one that runs 5 times/month.</p>
  <p class="section-text"><strong>"What breaks when someone forgets a step?"</strong> This reveals the pain. Lost leads, missed invoices, angry customers. The bigger the pain, the higher you can price.</p>
  <p class="section-text"><strong>The five automations every small business needs:</strong></p>
  <p class="section-text">1. <strong>Lead Capture to CRM</strong> — Form submission or email &rarr; CRM entry &rarr; assignment &rarr; follow-up sequence. Price: $500-$1,000.</p>
  <p class="section-text">2. <strong>Invoice & Payment Processing</strong> — Completed work &rarr; auto-generate invoice &rarr; send to client &rarr; track payment &rarr; follow up on overdue. Price: $750-$1,500.</p>
  <p class="section-text">3. <strong>Social Media Scheduling</strong> — Content approval &rarr; auto-schedule across platforms &rarr; track engagement &rarr; weekly report. Price: $500-$1,000.</p>
  <p class="section-text">4. <strong>Employee Onboarding</strong> — New hire added &rarr; welcome email &rarr; account creation &rarr; task assignments &rarr; check-in reminders. Price: $1,000-$2,000.</p>
  <p class="section-text">5. <strong>Customer Support Triage</strong> — Incoming ticket &rarr; AI categorization &rarr; priority routing &rarr; auto-response for common issues &rarr; escalation for complex ones. Price: $1,500-$3,000.</p>
</div>

<div class="lesson-section">
  <span class="section-label">Pricing & Recurring Revenue</span>
  <h2 class="section-title">Build once, maintain monthly. That's how you build wealth.</h2>
  <p class="section-text"><strong>Project pricing:</strong> Charge $500-$5,000 per automation based on complexity, number of integrations, and business impact. Always price based on ROI, not hours. If your automation saves them $3,000/month in labor, charging $2,000 once is a steal.</p>
  <p class="section-text"><strong>Recurring maintenance:</strong> This is where the real money lives. Offer a monthly maintenance package: $200-$500/month for monitoring, updates, troubleshooting, and new automations. Automations break when APIs update, tools change, or businesses evolve. They need you.</p>
  <p class="section-text"><strong>The math:</strong> Build 2 automations per month ($2,000 average each = $4,000 project revenue). Sign both clients on $300/month maintenance. After 6 months: $4,000/month in project work + $1,800/month in recurring maintenance = $5,800/month. After 12 months: $4,000 + $3,600 recurring = $7,600/month. The recurring base keeps growing.</p>
  <p class="section-text"><strong>Portfolio effect:</strong> Every automation you build becomes a template you can sell to similar businesses. Built a lead capture automation for a dentist? Sell it to 20 other dentists with minor customization. One build, twenty sales.</p>
</div>
