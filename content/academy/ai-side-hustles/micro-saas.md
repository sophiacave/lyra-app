<div class="lesson-section">
  <span class="section-label">What Is Micro-SaaS</span>
  <h2 class="section-title">Solve one problem. Charge monthly. That's the whole game.</h2>
  <p class="section-text">Micro-SaaS is a tiny software tool that solves one specific problem for one specific audience and charges a monthly subscription. No VC funding. No team of 50. Just you, AI coding assistants, and a credit card processor.</p>
  <p class="section-text">Examples that print money: a Chrome extension that summarizes YouTube videos ($9/month, 5,000 users = $45K/month). A Slack bot that tracks team standup responses ($12/month per team). A simple tool that converts PDFs to branded proposals ($19/month for freelancers). None of these required a CS degree to build.</p>
  <p class="section-text">The magic of micro-SaaS is recurring revenue. A freelancer earning $5K/month has to win new projects constantly. A micro-SaaS founder earning $5K/month from 200 users at $25/month just needs to keep those users happy. Revenue compounds, effort flattens.</p>
</div>

<div class="lesson-section">
  <span class="section-label">Finding Ideas</span>
  <h2 class="section-title">Your best idea is hiding in someone else's complaint.</h2>
  <p class="section-text">Don't brainstorm in isolation. Hunt for problems in the wild. Here are the five best hunting grounds:</p>
  <p class="section-text"><strong>1. Reddit & Indie Hackers:</strong> Search subreddits like r/smallbusiness, r/freelance, or r/SaaS for posts starting with "I wish there was..." or "Is there a tool that..." Each one is a validated pain point.</p>
  <p class="section-text"><strong>2. Upwork & Fiverr:</strong> Look for services that are repetitive and rules-based. If someone pays $50/gig for something that could be automated, you've found a micro-SaaS idea. The service price tells you the willingness to pay.</p>
  <p class="section-text"><strong>3. Existing tool reviews:</strong> Read 1-star and 2-star reviews of popular SaaS tools. "I love Calendly but I wish it could..." is your product roadmap. Build the feature they want as a standalone tool.</p>
  <p class="section-text"><strong>4. Your own frustrations:</strong> What repetitive task do you do every week that annoys you? If it annoys you, it annoys thousands of others. Scratch your own itch.</p>
  <p class="section-text"><strong>5. AI workflow gaps:</strong> People using ChatGPT or Claude for work constantly need bridges — tools that connect AI outputs to real workflows. Prompt managers, output formatters, and AI-to-spreadsheet connectors are all wide open.</p>
  <div class="tip-box">
    <strong>Idea validation in 24 hours:</strong> Post your concept on Reddit, Indie Hackers, or Twitter. Create a simple landing page with Carrd ($19/year) describing the problem and solution. Add a "Join the waitlist" button. If 50+ people sign up in a week without ads, you have something. If not, move on.
  </div>
</div>

<div class="lesson-section">
  <span class="section-label">The Build Stack</span>
  <h2 class="section-title">You don't need to be a developer. You need to be a director.</h2>
  <p class="section-text">AI coding tools have made it possible for non-developers to build functional web apps. Here's the stack:</p>
  <p class="section-text"><strong>Cursor + Claude:</strong> An AI-powered code editor that writes full features from natural language descriptions. Describe what you want, Cursor builds it. You review, test, and guide.</p>
  <p class="section-text"><strong>Next.js + Supabase:</strong> The go-to combo for indie SaaS. Next.js for the frontend, Supabase for the database and authentication. Both have generous free tiers. Cursor can scaffold an entire app with this stack in hours.</p>
  <p class="section-text"><strong>Vercel:</strong> One-click deployment. Push your code to GitHub, Vercel deploys it automatically. Free for hobby projects, $20/month for production.</p>
  <p class="section-text"><strong>Stripe:</strong> Payment processing. Integration takes 30 minutes with AI assistance. Handles subscriptions, invoicing, and tax compliance. They take 2.9% + $0.30 per transaction.</p>
  <p class="section-text"><strong>Alternative no-code path:</strong> If coding feels too intimidating, use Bubble.io or Softr to build your MVP. Lower ceiling but faster launch. You can always rebuild with code once you've validated demand.</p>
  <p class="section-text">Realistic timeline: Weekend 1 — build core feature. Weekend 2 — add auth, payments, and polish. Weekend 3 — launch. Total investment: ~$50 in hosting/domains and 30-40 hours of focused work.</p>
</div>

<div class="lesson-section">
  <span class="section-label">Launch & Growth</span>
  <h2 class="section-title">First 100 users define your entire trajectory.</h2>
  <p class="section-text"><strong>Launch channels that work for micro-SaaS:</strong></p>
  <p class="section-text"><strong>Product Hunt:</strong> Free to launch. Prepare a compelling tagline, 4-5 screenshots, and a short demo video (Loom works fine). A top-10 finish can bring 500-2,000 signups in 24 hours.</p>
  <p class="section-text"><strong>Indie Hackers / Hacker News:</strong> Share your building journey. "I built a [tool] in a weekend using AI" posts consistently hit the front page. Authenticity beats polish here.</p>
  <p class="section-text"><strong>Niche communities:</strong> Go where your users already hang out. Built a tool for teachers? Post in education Facebook groups. Built one for Shopify stores? Share in Shopify subreddits. Direct, targeted, free.</p>
  <p class="section-text"><strong>SEO content:</strong> Write 5-10 blog posts targeting "[your niche] + tool/software/app." These compound over months and become your primary traffic source by month 6.</p>
  <p class="section-text"><strong>Pricing psychology:</strong> Start with three tiers. Free (limited features, drives adoption), Pro ($9-$29/month, core features), and Team ($49-$99/month, collaboration features). The free tier is your marketing engine — most micro-SaaS tools convert 3-5% of free users to paid.</p>
</div>
