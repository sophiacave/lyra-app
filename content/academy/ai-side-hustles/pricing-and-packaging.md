<div class="lesson-section">
  <span class="section-label">The Pricing Problem</span>
  <h2 class="section-title">You're not charging for your time. You're charging for the gap.</h2>
  <p class="section-text">Most beginners price based on how long something takes them. AI made you faster, so you charge less. This is backwards. You should charge based on the gap between what the client has and what they need.</p>
  <p class="section-text">A landing page that takes you 3 hours to build with AI might take a traditional developer 30 hours. The client doesn't care which one of you built it — they care that it converts visitors into customers. If that page generates $10,000/month in revenue, it's worth $3,000-$5,000 regardless of whether it took you 3 hours or 30.</p>
  <p class="section-text">The rule: never reveal how fast you are. Deliver on the timeline you promised, but don't advertise that AI made you 10x faster. The speed is your competitive advantage, not a discount coupon.</p>
</div>

<div class="lesson-section">
  <span class="section-label">Three Pricing Models</span>
  <h2 class="section-title">Pick the model that matches the engagement type.</h2>
  <p class="section-text"><strong>1. Project-Based Pricing:</strong> A fixed fee for a defined deliverable. Best for one-time projects with clear scope. "I'll build your email automation for $1,500." Advantages: simple, predictable, easy to sell. Risk: scope creep. Always define what's included and what costs extra.</p>
  <p class="section-text"><strong>2. Retainer Pricing:</strong> A monthly fee for ongoing access and deliverables. Best for content, social media, and maintenance. "For $2,000/month, I deliver 12 blog posts and manage your social calendar." Advantages: predictable income, deeper client relationships. Require a 3-month minimum commitment.</p>
  <p class="section-text"><strong>3. Value-Based Pricing:</strong> Pricing tied directly to the result you deliver. Best for high-impact projects where you can measure ROI. "I'll optimize your lead funnel. My fee is 10% of the revenue increase in the first 90 days." Advantages: highest earning potential. Risk: requires confident measurement and trust. Use this once you have case studies.</p>
  <div class="callout">
    <p><strong>When to use each:</strong> Start with project-based to build your portfolio. Move to retainers once clients trust you. Graduate to value-based once you have proven results. Most AI side hustlers should be on retainers within 3-6 months.</p>
  </div>
</div>

<div class="lesson-section">
  <span class="section-label">Package Architecture</span>
  <h2 class="section-title">Three tiers. Always three tiers. Here's why.</h2>
  <p class="section-text">Never offer a single price. Always offer three options. This isn't a gimmick — it's pricing psychology backed by decades of research. When faced with three options, most people choose the middle one. The Goldilocks effect.</p>
  <p class="section-text"><strong>Tier 1 — Starter:</strong> The minimum viable version of your service. Just enough to solve the core problem. Priced at what most competitors charge. This tier exists to make the middle tier look like a deal.</p>
  <p class="section-text"><strong>Tier 2 — Professional:</strong> Your recommended package. Includes everything in Starter plus strategic extras that dramatically increase value. Priced 60-80% higher than Starter. This is where 60-70% of clients land. Your sweet spot.</p>
  <p class="section-text"><strong>Tier 3 — Premium:</strong> The full experience. White-glove service, priority support, expanded scope. Priced 2-3x the Starter tier. Only 10-15% of clients choose this, but they're your most profitable relationships.</p>
  <div class="tip-box">
    <strong>Example for AI Content Services:</strong>
    <ul>
      <li><strong>Starter ($800/mo):</strong> 4 blog posts, basic SEO, standard delivery.</li>
      <li><strong>Professional ($1,500/mo):</strong> 8 blog posts, full SEO strategy, social repurposing, monthly analytics report. (Most pick this.)</li>
      <li><strong>Premium ($3,000/mo):</strong> 12 blog posts, full SEO, social management, email newsletter, quarterly strategy call, priority delivery.</li>
    </ul>
  </div>
</div>

<div class="lesson-section">
  <span class="section-label">Anchoring & Negotiation</span>
  <h2 class="section-title">The first number you say controls the entire conversation.</h2>
  <p class="section-text"><strong>Anchoring:</strong> Always present your Premium tier first. When a client hears "$3,000/month" first, the $1,500 Professional tier feels reasonable. If you lead with $800, even $1,500 feels expensive. Order matters.</p>
  <p class="section-text"><strong>The "too cheap" trap:</strong> Pricing too low doesn't attract more clients — it attracts worse clients. Low-price clients demand more revisions, pay late, and churn faster. Premium clients trust your expertise, respect your time, and refer their peers.</p>
  <p class="section-text"><strong>Handling price objections:</strong> Never drop your price. Instead, reduce scope. "I understand the budget is tight. We could start with the Starter package and upgrade in month two when you see the results." This maintains your rate integrity while giving the client a path forward.</p>
  <p class="section-text"><strong>The annual discount:</strong> Offer 15-20% off for annual prepayment. A $1,500/month retainer becomes $1,250/month paid annually ($15,000 upfront). You get cash flow certainty. They get a discount. Everyone wins.</p>
</div>

<div class="lesson-section">
  <span class="section-label">When to Raise Prices</span>
  <h2 class="section-title">If you haven't raised prices in 6 months, you're leaving money on the table.</h2>
  <p class="section-text">Raise prices when: you're at 80%+ capacity, your close rate is above 50% (means you're too cheap), you've added new skills or tools, or you have 5+ testimonials. Raise by 20-30% each time. Grandfather existing clients for 2-3 months, then transition them.</p>
  <p class="section-text">The market is growing. AI services were novel in 2024. In 2026, they're essential. As demand grows and you build reputation, your prices should reflect the market reality. Top AI consultants charge $200-$500/hour. That's your trajectory.</p>
</div>
