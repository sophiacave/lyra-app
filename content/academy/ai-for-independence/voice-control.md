<!-- SECTION 1: WHY VOICE FIRST -->
<div class="lesson-section">
  <span class="section-label">Why This Matters</span>
  <h2 class="section-title">When typing is pain, your voice becomes your keyboard.</h2>
  <p class="section-text">For millions of people, typing is not just inconvenient — it is physically painful. Carpal tunnel, fibromyalgia, arthritis, repetitive strain injury, chronic pain conditions — all of them make keyboards an enemy. And the world runs on keyboards.</p>
  <p class="section-text">Voice control changes that equation entirely. Instead of typing an email character by character, you say it. Instead of filling out a form field by field, you dictate the information. Instead of typing a prompt to Claude, you speak it. <strong>Your voice does the work your hands cannot.</strong></p>

<!-- SECTION 3: SETUP GUIDES -->
<div class="lesson-section">
  <span class="section-label">Set It Up</span>
  <h2 class="section-title">Turn on voice control in 2 minutes.</h2>

  <div class="tip-box">
    <div class="tip-label">iPhone / iPad</div>
    <p>Settings > Accessibility > Voice Control > turn it on. For dictation: Settings > General > Keyboard > Enable Dictation. Now tap the microphone icon on any keyboard to start dictating. Say "tap [button name]" to click things.</p>
  </div>

  <div class="tip-box">
    <div class="tip-label">Mac</div>
    <p>System Settings > Accessibility > Voice Control > turn it on. For dictation: System Settings > Keyboard > Dictation > turn on. Press the Fn key twice to start dictating in any text field. Say "click [item]" to click things on screen.</p>
  </div>

  <div class="tip-box">
    <div class="tip-label">Windows</div>
    <p>Settings > Accessibility > Speech > Voice Access > turn it on. For dictation: press Windows+H in any text field. Say "click [item]" to interact with on-screen elements.</p>
  </div>

  <div class="tip-box">
    <div class="tip-label">Android</div>
    <p>Settings > Accessibility > Voice Access > turn it on. For dictation: tap the microphone icon on the keyboard. Say "tap [item]" to interact with the screen.</p>
  </div>
</div>

<!-- SECTION 5: SPEECH DIFFERENCES -->
<div class="lesson-section">
  <span class="section-label">Real Talk</span>
  <h2 class="section-title">When dictation does not understand you.</h2>
  <p class="section-text">If you have a speech impediment, accent, or neurological condition that affects your speech, dictation can be frustrating. Here are real strategies that help:</p>

  <div class="tip-box">
    <div class="tip-label">Train the system</div>
    <p>Most dictation systems learn your voice over time. The more you use it, the better it gets. Early sessions will have more errors — stick with it for a week before judging accuracy.</p>
  </div>

  <div class="tip-box">
    <div class="tip-label">Use a good microphone</div>
    <p>Built-in laptop microphones pick up background noise. A $20 USB headset with a boom microphone dramatically improves accuracy. AirPods and earbuds also work well because the mic is close to your mouth.</p>
  </div>

  <div class="tip-box">
    <div class="tip-label">Speak in phrases, not words</div>
    <p>Dictation is more accurate with natural phrases than individual words. Instead of dictating word by word, speak in full sentences at your natural pace. The AI uses context to figure out ambiguous words.</p>
  </div>

  <div class="tip-box">
    <div class="tip-label">Let AI fix dictation errors</div>
    <p>If dictation gets words wrong, do not manually correct them. Paste the messy dictation into Claude and say: "Clean up this dictated text. Fix any obvious speech-to-text errors but keep my meaning." AI is excellent at understanding what you meant.</p>
  </div>
</div>
