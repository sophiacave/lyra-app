<!-- SECTION 1: WHAT THIS IS -->
<div class="lesson-section">
  <span class="section-label">The Truth</span>
  <h2 class="section-title">AI is not a luxury. For disabled people, it is infrastructure.</h2>
  <p class="section-text">When most people hear "AI," they think of chatbots and image generators. Fun tools. Nice to have. But for people with disabilities, AI is something fundamentally different. <strong>It is the difference between depending on others and doing things yourself.</strong></p>
  <p class="section-text">When typing causes pain, AI takes dictation. When reading dense documents is impossible with brain fog, AI summarizes. When executive dysfunction makes scheduling feel like climbing a mountain, AI manages the calendar. When filling out forms is a two-hour ordeal, AI fills them in seconds.</p>
  <p class="section-text">This is not about convenience. This is about <strong>independence.</strong> The ability to handle your own life without constantly asking for help, without burning through your limited energy on tasks that able-bodied people do without thinking.</p>

  <div class="callout">
    <p><strong>The shift:</strong> For decades, assistive technology meant expensive specialized devices. A $3,000 screen reader. A $5,000 wheelchair control system. AI changes the equation. Now, a free or low-cost AI assistant can do what used to require expensive specialized tools — and it gets better every month.</p>
  </div>
</div>

<!-- SECTION 2: ANALOGY -->
<div class="lesson-section">
  <span class="section-label">Think Of It Like This</span>
  <h2 class="section-title">AI is a prosthetic for the tasks your disability makes hard.</h2>
  <p class="section-text">A prosthetic leg does not make someone "less disabled." It gives them back mobility. AI works the same way — not for your body, but for the cognitive and physical tasks that your disability makes exhausting, painful, or impossible.</p>
  <p class="section-text">If you have chronic fatigue, AI is a prosthetic for energy-intensive tasks. If you have ADHD, AI is a prosthetic for executive function. If you have chronic pain in your hands, AI is a prosthetic for typing. If you have anxiety, AI is a prosthetic for the phone calls and emails that paralyze you.</p>
  <p class="section-text"><strong>You are not broken. You have a disability. AI is a tool that bridges the gap between what your body can do and what the world demands.</strong></p>
</div>

<!-- SECTION 4: THE SOVEREIGN APPROACH -->
<div class="lesson-section">
  <span class="section-label">The Philosophy</span>
  <h2 class="section-title">Your AI, your rules, your independence.</h2>
  <p class="section-text">This course teaches a specific approach: <strong>sovereign AI.</strong> That means AI tools and systems that <strong>you control.</strong> Not an app that might shut down. Not a service that might change its terms. Tools that you own, that work for you, that nobody can take away.</p>
  <p class="section-text">For disabled people, dependency is not just inconvenient — it is dangerous. Every system you depend on is a system that can fail you. The goal of this course is to build AI workflows so reliable that they become your infrastructure. Like electricity. Like running water. <strong>Always there when you need them.</strong></p>

  <div class="tip-box">
    <div class="tip-label">What this course covers</div>
    <p>Over 10 lessons, you will build AI systems for: voice control, forms and documents, email, scheduling, finances, medical advocacy, social media, and your complete personal AI toolkit. Each lesson gives you tools you can use the same day.</p>
  </div>
