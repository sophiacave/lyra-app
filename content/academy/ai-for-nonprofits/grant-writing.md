<div class="lesson-section">
<h2>The Grant Writing Pipeline</h2>
<p>Grant writing follows a predictable structure: research funders, write the Letter of Inquiry (LOI), receive an invitation, write the full proposal, submit, report. AI accelerates every stage except the relationship-building -- and even there, it helps you prepare.</p>
<p>The key insight: <strong>AI is not a grant writer. AI is a grant writing accelerator.</strong> It takes your program knowledge, your outcome data, and your mission language, then structures them into fundable narratives faster than you could alone.</p>
<p>A typical 10-page proposal that takes 20-30 hours drops to 8-12 hours with AI assistance. That's not magic -- it's the elimination of blank-page syndrome and structural guesswork.</p>
</div>

<div class="lesson-section">
<h2>Building Your Grant Context Document</h2>
<p>Before you write a single prompt, build a context document. This is the foundation AI draws from for every grant you write. Include:</p>
<p><strong>Organization basics:</strong> Mission statement, founding year, service area, annual budget, staff size, key programs.</p>
<p><strong>Impact data:</strong> Number served, outcomes achieved, success rates, geographic reach. Use real numbers from your most recent fiscal year.</p>
<p><strong>Voice samples:</strong> Paste 2-3 paragraphs from your best previous grant that was funded. This teaches AI your organization's tone.</p>
<p><strong>Boilerplate sections:</strong> Organizational history, leadership bios, financial sustainability statement.</p>

<div class="tip-box">
<strong>Prompt template for your context document:</strong><br>
"You are a grant writing assistant for [Organization Name]. Here is our context: [paste context document]. For all grant writing tasks, maintain our voice -- mission-driven, data-backed, community-centered. Never fabricate statistics. Flag any claims that need verification."
</div>
</div>

<div class="lesson-section">
<h2>Writing the LOI</h2>
<p>The Letter of Inquiry is your foot in the door. Most funders want 1-2 pages covering: who you are, what you want to do, how much it costs, and why it matters. AI excels here because LOIs are highly structured.</p>
<p><strong>Step 1: Research the funder.</strong> Paste the funder's guidelines, priority areas, and recent grantees into your AI tool. Ask: "Based on these funding priorities, which of our programs is the strongest fit? Explain why."</p>
<p><strong>Step 2: Generate the LOI draft.</strong> Use this prompt structure:</p>

<div class="demo-container">
<h4>LOI Prompt Template</h4>
<p>"Write a 2-page Letter of Inquiry to [Funder Name] for our [Program Name]. Use our context document above. The funder's priorities are [paste priorities]. Structure the LOI as: (1) Opening hook with a specific beneficiary story or statistic, (2) Problem statement with local data, (3) Our solution and track record, (4) Budget summary and ask amount of $[X], (5) Closing with why this partnership matters. Keep the tone confident but not arrogant."</p>
</div>

<p><strong>Step 3: Human review.</strong> Check every statistic. Verify the funder alignment. Read it aloud -- does it sound like your organization? Adjust tone where AI defaulted to generic nonprofit language.</p>
</div>

<div class="lesson-section">
<h2>The Full Proposal: Section by Section</h2>
<p>When a funder invites a full proposal, use AI to tackle each section with targeted prompts:</p>
<p><strong>Executive Summary:</strong> Write this last. Ask AI: "Summarize the following proposal sections into a compelling 1-page executive summary that leads with impact."</p>
<p><strong>Statement of Need:</strong> "Using the following local data [paste data], write a statement of need that connects community-level problems to our proposed solution. Include at least 3 cited statistics. Do not use deficit-based language about the community we serve."</p>
<p><strong>Program Design:</strong> "Describe our program model using the following components: [activities, timeline, staffing, partners]. Structure it with clear inputs, activities, outputs, and outcomes. Include a logic model narrative."</p>
<p><strong>Evaluation Plan:</strong> "Draft an evaluation plan with process measures (are we doing what we said?) and outcome measures (is it working?). Include data collection methods, timeline, and who is responsible."</p>
<p><strong>Budget Narrative:</strong> "Using this line-item budget [paste budget], write a budget narrative that justifies each expense and shows cost-effectiveness. Highlight any matching funds or in-kind contributions."</p>

<div class="callout">
<strong>The #1 AI grant writing mistake:</strong> Letting AI invent statistics. Language models will confidently fabricate data that sounds plausible. Every number in your proposal must come from your actual records or a verified source. Use AI for narrative, never for data generation.
</div>
</div>

<div class="lesson-section">
<h2>Maintaining Your Voice</h2>
<p>Generic AI output reads like generic AI output -- and grant reviewers can spot it. Three techniques to keep your authentic voice:</p>
<p><strong>1. Feed it your best work.</strong> Include 500+ words of previously funded grant narrative in your context. The more samples, the better AI mirrors your style.</p>
<p><strong>2. Use the "rewrite" pattern.</strong> Instead of generating from scratch, write a rough draft yourself (even bullet points), then ask AI: "Rewrite this into polished grant narrative while keeping the specific examples and voice."</p>
<p><strong>3. Add your stories last.</strong> AI can structure arguments, but your beneficiary stories, your staff's observations, and your community's language are what make a proposal unforgettable. Drop those in after the AI draft.</p>
<p>A funded proposal reads like a human wrote it with deep program knowledge -- because one did. AI just handled the scaffolding.</p>
</div>
