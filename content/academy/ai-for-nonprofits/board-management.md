<div class="lesson-section">
<h2>The Board-Staff Communication Gap</h2>
<p>Most executive directors spend 5-10 hours preparing for each board meeting. The board packet alone -- financial summaries, program updates, committee reports, strategic metrics -- can take an entire day to compile. Then the meeting itself often gets mired in information-sharing instead of strategic discussion.</p>
<p>AI flips this: automate the information-sharing so meetings become decision-making sessions. A well-prepared board makes better decisions, asks sharper questions, and stays engaged in governance instead of drifting into management.</p>
</div>

<div class="lesson-section">
<h2>AI-Powered Board Packets</h2>
<p>The board packet is your primary communication tool with governance. AI can assemble it from your existing data sources:</p>
<p><strong>Executive Director report:</strong> "Using these program updates [paste bullet points from each program manager], financial summary [paste key numbers], and recent wins/challenges [paste notes], write a 2-page ED report for the board. Structure: Organizational highlights (top 3), Financial snapshot (revenue, expenses, cash position), Program updates (1 paragraph each), Challenges and risks (be direct, not evasive), Recommendations for board action."</p>
<p><strong>Financial dashboard narrative:</strong> "Translate this financial statement [paste P&L or budget-to-actual] into plain-language narrative. Highlight: Are we on budget? Where are we over/under? What's our cash runway? Any concerns? Write for board members who are not financial experts."</p>

<div class="demo-container">
<h4>Committee Report Prompt</h4>
<p>"Summarize these committee meeting notes [paste raw notes] into a structured committee report. Format: Committee name, Meeting date, Members present, Key discussion points (3-5 bullets), Decisions made, Action items with owners and deadlines, Items requiring full board attention or vote."</p>
</div>

<p><strong>Consent agenda items:</strong> AI can flag which items are routine (approve minutes, accept financial report) vs. which need discussion. "Review these board packet items and recommend which should go on the consent agenda (routine, informational) vs. the discussion agenda (requires debate or vote)."</p>
</div>

<div class="lesson-section">
<h2>Meeting Agendas and Minutes</h2>
<p><strong>Agenda design:</strong> AI helps create outcome-focused agendas: "Draft a board meeting agenda for [date]. We have 90 minutes. Discussion items: [list with time estimates]. For each item, specify: the goal (inform, discuss, or decide), the lead presenter, and the expected outcome. Put the most important decision first while energy is high."</p>
<p><strong>Meeting minutes:</strong> Record the meeting (with board consent) and use Whisper for transcription. Then: "Convert this board meeting transcript into formal minutes. Format: Attendees, Call to order time, each agenda item with summary of discussion and any motions (include who moved, who seconded, vote outcome). List all action items with owners and deadlines. Note adjournment time."</p>

<div class="tip-box">
<strong>The 24-hour rule:</strong> Send draft minutes within 24 hours while memory is fresh. AI makes this possible -- what used to take 3-4 hours of careful writing now takes 30 minutes of review and editing.
</div>
</div>

<div class="lesson-section">
<h2>Strategic Planning Support</h2>
<p>AI is a powerful strategic planning assistant when fed the right data:</p>
<p><strong>SWOT analysis:</strong> "Based on these organizational documents [paste annual report, financial trends, program data, staff survey results], conduct a SWOT analysis. For each item, provide evidence from the data, not generic platitudes. Be honest about weaknesses and threats."</p>
<p><strong>Scenario planning:</strong> "Our organization faces these three possible scenarios: (1) Major funder reduces grant by 30%, (2) Demand for services increases 50%, (3) Key partnership dissolves. For each scenario, outline: immediate impact, 6-month impact, mitigation strategies, and opportunity identification."</p>
<p><strong>Competitive landscape:</strong> "Research these organizations in our service area [list similar nonprofits]. How does our programming differ? Where do we overlap? Are there underserved needs that none of us address? Frame this as a collaboration opportunity map, not a competition analysis."</p>

<div class="callout">
<strong>AI as facilitator prep:</strong> Before a strategic planning retreat, use AI to synthesize stakeholder input (surveys, interviews, community data) into a briefing document. This ensures the retreat starts with shared understanding instead of spending the first hour on context-setting.
</div>
</div>

<div class="lesson-section">
<h2>Board Recruitment and Onboarding</h2>
<p>AI helps you recruit strategically and onboard effectively:</p>
<p><strong>Board matrix analysis:</strong> "Review our current board composition [paste members with skills, demographics, connections]. Identify gaps in: financial expertise, legal expertise, fundraising connections, diversity dimensions, program area knowledge, and community representation. Prioritize the top 3 recruitment needs."</p>
<p><strong>Recruitment outreach:</strong> AI drafts personalized recruitment messages: "Write a board recruitment letter for [candidate name], a [title] at [organization]. Reference their expertise in [area] and explain why that's critical for our mission. Include: time commitment, expectations, and what they'll gain from board service. Tone: respectful, not desperate."</p>
<p><strong>Board orientation packet:</strong> "Create a board orientation guide including: organization history and mission, current strategic plan summary, financial overview, program descriptions, board roles and responsibilities, committee structure, meeting schedule, key contacts, and a glossary of organization-specific terms."</p>
</div>
