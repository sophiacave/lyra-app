<div class="lesson-section">
<h2>The Nonprofit Paradox</h2>
<p>Nonprofits operate under a brutal contradiction: the missions that matter most are chronically under-resourced. A team of three is expected to do the work of twelve. Grant reports pile up while the actual program work suffers. Donor communications fall through the cracks because everyone is stretched thin.</p>
<p>AI doesn't solve the funding gap -- but it compresses the labor gap. A single program manager with the right AI toolkit can draft grant narratives, analyze outcome data, segment donor lists, and generate social content in the time it used to take just to format a quarterly report.</p>
<p>Here's the structural advantage: nonprofits are <strong>text-heavy organizations</strong>. Grant proposals, impact reports, board memos, donor thank-you letters, policy briefs, volunteer handbooks -- these are all language tasks. And language is exactly where modern AI excels.</p>

<div class="tip-box">
<strong>The 80/20 Rule for Nonprofit AI:</strong> 80% of the value comes from three capabilities -- drafting text, summarizing documents, and analyzing spreadsheet data. Start there. Ignore everything else until those three are second nature.
</div>
</div>

<div class="lesson-section">
<h2>Three Categories That Matter</h2>
<p>Forget the hype cycle. For nonprofits, AI tools fall into three practical buckets:</p>
<p><strong>1. Writing Assistants</strong> -- Tools like Claude, ChatGPT, or locally-hosted models that draft, edit, and refine text. Use cases: grant proposals, donor emails, social media, board reports, policy memos.</p>
<p><strong>2. Data Analysis</strong> -- AI that reads your spreadsheets, finds patterns, and generates visualizations. Use cases: outcome measurement, donor segmentation, program effectiveness analysis, geographic impact mapping.</p>
<p><strong>3. Workflow Automation</strong> -- Connectors like Make (formerly Integromat) or Zapier that chain AI into your existing tools. Use cases: auto-categorizing donations, routing volunteer applications, generating personalized thank-you sequences.</p>

<div class="callout">
<strong>What about image and video AI?</strong> Useful for marketing (Lesson 6), but they're a distraction at this stage. Nail the text and data fundamentals first. A beautifully generated social graphic means nothing if your grant proposal is late.
</div>
</div>

<div class="lesson-section">
<h2>The AI Readiness Audit</h2>
<p>Before you adopt any tools, spend 30 minutes mapping your organization's workflows. Use this framework:</p>
<p><strong>Step 1: List every recurring task.</strong> Weekly donor emails, monthly board reports, quarterly grant narratives, annual impact summaries. Write them all down.</p>
<p><strong>Step 2: Tag each task.</strong> Mark each one as (a) text-heavy, (b) data-heavy, or (c) relationship-heavy. AI handles (a) and (b) well. For (c), AI assists but doesn't replace the human connection.</p>
<p><strong>Step 3: Rank by pain.</strong> Which tasks cause the most stress, take the most time, or get skipped entirely? Those are your AI priorities.</p>
<p><strong>Step 4: Check for data.</strong> Do you have existing examples? Past grant proposals, previous reports, donor records? AI works best when it can learn from your organization's existing voice and data.</p>

<div class="demo-container">
<h4>Example: Small Environmental Nonprofit</h4>
<p><strong>Top pain points identified:</strong></p>
<ul>
<li>Grant LOIs take 6 hours each (text-heavy) -- AI priority #1</li>
<li>Monthly donor newsletter takes 4 hours (text-heavy) -- AI priority #2</li>
<li>Quarterly outcome data compilation takes 8 hours (data-heavy) -- AI priority #3</li>
<li>Board meeting prep takes 3 hours (mixed) -- AI priority #4</li>
</ul>
<p><strong>Estimated time saved:</strong> 15+ hours/month with AI handling first drafts</p>

<div class="lesson-section">
<h2>Ethical Guardrails from Day One</h2>
<p>Nonprofits carry a higher ethical bar than for-profit companies. Your donors, beneficiaries, and community trust you. That trust requires guardrails:</p>
<p><strong>Never feed beneficiary PII into cloud AI.</strong> Names, addresses, health data, immigration status -- none of it goes into ChatGPT or Claude's cloud API. Use anonymized or synthetic data for analysis. For sensitive work, use local AI models (covered in our Local AI & Privacy course).</p>
<p><strong>Always disclose AI use in grants.</strong> More funders are asking. Even when they don't ask, transparency builds trust. A simple line: "This proposal was drafted with AI assistance and reviewed by [staff name]."</p>
<p><strong>Human review is non-negotiable.</strong> AI generates the first draft. A human with program knowledge reviews, edits, and approves. Every time. No exceptions.</p>
<p><strong>Document your AI policy.</strong> Even a one-page document covering what tools you use, what data goes into them, and who reviews AI output. Your board will thank you.</p>
</div>

<div class="lesson-section">
<h2>Your First Week with AI</h2>
<p>Here is a concrete 5-day plan to start using AI at your nonprofit:</p>
<p><strong>Day 1:</strong> Complete the AI Readiness Audit above. Identify your top 3 pain points.</p>
<p><strong>Day 2:</strong> Sign up for a free AI tool (Claude.ai free tier or ChatGPT free). Paste in a past grant proposal and ask: "Analyze this proposal's strengths and weaknesses."</p>
<p><strong>Day 3:</strong> Draft a real donor thank-you email using AI. Give it context: the donor's giving history, your program impact, and your organization's voice. Compare the AI draft to what you'd normally write.</p>
<p><strong>Day 4:</strong> Upload a spreadsheet of program data (anonymized). Ask AI to identify trends and generate a summary paragraph suitable for a board report.</p>
<p><strong>Day 5:</strong> Write your one-page AI policy. Share it with your team. You're now ahead of 90% of nonprofits.</p>
