<div class="lesson-section">
<h2>Advocacy Is Not Lobbying</h2>
<p>Before diving into tactics, clarity on the law: 501(c)(3) organizations can and should advocate. Education, awareness, and public policy engagement are not only legal -- they're essential to most missions. What's limited is <strong>lobbying</strong> -- directly asking legislators to vote a specific way on specific legislation.</p>
<p>The IRS distinguishes between:</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Direct lobbying:</strong> Communicating with a legislator about specific legislation and expressing a view. Limited but allowed under the 501(h) election (up to 20% of the first $500K of exempt purpose expenditures).</li>
<li><strong>Grassroots lobbying:</strong> Asking the public to contact legislators about specific legislation. More strictly limited (25% of your lobbying limit).</li>
<li><strong>Advocacy/education:</strong> Educating the public or policymakers about issues without referencing specific legislation. Unlimited.</li>
</ul>
<p>AI helps you stay on the right side of this line by reviewing your materials: "Review this advocacy communication. Does it constitute direct lobbying, grassroots lobbying, or issue education under IRS guidelines for 501(c)(3) organizations? Flag any language that crosses into lobbying territory."</p>
</div>

<div class="lesson-section">
<h2>Policy Research with AI</h2>
<p>Understanding the policy landscape is the foundation of effective advocacy. AI accelerates research that used to require a policy analyst:</p>
<p><strong>Legislative summary:</strong> "Summarize [Bill Name/Number] in plain language. Include: what it would change, who it affects, current status, key supporters and opponents, and how it relates to [your issue area]. Note any amendments since introduction."</p>
<p><strong>Comparative policy analysis:</strong> "Compare how [3 states/cities] have addressed [policy issue]. For each, summarize the legislation, implementation outcomes, and lessons learned. Recommend which model would best fit [your state/city] and why."</p>
<p><strong>Stakeholder mapping:</strong> "For [policy issue] in [jurisdiction], identify the key stakeholders: elected officials with jurisdiction, relevant committee chairs, allied organizations, opposition groups, and media outlets covering the issue. For each elected official, note their stated position or relevant voting history."</p>

<div class="tip-box">
<strong>Important limitation:</strong> AI's training data has a cutoff date. Always verify legislative status, vote counts, and official positions through official sources like congress.gov, your state legislature's website, or LegiScan. AI gives you the framework; you verify the facts.
</tip>
</div>

<div class="lesson-section">
<h2>Advocacy Materials at Speed</h2>
<p>When a policy window opens, speed matters. AI lets you produce professional advocacy materials in hours instead of weeks:</p>
<p><strong>Fact sheets:</strong> "Create a one-page fact sheet on [issue]. Structure: headline statistic, 3 key facts with sources, how this affects [target community], what the solution looks like, and a call to action. Design for a legislator's aide who has 60 seconds to read it."</p>
<p><strong>Talking points:</strong> "Generate talking points for a meeting with [legislator name] about [issue]. Include: 3 key messages (each with a supporting statistic and a local story hook), anticipated pushback and responses, a specific ask, and a leave-behind summary."</p>
<p><strong>Public testimony:</strong> "Draft 3-minute public testimony for [hearing/council meeting] on [issue]. Open with a personal or organizational connection. Present 3 evidence-based arguments. Close with a clear request. Time the reading to stay under 3 minutes."</p>

<div class="demo-container">
<h4>Op-Ed Prompt</h4>
<p>"Write a 700-word op-ed for [local newspaper] about [issue]. Author: [ED name and title]. Structure: news hook that connects to a current event, the problem with local data, our organization's perspective based on frontline experience, the solution we're advocating for, and a closing call for community action. Tone: passionate but evidence-based. No jargon."</p>
</div>
</div>

<div class="lesson-section">
<h2>Supporter Mobilization</h2>
<p>Advocacy campaigns need people. AI helps you mobilize your base effectively:</p>
<p><strong>Action alerts:</strong> When time-sensitive policy action is needed, AI drafts urgent communications: "Write an action alert email about [bill/vote/hearing]. Include: what's happening and when, why it matters to our community, exactly what action to take (call this number, attend this hearing, sign this petition), and a sample script for calling. Keep under 200 words. Subject line should convey urgency."</p>
<p><strong>Petition language:</strong> "Draft petition language for [issue]. Include a headline that captures the ask in 10 words, 150-word description of the problem and solution, specific signatories (who should sign), and the target (who receives the petition)."</p>
<p><strong>Social media campaign:</strong> "Create a 7-day social media campaign for [advocacy issue]. Include daily posts with: (1) awareness-building content (days 1-3), (2) personal story sharing prompts (days 4-5), (3) direct action asks (days 6-7). Include hashtag suggestions and shareable statistics formatted as quote graphics."</p>

<div class="callout">
<strong>Coalition building:</strong> AI can draft outreach to potential coalition partners: "Write an email to [organization name] proposing a coalition partnership on [issue]. Reference our shared interests, what we bring to the table, what we're asking of them, and next steps. Tone: collaborative, not transactional."
</div>
</div>

<div class="lesson-section">
<h2>Measuring Advocacy Impact</h2>
<p>Advocacy impact is harder to measure than program outcomes, but it's not impossible:</p>
<p><strong>Process metrics:</strong> Letters sent, calls made, meetings held, media placements, petition signatures, event attendance. Track these to show mobilization capacity.</p>
<p><strong>Influence metrics:</strong> Legislator positions changed, bills amended with your language, invitations to testify, media quotes and citations, coalition growth.</p>
<p><strong>Outcome metrics:</strong> Policy changes achieved, regulations modified, funding appropriated, executive actions taken.</p>
<p>AI helps track and report: "Using our advocacy activity log [paste data], generate a quarterly advocacy impact report. Show: total supporter actions taken, media coverage summary, legislative meetings and outcomes, and progress toward policy goals. Highlight the top 3 wins and the top challenge."</p>
</div>
