  <h1>Thought Leadership & <span class="accent">Writing.</span></h1>
  <p class="sub">Stop sharing other people's ideas. Start being the source.</p>
</div>

<div class="learn-card">
  <h3>After this lesson you'll know</h3>
  <ul><li>What thought leadership actually means (and what it doesn't)</li><li>How to develop original ideas worth sharing</li><li>The AI-powered writing system for long-form content</li><li>Where to publish for maximum authority and reach</li></ul>
</div>

<div class="lesson-section">
  <span class="section-label">What It Means</span>
  <h2 class="section-title">Thought leaders don't repeat. They originate.</h2>
  <div class="section-text">
    <p>Thought leadership isn't posting motivational quotes or summarizing articles you read. It's having a distinct point of view on your industry and backing it up with original thinking.</p>
    <p>The bar is lower than you think. You don't need groundbreaking research. You need a perspective shaped by real experience -- patterns you've noticed, mistakes you've made, counterintuitive lessons you've learned.</p>
    <div class="callout">
      <strong>The thought leadership formula:</strong> Observation + Experience + Contrarian Angle = Original Thought. "Everyone says X, but in my experience Y, because Z." That's it. That's the whole formula. AI can help you structure and articulate it, but the raw insight has to come from you.
    </div>
    <p>The person who writes "5 AI tools you should try" is a content creator. The person who writes "Why most AI tools are solving the wrong problem" is a thought leader. Same topic, different level.</p>
  </div>
</div>

<div class="lesson-section">
  <span class="section-label">Idea Engine</span>
  <h2 class="section-title">Never run out of things to say.</h2>
  <div class="section-text">
    <p>Writer's block isn't a creativity problem. It's an input problem. If you're not consuming interesting things, you won't produce interesting things. Here's the system:</p>
    <p><strong>The Input Diet:</strong> Read 2-3 sources outside your industry every week. The best ideas come from cross-pollination. A UX designer reading about behavioral economics. A marketer reading about urban planning. Novel connections create original insights.</p>
    <p><strong>The Idea Capture:</strong> Keep a running note called "Things I Believe." Every time you have a strong reaction to something -- agreement, disagreement, surprise -- write down your take in one sentence. This is your content goldmine.</p>
    <div class="tip-box">
      <strong>AI idea generator:</strong> "Here are 10 things I believe about [your industry]: [list]. Turn each belief into a potential article title with a contrarian angle. For each title, give me a one-sentence thesis and three supporting points I could make."
    </div>
    <p>You'll never stare at a blank page again. The hard part isn't finding ideas -- it's choosing which one to write first.</p>
  </div>
</div>

<div class="lesson-section">
  <span class="section-label">Writing System</span>
  <h2 class="section-title">Write a 1,500-word article in two hours.</h2>
  <div class="section-text">
    <p>Long-form writing builds authority like nothing else. A well-written article positions you as the expert. Here's the AI-assisted workflow:</p>
    <p><strong>Phase 1 -- Outline (20 min):</strong> Give AI your thesis and ask for a structured outline. Review it, rearrange sections, add your own points. The outline is the blueprint -- get it right.</p>
    <p><strong>Phase 2 -- First draft (40 min):</strong> Use AI to expand each outline section into paragraphs. Feed it your voice notes or rough thoughts for each section so it captures your perspective, not generic advice.</p>
    <p><strong>Phase 3 -- Rewrite (45 min):</strong> This is where the magic happens. Read every sentence. Kill the generic ones. Add your specific stories, data, and opinions. If a paragraph could've been written by anyone, rewrite it until it could only come from you.</p>
    <p><strong>Phase 4 -- Polish (15 min):</strong> Ask AI to tighten the prose, fix transitions, and suggest a stronger opening and closing. Final read-through. Publish.</p>
    <div class="demo-container">
      <p><em>"I'm writing an article with this thesis: [your thesis]. Here's my rough outline: [outline]. Expand each section into 2-3 paragraphs. Use a direct, conversational tone. Include specific examples where possible. Flag spots where I should add a personal story or data point."</em></p>
    </div>
  </div>
</div>

<div class="lesson-section">
  <span class="section-label">Publishing Strategy</span>
  <h2 class="section-title">Where you publish matters as much as what you write.</h2>
  <div class="section-text">
    <p>Writing a great article is half the battle. Putting it where the right people will see it is the other half.</p>
    <div class="callout">
      <strong>Your own platform first:</strong> Always publish on your blog or newsletter first. This builds your domain authority and keeps the content on your territory. After 1-2 weeks, republish elsewhere.
    </div>
    <div class="callout">
      <strong>Syndication targets:</strong> Medium (built-in audience, great for discovery). LinkedIn Articles (reaches your professional network). Industry publications and blogs (highest authority, hardest to get into). Dev.to, Hashnode, or niche platforms for technical content.
    </div>
    <div class="callout">
      <strong>Repurpose everything:</strong> One long-form article becomes 5 LinkedIn posts, 10 tweets, a newsletter issue, a podcast talking point, and a conference talk section. AI handles the reformatting. You write once, distribute ten times.
    </div>
    <div class="tip-box">
      <strong>The canonical URL trick:</strong> When republishing on Medium or LinkedIn, set the canonical URL to your original blog post. This tells search engines your site is the source, protecting your SEO while getting distribution on other platforms.
    </div>
  </div>
</div>
