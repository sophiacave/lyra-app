<div class="lesson-section">
  <span class="section-label">Revenue Streams</span>
  <h2 class="section-title">Five ways your brand makes money.</h2>
  <div class="section-text">
    <p>A personal brand without monetization is a hobby. Nothing wrong with hobbies -- but you're here to build something that pays. Here are the five revenue streams, ranked by effort to launch:</p>
    <div class="callout">
      <strong>1. Freelance & Consulting:</strong> The fastest path to revenue. Your brand attracts clients who already trust you. Inbound consulting leads close 3-5x faster than cold outreach because your content pre-sold them on your expertise.
    </div>
    <div class="callout">
      <strong>2. Digital Products:</strong> Courses, templates, guides, prompts, toolkits. Create once, sell forever. AI lets you build these in days, not months. A $29 template that sells 100 copies is $2,900 while you sleep.
    </div>
    <div class="callout">
      <strong>3. Paid Community:</strong> A Discord, Slack, or Circle community where members pay monthly for access to you and each other. Recurring revenue with compounding value. Start at $19-49/month.
    </div>
    <div class="callout">
      <strong>4. Speaking & Workshops:</strong> Paid talks, corporate workshops, bootcamps. Rates range from $500 for local events to $10,000+ for keynotes. Your content is your audition tape.
    </div>
    <div class="callout">
      <strong>5. Sponsorships & Partnerships:</strong> Once your audience is large enough, brands pay to reach them. Newsletter sponsors, social media partnerships, affiliate deals. Typically kicks in around 5,000+ engaged followers.
    </div>
  </div>
</div>

<div class="lesson-section">
  <span class="section-label">Pricing</span>
  <h2 class="section-title">Stop undercharging. Here's how to price right.</h2>
  <div class="section-text">
    <p>Most people price based on what they'd pay, not what the market will pay. That's how you end up charging $50/hour when you should be charging $250.</p>
    <p><strong>Value-based pricing:</strong> Don't charge for your time. Charge for the outcome. If your consulting saves a company $100,000/year, charging $10,000 for that engagement is a bargain. Frame your price around the value delivered, not the hours spent.</p>
    <div class="tip-box">
      <strong>The pricing ladder:</strong> Free content builds trust. A $29 digital product proves they'll pay. A $299 course proves they'll invest. A $2,999 consulting package proves they'll commit. Each rung validates the next. Never skip rungs -- build the ladder.
    </div>
    <p><strong>AI for pricing research:</strong> "What do [your niche] consultants typically charge for [service]? Give me a range from entry-level to premium, and explain what justifies the premium pricing." Use this as a baseline, then position yourself based on your unique value.</p>
  </div>
</div>

<div class="lesson-section">
  <span class="section-label">Digital Products</span>
  <h2 class="section-title">Build a product this week with AI.</h2>
  <div class="section-text">
    <p>Your first digital product doesn't need to be a 40-hour course. Start with something small that solves a specific problem:</p>
    <p><strong>Templates and frameworks:</strong> Notion templates, spreadsheet frameworks, prompt libraries, checklists. AI helps you create them. Canva helps you make them look professional. Gumroad or Lemonsqueezy handles the selling. Total build time: 1-2 days.</p>
    <p><strong>Mini-courses:</strong> A 5-lesson email course or a 60-minute video workshop. Not a massive production. Just you sharing exactly how you do something, step by step. Price: $29-99.</p>
    <div class="demo-container">
      <p><em>"I'm an expert in [your niche]. My audience struggles with [specific problem]. Create an outline for a digital product that solves this. Include: product name, description, what's included (templates, guides, videos), pricing suggestion, and a landing page headline."</em></p>
    </div>
    <p><strong>Validation before building:</strong> Before you spend a week building something, test demand. Post about the idea on social media. Create a waitlist landing page. If 50 people sign up, build it. If 5 people sign up, try a different angle. AI makes pivoting cheap.</p>
  </div>
</div>

<div class="lesson-section">
  <span class="section-label">Launch Playbook</span>
  <h2 class="section-title">The 7-day launch that actually works.</h2>
  <div class="section-text">
    <p>You don't need a 6-week launch campaign. Here's the 7-day playbook for your first product:</p>
    <p><strong>Day 1-2:</strong> Build the product. Use AI to draft content, Canva for design, your platform of choice for delivery.</p>
    <p><strong>Day 3:</strong> Create the sales page. One clear headline, three bullet points on what they get, social proof if you have it, a price, and a buy button. That's it.</p>
    <p><strong>Day 4:</strong> Soft launch to your email list. "I made this for you. Here's why." Your warmest audience buys first and gives you testimonials.</p>
    <p><strong>Day 5-6:</strong> Social media launch. Share the story behind why you built it. Show behind-the-scenes. Post testimonials from early buyers.</p>
    <p><strong>Day 7:</strong> Final push. "Last chance" or "early pricing ends tonight." Urgency works when it's honest.</p>
    <div class="tip-box">
      <strong>Your first launch will feel small.</strong> Maybe you sell 10 copies. That's a win. You now have proof of concept, testimonials, and a product that sells passively forever. The second launch will be bigger. The tenth will be transformative.
    </div>
  </div>
