<div class="lesson-section">
  <span class="section-label">Strategy First</span>
  <h2 class="section-title">Random posting is random results.</h2>
  <div class="section-text">
    <p>Most people treat content creation like throwing spaghetti at a wall. Monday they post a selfie, Tuesday a hot take, Wednesday nothing, Thursday a meme. No pattern. No strategy. No growth.</p>
    <p>A content strategy answers three questions: What topics reinforce your expertise? Who specifically are you creating for? What action do you want people to take after consuming your content?</p>
    <div class="callout">
      <strong>The brand-building formula:</strong> Every piece of content should do at least one of these: demonstrate expertise, share a unique perspective, tell a relatable story, or provide actionable value. If it doesn't do any of these, don't publish it.
    </div>
  </div>
</div>

<div class="lesson-section">
  <span class="section-label">Content Pillars</span>
  <h2 class="section-title">Three pillars, infinite content.</h2>
  <div class="section-text">
    <p>Content pillars are the 3-5 core topics you consistently create around. They prevent you from drifting off-brand and give your audience a reason to follow you.</p>
    <p>Example for an AI engineer: Pillar 1 = AI tutorials and how-tos. Pillar 2 = Industry insights and predictions. Pillar 3 = Behind-the-scenes of building projects. Pillar 4 = Career advice for tech professionals.</p>
    <div class="demo-container">
      <p><em>"I'm a [role] who helps [audience] with [problem]. Generate 5 content pillars for my personal brand. For each pillar, give me 10 specific post ideas that demonstrate expertise and provide value. Mix formats: how-to posts, opinion pieces, case studies, quick tips, and personal stories."</em></p>
    </div>
    <p>That prompt gives you 50 post ideas. At 3 posts per week, that's over 4 months of content planned in one AI session.</p>
  </div>
</div>

<div class="lesson-section">
  <span class="section-label">The Calendar</span>
  <h2 class="section-title">Build your content machine in 30 minutes.</h2>
  <div class="section-text">
    <p>A content calendar removes daily decision fatigue. You never stare at a blank screen wondering "what should I post today?" because it's already planned.</p>
    <p><strong>Weekly rhythm example:</strong></p>
    <p>Monday: Educational post (how-to, tutorial, tip). Wednesday: Opinion or insight (industry take, prediction). Friday: Personal story or behind-the-scenes.</p>
    <div class="tip-box">
      <strong>Batch creation:</strong> Don't create content daily. Set aside 2 hours on Sunday. Use AI to draft all your posts for the week. Edit them with your voice. Schedule them. Done. The rest of the week, you just engage with comments.
    </div>
    <p>Tools for scheduling: Buffer (free tier), Later, or native platform scheduling on LinkedIn and Twitter. Pick one and use it consistently.</p>
  </div>
</div>

<div class="lesson-section">
  <span class="section-label">Platform Strategy</span>
  <h2 class="section-title">Be everywhere by repurposing, not recreating.</h2>
  <div class="section-text">
    <p>You don't need unique content for every platform. Create once, repurpose everywhere.</p>
    <p><strong>The repurposing chain:</strong> Write one long-form article or LinkedIn post. Pull 3-5 key points as individual tweets. Turn the main insight into a short video script. Extract a quote graphic for Instagram. Use the article as a newsletter issue.</p>
    <div class="callout">
      <strong>One piece of content becomes five.</strong> This is how solo creators appear to be everywhere without burning out. The content is the same core idea, adapted for each platform's format.
    </div>
    <p>Posting frequency that works: LinkedIn 3-5x/week, Twitter 1-3x/day, Newsletter 1x/week, Blog 1-2x/month. Start with one platform, master it, then expand.</p>
    <div class="tip-box">
      <strong>AI repurposing prompt:</strong> "Take this LinkedIn post and create: 3 tweets, 1 newsletter intro paragraph, and 1 Instagram caption. Maintain my brand voice. Each version should feel native to its platform, not copy-pasted."
    </div>
  </div>
</div>
