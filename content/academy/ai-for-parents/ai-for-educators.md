<div class="lesson-section">
  <span class="section-label">The Teacher's Reality</span>
  <h2 class="section-title">Teachers are drowning — AI can help.</h2>
  <p>Before we talk about AI in the classroom, let's talk about what teachers actually deal with: lesson planning, grading, differentiation for 30+ students, IEP compliance, parent communication, administrative paperwork, and actually teaching. Most teachers work 50+ hours a week and still feel behind.</p>
  <p>AI isn't going to replace teachers. Nothing replaces a human who cares about your child, who reads the room, who knows that Marcus needs encouragement and Elena needs challenge. But AI can take hours of mechanical work off their plate so they can focus on what only humans can do.</p>
  <div class="callout">
    <strong>If you're a teacher reading this:</strong> You are not going to be replaced by AI. You are going to be enhanced by it. The teachers who learn AI will outperform those who don't — not because AI teaches better, but because it frees you to teach more.
  </div>
</div>

<div class="lesson-section">
  <span class="section-label">Time Savers</span>
  <h2 class="section-title">Where AI saves teachers the most time.</h2>
  <p><strong>Lesson planning:</strong> AI can generate lesson plan drafts, create age-appropriate activities, and suggest differentiation strategies in minutes. A teacher still reviews and customizes — but starting from a draft instead of a blank page saves hours every week.</p>
  <p><strong>Grading and feedback:</strong> AI can provide initial feedback on student writing, flag common errors, and generate rubric-aligned comments. The teacher reviews, adjusts, and adds personal touches. This cuts grading time by 40-60% for many educators.</p>
  <p><strong>Differentiation:</strong> AI can take a single lesson and adapt it for different reading levels, learning styles, or language needs. Creating three versions of a worksheet used to take an hour. Now it takes five minutes.</p>
  <p><strong>Communication:</strong> Parent emails, progress reports, recommendation letters — AI can draft all of these. The teacher personalizes and sends. The tone stays human; the time investment drops dramatically.</p>
  <div class="tip-box">
    <strong>Tools to know:</strong> Khanmigo (teacher mode), MagicSchool AI, Diffit, Brisk Teaching, and Curipod are all built specifically for educators with classroom-appropriate guardrails.
  </div>
</div>

<div class="lesson-section">
  <span class="section-label">In the Classroom</span>
  <h2 class="section-title">AI as a teaching tool — not a teaching replacement.</h2>
  <p>The most effective teachers using AI aren't having AI teach for them. They're using AI to create better learning experiences:</p>
  <ul>
    <li><strong>Personalized tutoring:</strong> AI can work with individual students who need extra help while the teacher focuses on small groups. It's like having a teaching assistant who never gets tired.</li>
    <li><strong>Instant formative assessment:</strong> AI-powered quizzes give teachers real-time data on who understands what. No more waiting until the test to find out half the class is lost.</li>
    <li><strong>Debate and discussion:</strong> AI can present opposing viewpoints for students to argue against, creating richer discussions without the teacher having to play devil's advocate on every topic.</li>
    <li><strong>Accessibility:</strong> Real-time translation, text-to-speech, and content adaptation make the same lesson accessible to every student in the room.</li>
  </ul>
</div>

<div class="lesson-section">
  <span class="section-label">Supporting Teachers</span>
  <h2 class="section-title">What parents can do to help.</h2>
  <p>Teachers are navigating AI with very little training or support. Here's how parents can help instead of adding pressure:</p>
  <ul>
    <li><strong>Be patient.</strong> Teachers are figuring this out in real time, often without professional development funding.</li>
    <li><strong>Share resources.</strong> If you find a good article or tool, send it to your child's teacher. They'll appreciate it.</li>
    <li><strong>Ask questions respectfully.</strong> "How are you thinking about AI in your classroom?" opens conversation. "Why aren't you using AI yet?" creates defensiveness.</li>
    <li><strong>Advocate for training.</strong> Push your school board to fund AI professional development. Teachers can't use what they don't understand.</li>
  </ul>
</div>
