<div class="lesson-section">
  <span class="section-label">The Landscape</span>
  <h2 class="section-title">Schools are all over the map — literally.</h2>
  <p>Right now, school AI policies range from total bans to full embrace, and everything in between. Most schools fall into one of four categories:</p>
  <ul>
    <li><strong>Ban it all:</strong> No AI use allowed, period. AI detection tools required. (This is increasingly rare because it's unenforceable.)</li>
    <li><strong>Ignore it:</strong> No official policy. Teachers figure it out individually. (This is the most common and the most confusing for families.)</li>
    <li><strong>Guided use:</strong> AI allowed with specific guidelines per assignment. Teachers indicate when AI is and isn't appropriate. (This is the gold standard.)</li>
    <li><strong>Full integration:</strong> AI tools provided by the school, embedded in curriculum. (Emerging, mostly in well-funded districts.)</li>
  </ul>
  <div class="callout">
    <strong>Reality check:</strong> AI detection tools (Turnitin, GPTZero, etc.) are unreliable. They produce false positives, disproportionately flag non-native English speakers, and can be easily fooled. If your school relies solely on detection, that's a problem worth raising.
  </div>
</div>

<div class="lesson-section">
  <span class="section-label">Academic Integrity</span>
  <h2 class="section-title">The definition of cheating just got complicated.</h2>
  <p>Here's the honest truth: schools haven't figured this out yet. What counts as "using AI appropriately" varies by teacher, by assignment, and by school. Your child is navigating genuinely unclear territory.</p>
  <p><strong>The safe framework for your kid:</strong></p>
  <ol>
    <li><strong>When in doubt, ask.</strong> Before using AI on any assignment, check with the teacher. "Can I use AI for this? How?" A 30-second question prevents a potential integrity violation.</li>
    <li><strong>Disclose use.</strong> If your child used AI in any way, they should mention it. "I used ChatGPT to help me understand the concept, then wrote the essay myself" is always safe.</li>
    <li><strong>Keep records.</strong> Save chat logs of AI interactions for important assignments. If questioned, your child can show exactly how they used it.</li>
  </ol>
  <div class="tip-box">
    <strong>Teach this phrase:</strong> "I used AI to help me learn this, but the work is mine." That sentence is almost always acceptable and shows intellectual honesty.
  </div>
</div>

<div class="lesson-section">
  <span class="section-label">When There's No Policy</span>
  <h2 class="section-title">Navigating the gray zone.</h2>
  <p>If your school doesn't have an AI policy — and many don't — here's how to protect your child while still letting them benefit from AI:</p>
  <p><strong>Email the teacher.</strong> A simple message: "Hi, I want to make sure [child's name] is using AI tools appropriately. What are your guidelines for AI use on homework and projects?" This does two things: it protects your child legally, and it nudges the teacher to think about it.</p>
  <p><strong>Default to conservative.</strong> Until you hear otherwise, treat AI like a reference book. You can look things up, you can learn from it, but the work you turn in is your own.</p>
  <p><strong>Document everything.</strong> If your child uses AI for a major project, keep the chat logs. You want a paper trail that shows learning, not copying.</p>
</div>

<div class="lesson-section">
  <span class="section-label">Be Part of the Solution</span>
  <h2 class="section-title">How to push your school forward.</h2>
  <p>Schools need parent input on AI policy. Here's how to contribute constructively:</p>
  <ul>
    <li><strong>Join the PTA conversation.</strong> Bring AI policy up at meetings. Come with information, not fear.</li>
    <li><strong>Share this course.</strong> Seriously. Many teachers and administrators don't know where to start.</li>
    <li><strong>Advocate for guided use.</strong> Push for clear per-assignment guidelines rather than blanket bans or total silence.</li>
    <li><strong>Ask about AI literacy.</strong> Should the school be teaching students how to use AI effectively and ethically? (Yes. The answer is yes.)</li>
  </ul>
</div>
