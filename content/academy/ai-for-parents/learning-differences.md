<div class="lesson-section">
  <span class="section-label">The Opportunity</span>
  <h2 class="section-title">AI might be the best thing that ever happened to your kid.</h2>
  <p>If your child has a learning difference, you already know the frustration of watching them struggle in systems that weren't built for them. You've sat through IEP meetings. You've fought for accommodations. You've watched your brilliant kid feel stupid because the delivery method didn't match their brain.</p>
  <p>AI changes this equation fundamentally. It can adapt to your child's pace, present information in multiple formats, provide infinite patience, and meet them exactly where they are. It doesn't get tired. It doesn't judge. And it can be customized in ways no classroom with 30 students ever could.</p>
  <div class="callout">
    <strong>Reframe this:</strong> AI accommodations aren't crutches. They're ramps. A wheelchair user isn't "cheating" by using a ramp. Your child isn't cheating by using tools that match how their brain works.
  </div>
</div>

<div class="lesson-section">
  <span class="section-label">By Difference</span>
  <h2 class="section-title">Specific tools for specific needs.</h2>
  <p><strong>Dyslexia & reading differences:</strong> Text-to-speech AI (Natural Reader, Speechify) reads content aloud. AI can rewrite complex passages at simpler reading levels. Tools like Ello provide AI-powered reading companions that listen to your child read and help with tricky words in real time.</p>
  <p><strong>ADHD & executive function:</strong> AI can break large projects into small, timed steps. Tools like Goblin Tools use AI to chunk tasks and reduce overwhelm. AI chatbots can serve as body-doubling companions — just having "someone" there while working helps many ADHD brains focus.</p>
  <p><strong>Autism spectrum:</strong> AI can provide predictable, patient social practice. It can explain idioms, sarcasm, and social cues when asked. Visual scheduling tools powered by AI can help with transitions and routine changes.</p>
  <p><strong>Dyscalculia:</strong> AI can explain math concepts visually and verbally, try multiple explanation styles until one clicks, and generate practice problems at exactly the right difficulty level.</p>
  <div class="tip-box">
    <strong>Important:</strong> These tools supplement professional support — they don't replace therapists, special educators, or medical providers. Think of AI as one powerful tool in a full toolkit.
  </div>
</div>

<div class="lesson-section">
  <span class="section-label">Custom Prompts</span>
  <h2 class="section-title">Set up AI to match your child's brain.</h2>
  <p>One of AI's superpowers is custom instructions. Most AI tools let you set a system prompt that shapes every interaction. Here's how to use this for your child:</p>
  <p><strong>Example custom instruction:</strong> "I'm helping a 10-year-old with dyslexia. Please use short sentences, simple words, and break everything into numbered steps. Avoid walls of text. Use examples before definitions. Be encouraging when they get things wrong."</p>
  <p>You can get even more specific: "My child processes information better with analogies and real-world examples. Start every explanation with a real-world comparison before getting technical."</p>
  <p>This kind of personalization would be impossible in a classroom of 30. With AI, it takes 60 seconds to set up and applies to every single interaction.</p>
</div>

<div class="lesson-section">
  <span class="section-label">Advocacy</span>
  <h2 class="section-title">Getting AI into your child's IEP or 504.</h2>
  <p>If your child has an IEP or 504 plan, AI tools can potentially be written in as formal accommodations. Here's how to approach it:</p>
  <ul>
    <li><strong>Document the impact.</strong> Show how AI tools help your child access the curriculum. Before/after examples are powerful.</li>
    <li><strong>Frame it as assistive technology.</strong> Schools already accommodate text-to-speech, voice-to-text, and calculators. AI is the next evolution of these same tools.</li>
    <li><strong>Be specific.</strong> Don't ask for "AI access." Ask for "text-to-speech AI for reading assignments" or "AI-powered task breakdown for multi-step projects."</li>
    <li><strong>Know your rights.</strong> Under IDEA and Section 504, schools must provide assistive technology if it's needed for FAPE (Free Appropriate Public Education).</li>
  </ul>
</div>
