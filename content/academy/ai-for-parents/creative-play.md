<div class="lesson-section">
  <span class="section-label">The Mindset</span>
  <h2 class="section-title">AI is a creative partner, not a replacement.</h2>
  <p>There's a fear that AI will make kids lazy creatively — that they'll just tell AI to make art and never pick up a pencil. But that's not what actually happens when you set it up right. Kids who use AI creatively tend to get more creative, not less. Why? Because AI removes the "I can't" barrier.</p>
  <p>A kid who says "I can't draw" can now describe what they imagine and see it come to life. That's not laziness — that's access. And once they see their vision realized, many kids get inspired to learn the underlying skills. AI becomes the gateway, not the destination.</p>
  <div class="tip-box">
    <strong>The key distinction:</strong> "Make me a picture of a dragon" is consumption. "I'm designing a dragon character for my story — it has crystal scales and lives underwater, help me visualize it" is creative direction. Teach your kids to be directors, not consumers.
  </div>
</div>

<div class="lesson-section">
  <span class="section-label">Visual Art</span>
  <h2 class="section-title">AI art tools for kids.</h2>
  <p><strong>Getting started:</strong> Tools like Canva (with AI image generation), DALL-E through ChatGPT, and Google's Gemini can generate images from text descriptions. For younger kids, Google's AutoDraw turns rough sketches into polished drawings.</p>
  <p><strong>Creative activities:</strong></p>
  <ul>
    <li><strong>Character design:</strong> Have your child describe a character in detail — then iterate. "Make the eyes bigger. Add armor. Change the color to purple." They're learning art direction.</li>
    <li><strong>Comic creation:</strong> Generate panels for a story your child writes. They do the storytelling; AI handles the illustration.</li>
    <li><strong>Style exploration:</strong> Take the same subject and generate it in different art styles — watercolor, pixel art, Art Deco, anime. This teaches art history through play.</li>
  </ul>
</div>

<div class="lesson-section">
  <span class="section-label">Music & Stories</span>
  <h2 class="section-title">Sound and narrative adventures.</h2>
  <p><strong>Music:</strong> Tools like Suno and Chrome Music Lab let kids create songs from descriptions. Your child can say "make a happy song about my dog with ukulele and drums" and hear it in seconds. It's not about replacing music lessons — it's about sparking the interest that leads to them.</p>
  <p><strong>Storytelling:</strong> This is where AI really shines for kids. Try collaborative storytelling: your child writes a paragraph, then asks AI to continue the story, then they write the next part. It's like having a writing partner who never gets tired.</p>
  <p><strong>World-building:</strong> Older kids can use AI to help build detailed fictional worlds — maps, characters, histories, languages. They provide the creative vision; AI helps flesh out the details. This is how professional writers and game designers actually use AI.</p>
  <div class="callout">
    <strong>Family activity:</strong> Try "Story Relay" at dinner. One person starts a story, then asks AI to add a twist, then the next person continues. It's hilarious, creative, and teaches prompt engineering without calling it that.
  </div>
</div>

<div class="lesson-section">
  <span class="section-label">Keeping It Human</span>
  <h2 class="section-title">The creativity stays with your kid.</h2>
  <p>The most important thing to reinforce: AI is the paintbrush, your child is the artist. Here's how to keep human creativity at the center:</p>
  <ul>
    <li><strong>Start analog:</strong> Sketch ideas on paper first, then bring them to AI. The ideas come from the human.</li>
    <li><strong>Iterate and refine:</strong> Don't accept the first output. Teach your child to critique, adjust, and improve. "What would make this better?" is a creative thinking exercise.</li>
    <li><strong>Mix mediums:</strong> Use AI-generated art as a starting point, then paint over it, collage it, or combine it with handmade elements.</li>
    <li><strong>Credit properly:</strong> Talk about what "I made this" means when AI was involved. Honesty about the process builds integrity.</li>
  </ul>
</div>
