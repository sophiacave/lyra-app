<div class="lesson-section">
  <span class="section-label">Your Agreement</span>
  <h2 class="section-title">Build it together — that's the whole point.</h2>
  <p>The best AI plan isn't one you impose on your kids. It's one you build with them. When kids co-create the rules, they're invested in following them. When rules come from on high with no input, they find workarounds.</p>
  <p>Sit down as a family and work through these five areas together:</p>
  <ol>
    <li><strong>Which tools are approved?</strong> List the AI tools each family member can use. Be specific by age.</li>
    <li><strong>Where and when?</strong> Common spaces? Own rooms? Before homework is done? After?</li>
    <li><strong>Homework rules.</strong> The 3-step flow: attempt, assist, redo. No exceptions.</li>
    <li><strong>Privacy boundaries.</strong> What never goes into AI: names, addresses, photos, school info.</li>
    <li><strong>The open door.</strong> If AI generates something confusing, scary, or weird — come talk. No punishment, ever.</li>
  </ol>
  <div class="tip-box">
    <strong>Write it down.</strong> Post it on the fridge or somewhere visible. A verbal agreement gets forgotten. A written one gets followed.
  </div>
</div>

<div class="lesson-section">
  <span class="section-label">By Age Group</span>
  <h2 class="section-title">Rules that grow with your kids.</h2>
  <p>Your plan should have different tiers. Here's a starting framework you can customize:</p>
  <p><strong>Ages 5-8:</strong> AI use only with a parent present. Kid-specific tools only (reading apps, drawing tools). No chatbots. Maximum 20-30 minutes per session.</p>
  <p><strong>Ages 9-12:</strong> Approved tools can be used independently. Chatbots with parent account, supervised initially, then spot-checked. Homework flow always applies. Monthly review of chat history together.</p>
  <p><strong>Ages 13-15:</strong> Own accounts on approved platforms. Privacy settings configured by parent. Homework disclosure required. Quarterly check-ins replace monthly reviews.</p>
  <p><strong>Ages 16-17:</strong> Full autonomy with agreed boundaries. Ongoing conversation replaces formal reviews. Parent available as advisor, not gatekeeper. Trust is earned and maintained.</p>
  <div class="callout">
    <strong>The graduation model:</strong> Think of AI access like driving. You start with a learner's permit (supervised), move to a restricted license (some independence), and eventually get full autonomy. Each stage is earned through demonstrated responsibility.
  </div>
</div>

<div class="lesson-section">
  <span class="section-label">Monthly Review</span>
  <h2 class="section-title">Plans that don't evolve become irrelevant.</h2>
  <p>AI changes fast. Your plan needs to keep up. Set a monthly family check-in (15 minutes, that's it) to cover:</p>
  <ul>
    <li><strong>What's working?</strong> Celebrate the wins. Your kid used AI to learn something cool? Talk about it.</li>
    <li><strong>What's not working?</strong> Rules that are too strict? Too loose? Adjust without shame.</li>
    <li><strong>New tools?</strong> Kids often discover new AI tools before parents do. This is your chance to evaluate together.</li>
    <li><strong>Level up?</strong> Has your child demonstrated enough responsibility to earn more independence?</li>
  </ul>
  <p>Keep these conversations light. This isn't an interrogation. It's a team huddle. The goal is continuous improvement, not perfection.</p>
</div>

<div class="lesson-section">

  <p>Here's what you now have that most parents don't:</p>
  <ul>
    <li>A clear understanding of what AI is and how kids use it</li>
    <li>Age-appropriate tool recommendations and evaluation frameworks</li>
    <li>Homework strategies that teach instead of enable</li>
    <li>Critical thinking exercises that transfer beyond AI</li>
