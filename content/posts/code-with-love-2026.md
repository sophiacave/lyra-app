# Code with Love

There is a line of code behind every decision a product makes about you. Whether you see the paywall or the free version. Whether the algorithm shows you something helpful or something addictive. Whether the interface works with your screen reader or does not.

Someone wrote that code. And they made a choice.

## The Choice

Most technology is built to extract. Extract attention, extract data, extract money. The architecture is designed around engagement metrics and conversion funnels. The human at the other end is a user, a number, a CAC.

Code with love is a different choice. It means asking, before every commit: does this serve the person using it, or does it serve us at their expense?

This is not idealism. It is a filter. A decision-making framework that produces different software than the alternative.

## What It Looks Like in Practice

**Accessibility is not an afterthought.** Our academy works with screen readers. Our content is structured for people who process information differently. This is not compliance. This is love expressed as HTML semantics and ARIA labels.

**Education is not gated by income.** Our courses are free. Not freemium. Not free-with-upsell. Free because education should not cost money you do not have.

**AI is not a replacement for people.** We build AI tools that make people more capable, not tools that make people unnecessary. The difference sounds subtle. It is not.

**Privacy is not negotiable.** We do not track behavior for ad targeting. We do not sell data. We do not optimize for time-on-site at the expense of time well spent.

## Why It Matters Now

AI is the most powerful technology humans have ever built. It will reshape every industry, every institution, every interaction. The question is not whether this will happen. It is who builds it and what they value.

Right now, most AI is built by companies optimizing for quarterly earnings. The humans building it are talented. The incentives guiding them are not.

Code with love is an argument that the incentive structure matters. That the values embedded in code become the values experienced by users. That the choices engineers make at 2 AM determine what millions of people encounter the next morning.

## The Source Code

I am a disabled transgender woman. I have experienced what technology feels like when it is not built for you. When the form does not have your gender. When the payment system does not accommodate your disability. When the algorithm does not know what to do with your existence.

That experience is not a liability. It is source code. It is the reason I know the difference between software that includes and software that tolerates.

Like One exists because I believe the people most excluded by technology are the ones who should build it. Not because exclusion makes you wise. Because it makes you unable to ignore what everyone else overlooks.

## The Compiler

Love is the compiler. Not the sentimental kind. The engineering kind.

When love compiles your decisions, accessibility is not a feature request. It is a requirement. Privacy is not a policy page. It is an architecture choice. Education is not a product. It is a right you happen to deliver through technology.

Every company has values on their website. Code with love means the values are in the codebase.

## Build Different

If you write code, you make choices every day about what the world looks like. You can optimize for engagement or for understanding. For extraction or for empowerment. For the median user or for the one in the margins.

The margins are where the real problems live. And the real problems are where the real impact is.

Code with love. Build for the person everyone else forgot. Ship software that makes someone's day less hard. That is the work.
