# Getting Started with Claude AI: Beginner to Power User in One Week

Most people use Claude like a search engine with better grammar. They type a question. They get an answer. They close the tab.

That is like buying a professional kitchen and using it to microwave leftovers.

Claude is the most capable AI writing and reasoning tool available today. But its real power is invisible to beginners. Nobody tells you about Projects, custom instructions, context windows, or the specific prompting patterns that separate amateur output from professional results.

This guide is the progression I wish I had. Seven days. Each day builds on the last. By the end, you will use Claude differently than 95% of people who pay for it.

## Day 1: Your First Real Conversation

Forget "write me a poem" demos. Open [claude.ai](https://claude.ai) and bring real work.

**What to try:**
- Paste an email you need to respond to. Ask Claude to draft a reply.
- Copy a document you are working on. Ask Claude to improve it.
- Describe a problem you are stuck on. Ask for three approaches.

**The one rule:** Be specific. "Help me write better" produces generic advice. "Rewrite this paragraph to be more direct and cut the word count by 40%" produces useful output.

**What you will learn:** Claude is better than you expect at first-draft work. But the output quality is directly proportional to how specific your input is.

## Day 2: Context Is Everything

Today you learn the single most important principle in AI: more context produces better output. Always.

**What to try:**
- Instead of "write a blog post about productivity," provide: your audience, your voice, your angle, an example of writing you like, and the specific point you want to make.
- Paste your company's about page, then ask Claude to write marketing copy. Compare the result to what you got without context on Day 1.
- Share a problem with background information. Then share the same problem without background. Notice the difference.

**The pattern:** Every time Claude gives you mediocre output, ask yourself: "What context am I assuming it has but never provided?"

The answer is almost always "a lot."

## Day 3: Roles and System Instructions

Claude follows instructions literally. Use this.

**What to try:**
- Start a conversation with: "You are a senior editor at a business publication. Your job is to make my writing tighter, clearer, and more authoritative. Cut any sentence that does not earn its place. Flag clichés. Be direct in your feedback."
- Try: "You are a CFO reviewing this proposal. Find every financial assumption. Challenge the weak ones. Tell me what a skeptical board member would ask."
- Try: "You are my brand voice. Here are five examples of my best writing: [paste examples]. Match this voice exactly in everything you produce."

**What you will learn:** Giving Claude a role and explicit standards transforms its output. The same prompt with and without a role produces noticeably different quality.

## Day 4: Claude Projects (This Changes Everything)

If you have Claude Pro ($20/month), Projects are the feature that justifies the subscription alone.

**What a Project is:** A persistent workspace where you set custom instructions and upload knowledge files. Every conversation inside that Project inherits the context automatically.

**What to build today:**
1. Create a Project called "My Business Writer"
2. In the custom instructions, define your brand voice, target audience, content standards, and any rules (word count limits, tone preferences, topics to avoid)
3. Upload key documents: your style guide, your best-performing content, your product descriptions, your customer personas
4. Start a conversation inside the Project and ask it to write something

**The difference:** Without a Project, you re-explain who you are and what you need every single conversation. With a Project, Claude already knows. The output quality jumps immediately because the context is already loaded. If you are coming from ChatGPT, see [how Claude Projects compare to Custom GPTs](/blog/custom-gpts-vs-claude-projects/) — the differences are significant.

**Power move:** Create separate Projects for different work streams. One for blog content. One for client communications. One for product strategy. Each with its own instructions and knowledge base.

## Day 5: The 200K Context Window

Claude can hold approximately 200,000 tokens in a single conversation. That is roughly 150,000 words — or an entire book.

Most people never use more than 1% of this.

**What to try:**
- Upload an entire report, contract, or manual. Ask Claude detailed questions about specific sections. Notice that it answers accurately from page 80 as easily as page 2.
- Paste the last three months of your company's meeting notes. Ask Claude to identify recurring themes, unresolved decisions, and action items nobody followed up on.
- Drop in your full website copy. Ask Claude to find inconsistencies in messaging, tone shifts, or promises you make on one page but contradict on another.

**Why this matters:** Other AI tools use retrieval — they search your document for relevant chunks and sometimes miss things. Claude actually reads the full document. For knowledge-heavy work, this is a fundamental difference, not an incremental one.

## Day 6: Advanced Prompting Patterns

Now you know the basics. Here are the patterns that separate power users from everyone else.

### Chain Prompting
Break complex tasks into steps. Instead of "write me a marketing strategy," try:
1. "Analyze my three closest competitors based on this information: [paste info]"
2. "Based on that analysis, identify three positioning gaps we could own"
3. "For the strongest gap, draft a messaging framework with headline, subhead, three supporting points, and proof points"

Each step builds on the last. The final output is dramatically better than asking for everything at once.

### The Critique Loop
Ask Claude to produce work, then ask it to critique its own work, then revise based on the critique. This self-correction pattern catches errors and improves quality in ways that a single prompt cannot.

### Constraint Stacking
The more constraints you give Claude, the more creative and specific the output becomes. "Write a headline" gives you generic. "Write a headline under 8 words that includes a number, addresses first-time founders, and avoids the word 'revolutionary'" gives you something you can actually use.

### Reference Anchoring
Paste examples of output you like. Say "match this quality and style." Claude calibrates to examples far better than it calibrates to abstract descriptions of what you want.

## Day 7: Building Systems, Not Conversations

The power user shift: stop having one-off conversations. Start building repeatable systems.

**What to build:**
- A Project that drafts your weekly newsletter from bullet points you provide
- A Project that reviews any document against your company's style guide and returns a tracked-changes-style edit
- A prompt template for your most common tasks that you copy-paste to start each session
- A workflow where Claude processes inputs from one format (meeting notes) and produces outputs in another (action items email, updated project plan, follow-up questions)

**The mindset shift:** Every time you find yourself doing the same kind of work with Claude more than twice, turn it into a system. Custom instructions, knowledge files, prompt templates. Make Claude do the repetitive setup so you can focus on the decisions.

## What Most People Never Figure Out

The gap between beginner and power user is not about knowing secret prompts. It is about three things:

1. **Context loading.** Power users give Claude everything it needs before asking for anything. Beginners give it almost nothing and wonder why the output is generic.

2. **Persistence.** Power users build Projects and templates so they never start from zero. Beginners have the same "getting to know you" conversation every single time.

3. **Iteration.** Power users treat Claude's first output as a draft and refine through feedback. Beginners accept the first response or give up.

Master these three patterns and you will extract more value from a $20/month subscription than most companies get from tools costing ten times as much.
