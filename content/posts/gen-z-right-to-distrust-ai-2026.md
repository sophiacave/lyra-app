# Gen Z Is Right to Distrust AI

Excitement about AI among Gen Z has dropped 14 percentage points in the past year. Anger toward AI has risen to 31 percent. More than a thousand educators have signed an open letter calling AI adoption a threat to student learning.

The tech industry's response has been predictable: Gen Z just does not understand yet. They will come around. They are afraid of change.

No. They are paying attention.

## What Gen Z Sees

Gen Z watched AI companies scrape the entire internet — including their art, their writing, their conversations — and use it to build products that replace them. Then those companies charged them a subscription to use the replacement.

They saw universities sign million-dollar contracts with OpenAI and Google, mandating AI tools on campus while cutting arts programs and adjunct positions. The money went to Silicon Valley. The cuts happened locally.

They noticed that every AI company says "AI for good" while lobbying against regulation, fighting labor organizing, and building surveillance tools for governments. The marketing says empowerment. The product says extraction.

They are not confused. They see the architecture. And they do not trust it.

## Why They Are Right

AI was built by the most concentrated accumulation of capital in human history, and it shows. The default AI experience is:

- Pay a subscription to access a product trained on your own data
- Use an interface designed to maximize engagement, not understanding
- Receive output that sounds confident regardless of accuracy
- Get locked into one vendor's ecosystem through "education" that only teaches their tools

This is not a technology problem. It is an incentive problem. AI is extraordinarily powerful. But the people who built it are optimizing for revenue, not for the people using it.

Gen Z sees this because they grew up watching the same playbook with social media. They know what it looks like when a technology promises connection and delivers extraction.

## The Part Nobody Says Out Loud

Here is what the AI industry will not tell Gen Z:

**You do not need a subscription.** Open-source AI models run on your laptop for free. Ollama gives you a local AI in three commands. No data leaves your machine. No tokens. No tracking.

**You do not need their courses.** AI literacy is documented extensively by researchers, educators, and practitioners who are not trying to sell you anything. Free. Complete. Vendor-neutral.

**You do not need to adopt AI at all.** For many tasks, AI is slower, less accurate, and more expensive than the thing it is replacing. The industry does not want you to evaluate this critically, because critical evaluation reduces adoption.

**Your distrust is leverage.** A generation that refuses to adopt AI uncritically is the most powerful force for making AI better. The only reason AI companies will build responsibly is if users demand it. And the only users who will demand it are the ones who do not trust the default.

## What AI Built Differently Looks Like

I am a disabled transgender woman who builds AI tools. Not at a corporation. At a nonprofit. Not with venture capital. With stubbornness and a MacBook.

I built an AI education platform because the existing options were either expensive, vendor-locked, inaccessible, or all three. Our academy has 30 courses and 300 lessons. It is free. Not freemium. Free.

The courses teach AI literacy — how the technology works, when it fails, how to use it without being used by it. They work with any AI tool, not just one vendor's product. They include courses on running AI locally with zero cost and zero data sharing.

This is what AI built for people — not for quarterly earnings — looks like. It exists because someone who was excluded by the industry decided to build something better.

## What To Do With Your Distrust

Do not let anyone tell you that your skepticism is ignorance. It is the most valuable thing you bring to this conversation.

Learn AI. Not because the industry tells you to. Because understanding how it works is the only way to hold the people building it accountable. Because knowing its limitations is the only way to protect yourself from its misuse. Because the future of AI should be shaped by people who question it, not by people who worship it.

Your distrust is not the problem. It is the beginning of the solution.
