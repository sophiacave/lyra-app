Most people think starting a nonprofit takes months. Lawyers. Consultants. Endless paperwork. Thousands in fees before you help a single person.

We filed the Like One Foundation — a 501(c)(3) dedicated to funding HIV cure research — in a single day. No lawyer. No consultant. Total cost: $275.

AI did the heavy lifting. Here is exactly how.

AI breaks that gate open.

The same approach we used works for any complex legal filing: business formation, trademark applications, grant proposals, compliance documentation. The pattern is the same. AI researches, drafts, checks, and organizes. You review, sign, and submit.

## Step 1: Research Requirements (20 Minutes)

Before touching any forms, we needed to understand the full picture. We asked Claude to map every requirement for a 501(c)(3) in our state:

- Federal IRS requirements (Form 1023-EZ eligibility criteria)
- State-level nonprofit formation requirements
- Required governing documents (articles of incorporation, bylaws)
- Board composition requirements
- Conflict of interest policies
- Record-keeping obligations

**The key prompt pattern:**

```
I'm forming a 501(c)(3) nonprofit foundation in [STATE].
The organization will [MISSION - one sentence].
Expected annual revenue: under $50,000 for the first 3 years.
Total assets: under $250,000.

Give me:
1. Every federal and state requirement I need to satisfy
2. Every document I need to prepare
3. Every form I need to file, in order
4. Common mistakes that cause rejections
5. The complete checklist, step by step
```

This prompt produces a comprehensive roadmap. The "common mistakes" part is critical — it surfaces gotchas that would otherwise cost you a rejection and months of back-and-forth with the IRS.

**What AI found that saved us:** Form 1023-EZ has specific eligibility criteria most people do not check carefully. If your projected revenue exceeds $50,000 or your assets exceed $250,000, you are disqualified and must use the full Form 1023 (which costs $600 and takes significantly longer). AI caught this threshold immediately and confirmed we qualified.

## Step 2: Draft Governing Documents (45 Minutes)

Every 501(c)(3) needs:

1. **Articles of Incorporation** — the legal document that creates your nonprofit entity at the state level
2. **Bylaws** — the internal rules for how your organization operates
3. **Conflict of Interest Policy** — required by the IRS for tax-exempt organizations
4. **Mission Statement** — clear, specific, aligned with IRS charitable purpose categories

We used Claude to draft each document, providing our specific details:

```
Draft articles of incorporation for a [STATE] nonprofit corporation.

Organization name: [NAME]
Purpose: [SPECIFIC CHARITABLE PURPOSE]
Registered agent: [NAME AND ADDRESS]
Initial board of directors: [NAMES]

Include all required IRS language for 501(c)(3) eligibility:
- Organized exclusively for charitable purposes under Section 501(c)(3)
- No part of net earnings inures to the benefit of any private individual
- Upon dissolution, assets distributed to another 501(c)(3) organization
- Does not participate in political campaigns
```

**Critical detail:** The IRS requires specific dissolution language in your articles of incorporation. If this language is missing or wrong, your application gets rejected. AI nails this every time because it is pulling from the actual IRC Section 501(c)(3) requirements — not paraphrasing from a blog post written in 2019.

We reviewed every document line by line. AI drafts. Humans verify. That is the pattern.

## Step 3: File State Formation (Same Day)

Most states let you file articles of incorporation online. We:

1. Created an account on the state's business filing portal
2. Uploaded the AI-drafted articles of incorporation
3. Paid the state filing fee
4. Received confirmation within hours

**Pro tip:** Some states offer expedited processing for an additional fee. For a standard filing, most states process within 1-7 business days. We got same-day confirmation.

## Step 4: Get an EIN (Same Day)

The IRS lets you apply for an Employer Identification Number (EIN) online at [irs.gov](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/apply-for-an-employer-identification-number-ein-online). It takes about 10 minutes and you get your EIN immediately.

You need your EIN before filing Form 1023-EZ. Do this step the moment your state formation is confirmed.

## Step 5: Prepare and File Form 1023-EZ (90 Minutes)

Form 1023-EZ is filed electronically through [pay.gov](https://www.pay.gov). It is a streamlined form — roughly 3 pages of questions about your organization's structure, purpose, and finances.

Here is where AI becomes indispensable:

```
I'm filling out IRS Form 1023-EZ for a 501(c)(3).
Here are my organization's details: [DETAILS]

For each section of the form, tell me:
1. What the question is really asking
2. The correct answer based on my details
3. Common mistakes for that specific question
4. Any red flags that trigger IRS scrutiny
```

AI walks you through every field. It explains what the IRS is actually looking for behind each question — which is not always obvious from the form language.

**What AI caught:** Several questions on the 1023-EZ use specific legal terminology ("private foundation" vs "public charity," "substantial part" test vs "expenditure" test). Choosing wrong does not just delay your application — it can change your tax obligations entirely. AI explained each option clearly and recommended the correct classification for our structure.

We reviewed every answer, double-checked against IRS instructions, and submitted.

**Filing fee: $275, paid through pay.gov.**

## Step 6: Open a Bank Account (Same Day)

With your EIN and state formation documents in hand, you can open a nonprofit bank account immediately. We used Mercury — they approve nonprofit accounts online, usually within 24 hours.

Having a dedicated bank account from day one is not optional. It is how you maintain the separation between personal and organizational funds that the IRS requires.

## What AI Cannot Do (And What Still Needs a Human)

Let's be honest about the boundaries:

- **AI cannot sign legal documents.** You are the responsible party.
- **AI cannot guarantee IRS approval.** It can dramatically reduce errors, but approval is the IRS's decision.
- **AI cannot replace legal counsel for complex situations.** If you have international operations, significant assets, or unusual revenue structures — talk to a lawyer.
- **AI cannot file forms for you.** You still click submit.

What AI does brilliantly: compress the research, drafting, and error-checking phases from weeks into hours. The actual filing still requires a human with a signature and a credit card.
