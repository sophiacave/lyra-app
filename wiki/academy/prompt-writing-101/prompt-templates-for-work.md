  Template 1
  ## The Email Writer.


    Write a [type: professional/casual/follow-up/cold] email.

From: [your role/name]
To: [recipient and their role]
Purpose: [what you want to accomplish]
Context: [relevant background — previous conversations, relationship, urgency]
Tone: [warm/formal/urgent/casual]
CTA: [what action should the reader take?]
Length: [under X words/sentences]

Don't: [sound desperate/be too formal/use exclamation marks/etc.]



  Template 2
  ## The Content Creator.


    Write a [format: blog post/LinkedIn post/tweet thread/newsletter].

Topic: [specific topic, not broad]
Audience: [who reads this and what they care about]
Angle: [what makes YOUR take different?]
Goal: [educate/entertain/persuade/drive traffic]
Voice: [describe it or paste a sample of your writing]
Length: [word count or section count]
Structure: [hook → story → lesson → CTA / or your preferred format]

Don't: [use clickbait/be generic/sound like a textbook]



  Template 3
  ## The Analyst.


    Analyze [the thing you want analyzed].

Data/Input: [paste the data, text, or describe what you're working with]
Looking for: [patterns/risks/opportunities/recommendations]
Context: [why this analysis matters, what decision it informs]
Audience: [who will read this — technical depth accordingly]

Format:
- Start with a 2-sentence executive summary
- Then detail your findings in [bullets/sections/table]
- End with [recommendations/next steps/questions to investigate]

Don't: [hedge with "it depends" — commit to conclusions]

**Card 2:**

Back: I need [number] ideas for [what].  Context: [situation, industry, audience] What has worked before: [examples] What I want to avoid: [overdone ideas] Constraints: [budget/timeline/team]  For each idea give me: - A catchy name - One-sentence description - Why it could work - Biggest risk  Push past the obvious. The first 5 ideas anyone would think of are useless.

**Card 3:**

  Template 7
  ## The Report Writer.


    Write a [type: weekly/monthly/quarterly/project] report.

Period: [timeframe covered]
Audience: [who reads this — manager/leadership/client]
Key data: [paste metrics, milestones, or raw notes]
Wins: [what went well]
Challenges: [what was difficult and why]
Next steps: [what is planned for the next period]

Format:
- Executive summary (3 sentences, bottom line first)
- Key metrics table
- Wins (bullet points)
- Challenges + how we're addressing them
- Next period priorities

Don't: [pad with filler/use vague language like "good progress"/
exceed [X] words]



  Template 8
  ## The Decision Memo.


    Help me structure a decision between [Option A] and [Option B].

Context: [why this decision matters and what it affects]
Criteria: [what factors matter most — cost, speed, quality, risk]
Constraints: [budget, timeline, team size, existing commitments]
What I'm leaning toward: [your gut feeling and why]

Format:
- 1-sentence recommendation (lead with it)
- Comparison table: [criteria as columns, options as rows]
- Top 3 reasons for the recommendation
- Biggest risk of this choice + mitigation
- What would change your recommendation

Don't: [hedge with "it depends" — commit to a clear recommendation
with reasoning]



  Template 9
  ## The Customer Response.


    Write a response to this customer [complaint/question/request]:

Customer message: [paste their message]
Context: [their account status, history, previous interactions]
Resolution: [what we can actually offer — refund, fix, workaround]
Tone: [empathetic/apologetic/firm-but-fair/enthusiastic]
Brand voice: [describe or paste a sample response]

Don't: [be defensive/blame the customer/make promises we can't keep/
use corporate phrases like "we value your business"]
Must: [acknowledge their frustration before offering solutions/
include a specific next step with a timeline]

  ## Which template fits the job?


### Quiz

**Q1: Your quarterly numbers are in and your manager needs a summary by end of day. Which template?**
    A. Email Writer
    B. Content Creator
  ✓ C. Report Writer
    D. Brainstormer
  *The Report Writer template gives you executive summary, metrics table, wins, challenges, and next steps — exactly what a quarterly summary needs.*

**Q2: A frustrated customer emailed about a billing error on their account. Which template?**
    A. Email Writer
  ✓ B. Customer Response
    C. Editor / Reviewer
    D. Decision Memo
  *The Customer Response template is built for exactly this: it includes fields for the customer message, resolution, tone, and the critical instruction to acknowledge frustration before offering solutions.*

**Q3: You need to decide between two vendors for your company CRM. Which template?**
    A. Analyst
  ✓ B. Decision Memo
    C. Meeting Prep
    D. Brainstormer
  *The Decision Memo template structures a clear recommendation with a comparison table, criteria weighting, and risk assessment — exactly what you need to present a vendor decision.*


  Your Assignment
  ## Build your own template library.

  The templates above cover common tasks, but **your most powerful templates will be the ones you build for YOUR recurring work.**

[Interactive: QuizMC]

Within a month, you'll have a personal prompt library that handles 80% of your AI work in seconds.

  **How to build your first template:**

    - Identify a task you do at least once a week — weekly reports, client emails, meeting prep, content creation

    - Write the best prompt you can for it — use the full RCFCE framework, spend extra time getting it right

    - Run it and iterate — refine until the output is exactly what you want

    - Replace the specific details with variable slots — [client name], [this week's metrics], [meeting topic]

    - Save it somewhere accessible — a note, a doc, a text expander, wherever you can grab it in seconds

    - Use and improve — every time you use the template, notice what you tweak and update the template accordingly



  The templates in this lesson are your starting library. But the ones you build yourself — tuned to YOUR voice, YOUR work, YOUR audience — will always outperform generic ones. Start with one template this week. Add one more next week. By month's end, you'll wonder how you ever worked without them.
