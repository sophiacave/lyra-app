  The Problem
  ## AI doesn't know what you sound like. Yet.

  Out of the box, AI writes in a generic, slightly corporate, aggressively helpful tone. It uses phrases like "dive deep," "in today's fast-paced world," and "let's unpack this." It sounds like everyone and no one at the same time.
  That's not AI's fault. It's writing in its default voice because you haven't taught it yours. This lesson fixes that permanently.


  Define
  ## Building your voice document.

  A voice document is a reference sheet that tells AI exactly how you communicate. Create one and paste it at the start of every content session. Here's what to include:
  **Tone words (pick 3-5):** Examples: "Direct, warm, occasionally irreverent, confident but not arrogant." These give AI a personality target.
  **Writing rules:** "Short paragraphs. No jargon unless I define it. Use contractions. Sentence fragments are OK for emphasis. Never use 'dive deep,' 'leverage,' 'unlock,' or 'game-changer.'"
  **Sample writing:** Paste 3-5 paragraphs of your best writing — content that sounds most like you at your best. Tell AI: "Match this voice exactly."
  **Audience awareness:** "My audience is [who]. They're sophisticated enough to handle [X] but turned off by [Y]. They appreciate honesty and hate fluff."


  The Prompt
  ## The voice-training prompt that changes everything.

  Use this to train AI on your voice at the start of any session:
  *"Here's my brand voice guide. Study it carefully and match this voice in everything you write for me today. [Paste voice document]. Before writing anything, confirm you understand my voice by describing it back to me in 2-3 sentences."*
  That last instruction — asking AI to describe your voice back — is crucial. It forces the model to internalize the patterns rather than just scanning past them. If the description doesn't sound right, correct it until it does. That calibration step takes 30 seconds and dramatically improves every piece of content in the session.


Prompt — Brand Voice Definition

```
You are a brand voice analyst. I'm going to give you 3-5
samples of my best writing. Analyze them and produce a
Brand Voice Document with these sections:

1. TONE (3-5 adjectives that define my voice)
2. SENTENCE STYLE (avg length, structure patterns, rhythm)
3. VOCABULARY (formality level, jargon policy, signature phrases)
4. PERSONALITY (how I use humor, empathy, authority)
5. KILL LIST (words/phrases I never use — infer from what's absent)
6. AUDIENCE LENS (who I'm writing for, based on how I address them)

Here are my writing samples:
[PASTE 3-5 PARAGRAPHS OF YOUR BEST CONTENT]

After the analysis, describe my voice back to me in 2-3
sentences as if you're explaining it to a new ghostwriter.
I'll correct anything that's off before we start writing.
```

  ## Voice document components.


### Voice Document Building Blocks

**Card 1:**
Front: Tone Words
Back: Pick 3-5 adjectives that describe your communication personality. Example: Direct, warm, occasionally irreverent, confident but not arrogant.

**Card 2:**
Front: Writing Rules
Back: Explicit instructions about structure and style. Example: Short paragraphs. No jargon. Use contractions. Never say "dive deep" or "game-changer".

**Card 3:**
Front: Sample Writing
Back: Paste 3-5 paragraphs of your best content. Tell AI: Match this voice exactly. Examples teach more than descriptions.

**Card 4:**
Front: Audience Awareness
Back: Who your reader is, what they can handle, and what turns them off. The AI writes differently when it knows who is reading.

**Card 5:**
Front: Kill List
Back: Words and phrases that must never appear in your content. AI will respect explicit bans better than vague tone instructions.

  ## Match voice document elements to their purpose.


  Template
  ## The complete voice document template.

  Copy this template, fill it in, and save it somewhere you can paste at the start of every AI session. This is the single most impactful thing you can do to improve AI output quality:


Template — Brand Voice Document

```
BRAND VOICE DOCUMENT — [Your Name / Brand]

TONE (pick 3-5 adjectives):
[e.g., Direct, warm, occasionally irreverent, confident
but not arrogant, conversational]

WRITING RULES:
- Sentence length: [e.g., mix short punchy with medium]
- Paragraph length: [e.g., 2-3 sentences max]
- Contractions: [yes/no]
- Jargon policy: [e.g., only if defined immediately]
- Sentence fragments: [OK for emphasis? yes/no]
- Formatting: [e.g., use bold for key phrases, headers
  every 200-300 words]

KILL LIST (never use these words/phrases):
[e.g., dive deep, leverage, unlock, game-changer,
in today's fast-paced world, let's unpack this,
at the end of the day, synergy, circle back]

SIGNATURE PHRASES (words/patterns I use often):
[e.g., "Here's the thing," "That's the move,"
"Not the vibe," short paragraphs for emphasis]

AUDIENCE:
[Who they are, what they know, what turns them off]

SAMPLE WRITING (paste 3-5 paragraphs of your best):
[Your actual writing samples here]
```


  The kill list is especially powerful. AI will respect explicit bans far better than vague instructions like "don't be too corporate." If you know the words that make your skin crawl when you read AI output, name them specifically and they will disappear from the output.


  Advanced
  ## Voice consistency across different content types.

  Your voice stays the same everywhere, but its expression shifts depending on the context. You are still you on Twitter and in a blog post — but the cadence, depth, and formality adjust. Here is how to handle that:
  **Social media voice:** Your most casual, punchy self. Shorter sentences. More attitude. Contractions everywhere. This is you talking to a friend in a crowded room — you need to be loud, clear, and memorable in a few words.
  **Blog/newsletter voice:** Your teaching self. Still conversational, but with more room for nuance, stories, and depth. This is you sitting across a coffee table from someone who has 5-10 minutes to listen.
  **Sales page voice:** Your most persuasive self. Still honest, still you — but with clear structure, benefit-focused language, and confident authority. This is not "salesy" — it is you genuinely believing in what you are offering and communicating that clearly.
  **Email voice:** Your most intimate self. Emails land in personal inboxes, so they should feel personal. Write like you are sending a message to one specific person, not broadcasting to a list.
  Add platform-specific notes to your voice document: *"On Twitter, I'm punchier and more opinionated. In emails, I'm warmer and more personal. In blog posts, I go deeper with more examples."* This gives AI the nuance to adjust without losing your core identity.


  Common Mistakes
  ## Five things that kill your voice in AI content.

  **1. Accepting the first draft.** AI's first attempt is never its best. Ask for revisions: "This is too formal. Make it sound like I'm talking to a friend over coffee."
  **2. Using vague tone instructions.** "Make it sound professional" means nothing. "Write like a smart friend explaining something at a dinner party" means everything.
  **3. Not providing examples.** AI learns better from examples than instructions. Show it what good looks like, don't just describe it.
  **4. Ignoring platform differences.** Your voice stays the same, but its expression shifts. You're still you on Twitter and LinkedIn — but the cadence, length, and formality level adjust. Specify this: "Same voice, but LinkedIn-appropriate length and tone."
  **5. Forgetting the kill list.** Every brand has words and phrases that should never appear. Build a "never use these words" list and include it in your voice document. AI will respect it if you're explicit.


  Testing
  ## The voice consistency test — does it pass?

  After AI generates content with your voice document loaded, run this three-part test before publishing:
  **The read-aloud test:** Read the content out loud. If any sentence makes you stumble, sounds unnatural, or feels like something you would never say in conversation, rewrite it. Your content should sound like you talking, not you performing.
  **The friend test:** Would a close friend recognize this as your writing without seeing your name on it? If the answer is no, the voice is not calibrated yet. Go back to AI with specific feedback: "This paragraph sounds too formal. Rewrite it like I'm explaining this over drinks."
  **The kill list test:** Search the content for every word on your kill list. AI sometimes slips banned words back in, especially in longer pieces. A quick Ctrl+F catches these before they reach your audience.
  **The consistency test:** Compare the new piece to something you published last month. Does the voice feel like the same person? If there is a noticeable shift in tone, formality, or personality, something drifted. Realign with your voice document.
  These tests take under five minutes and are the difference between content that sounds like you and content that sounds like AI pretending to be you. The audience can always tell the difference, even if they cannot articulate why.


  Evolution
  ## Your voice will evolve — and that is good.

  Your voice document is not permanent. As you grow as a creator, your voice will naturally evolve. You will become more confident, more opinionated, more refined. Update your voice document every quarter to reflect who you are now, not who you were six months ago.
  When you update, paste your three most recent best-performing pieces and ask AI to analyze how your voice has shifted. The evolution is usually subtle — slightly shorter sentences, more willingness to share personal stories, a growing comfort with strong opinions. Capture these shifts in the document so AI evolves with you.


  Try It
  ## Create your voice document now.

  Gather 3-5 pieces of your best writing. Open AI and paste this: *"Analyze these writing samples and describe my brand voice. Cover: tone, sentence structure, vocabulary level, personality traits, and what makes this voice distinctive. Then create a 'Voice Guide' I can paste into future sessions."* Review the output. Refine it. Save it somewhere you can access every time you start a content session. This one document will transform your AI output quality overnight.


  Case Study
  ## Voice before and after — the transformation.

  Here is what happens when you go from no voice document to a calibrated one:
  **Without voice document:** "In today's competitive landscape, leveraging AI tools can help content creators unlock their full potential and drive meaningful engagement with their target audience." — Generic, corporate, could belong to anyone.
  **With voice document:** "Look, most content advice is trash. Use AI like a microwave — throw something in, hope it comes out edible. That's not how this works. AI is your sous chef. You still run the kitchen." — Specific, opinionated, unmistakably one person.
  The difference is not talent. It is input. The second version came from the same AI model, given explicit tone words (direct, irreverent, confident), writing rules (short paragraphs, no jargon, contractions required), and a kill list (never say leverage, unlock, landscape, or game-changer). The voice document is the input that produces the output you want.
