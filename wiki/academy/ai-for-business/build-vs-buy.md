  # Build vs. Buy.

  Should you build a custom AI solution, buy a SaaS tool, hire a consultant, or just wait? This lesson gives you the framework to decide — fast.


  ### After this lesson you'll know


    - The four strategic options: Build, Buy, Hire, and Wait — and when each is right

    - The 5 key factors that determine which option fits your situation

    - How to match real business scenarios to the right approach

    - Which factor should carry the most weight in your decision




  Section 1
  ## The four options.

  Every AI decision your business faces comes down to four choices. Each one has a different cost profile, timeline, risk level, and set of circumstances where it makes sense. Most businesses default to Buy because it is the easiest — but that is not always right.

  Flip each card to understand the cost, timeline, risk, and ideal use case for each option. Pay special attention to the "Best when" criteria — these are the conditions that must be true for each option to make sense. If the conditions do not match your situation, that option is wrong regardless of how appealing it sounds.


### The Four AI Strategy Options

**Card 1:**
Front: BUY a SaaS Tool
Back: Best when: the problem is common, budget is under $5K/year, you need results fast, and no sensitive data is involved. Examples: Jasper for writing, Zapier for automation, Otter.ai for transcription. Cost: $20-$500/month. Timeline: days. Risk: low.

**Card 2:**
Front: BUILD Custom AI
Back: Best when: your problem is genuinely unique, you have developers on staff, budget exceeds $10K, and data sensitivity requires keeping everything in-house. Examples: custom ML model, proprietary recommendation engine. Cost: $10K-$200K+. Timeline: 1-6 months. Risk: high.

**Card 3:**
Front: HIRE a Consultant
Back: Best when: your needs are specific enough that off-the-shelf tools do not fit, but not so unique that a custom build is warranted. Also when you have budget ($2K-$15K) but no technical staff. Examples: custom GPT setup, AI strategy session, workflow automation. Timeline: 2-6 weeks.

**Card 4:**
Front: WAIT (Do Nothing Yet)
Back: Best when: budget is under $500, the problem is not urgent, or the AI tools in your category are evolving so fast that buying now means replacing in 6 months. AI tool prices drop every quarter. Sometimes the best move is to revisit in 90 days.


  Section 2
  ## The five decision factors.

  The right choice between Build, Buy, Hire, and Wait depends on five factors. None of them alone gives you the answer — it is the combination that matters. Understanding these factors transforms you from someone who guesses at strategy to someone who scores the decision and lets data lead. Here is each one and what it tells you:

  **1. Problem Uniqueness.** Is the problem your business faces something every business deals with — or is it genuinely specific to your company, your industry, or your proprietary process? The more unique the problem, the less likely an off-the-shelf SaaS tool solves it well. Common problems have common tools. Unique problems may require custom work.

  **2. Budget.** Not just what you have, but what makes financial sense given the expected ROI. A $500/year tool that saves $10,000 is a no-brainer. A $50,000 custom build for a problem that saves $8,000 per year is a five-year payback — maybe not worth it. Use the ROI formula from Lesson 3 before making any budget commitment.

  **3. Technical Capability.** What can your team actually maintain after the initial setup? A custom-built solution with no one to support it becomes a liability. If you have no technical staff, anything that requires ongoing development is a hire or buy decision — not a build. Be honest about this. Many businesses overestimate their internal technical capacity.

  **4. Speed Needed.** How quickly do you need results? If the problem is urgent — you are losing customers, or a manual process is creating daily errors — the right answer is usually Buy. The fastest path to working AI is almost always a SaaS tool. Custom builds take months. Consultants take weeks. SaaS tools take days.

  **5. Data Sensitivity.** Does the AI need access to customer PII, financial records, health data, or proprietary intellectual property? If yes, sending that data to a third-party SaaS tool may violate contracts, regulations (GDPR, HIPAA, SOC 2), or customer trust. Sensitive data often pushes the decision toward Build or a Hire who can set up a private deployment.

  Vendor Evaluation
  ## Seven questions to ask any AI vendor.

  If you decide to Buy, you still need to evaluate which vendor to buy from. These seven questions separate serious tools from marketing fluff. Ask them in every demo, every sales call, every evaluation.

  **1. "Where does my data go?"** Acceptable answers: "Your data is stored in [specific region], encrypted at rest and in transit, and not used for model training." Red flag: vague answers about "cloud infrastructure" or inability to specify data residency.

  **2. "Can I export my data if I leave?"** Acceptable answer: "Yes, you can export all data in standard formats (CSV, JSON, API)." Red flag: no export option, proprietary formats only, or a fee to export your own data.

  **3. "What happens when the AI is wrong?"** Acceptable answer: "We have a feedback loop, human review options, and accuracy metrics we share with customers." Red flag: claims of 99% accuracy without methodology, or dismissal of the question.

  **4. "What does your pricing look like at 3x my current usage?"** Acceptable answer: clear pricing tiers with predictable scaling. Red flag: usage-based pricing with no caps, or inability to estimate costs at higher volume.

  **5. "Do you have SOC 2, GDPR compliance, or equivalent certifications?"** Acceptable answer: yes, with documentation available. Red flag: "we are working on it" or "our cloud provider has it" (that is not the same thing).

  **6. "What is your uptime over the last 12 months?"** Acceptable answer: 99.5% or higher with a public status page. Red flag: no status page, no uptime data, or refusal to share incident history.

  **7. "Can I talk to three current customers in my industry?"** Acceptable answer: yes, here are references. Red flag: "we cannot share customer names" (every serious vendor has reference customers willing to talk).

  Common Mistakes
  ## Five build-vs-buy traps that waste money.

  The decision framework works. But even with a framework, businesses fall into predictable traps. Knowing these in advance saves you from learning them the expensive way.

  **Trap 1: Building because "we need it custom."** Most businesses overestimate how unique their problem is. Before you commit to a custom build, search for SaaS tools in your exact industry vertical. Nine times out of ten, someone has already built what you need and is selling it for $50/month. The uniqueness test: if you cannot explain in one sentence what makes your problem different from every other company in your industry, it is not unique enough to justify building.

  **Trap 2: Buying the most expensive tool "because it is enterprise grade."** Enterprise features — SSO, audit logs, dedicated support — matter when you have 50+ users and compliance requirements. For a 5-person team, you are paying for features you will never use. Start with the tool that solves your problem at the lowest price. Upgrade when you hit real limits, not imagined ones.

  **Trap 3: Hiring a consultant without a clear scope.** "Help us figure out AI" is not a scope. A good consultant engagement has a defined deliverable, timeline, and success metric before you sign the contract. Without these, you pay for exploration instead of results. Good scope: "Set up an AI-powered FAQ chatbot that handles our top 20 questions within 3 weeks." Bad scope: "Advise us on AI strategy."

  **Trap 4: Waiting because "AI is changing too fast."** AI will always be changing. Waiting for the "right moment" means waiting forever. The cost of waiting is not zero — it is the value you are not capturing while your competitors are. If the ROI math works today, start today. You can always switch tools later.

  **Trap 5: Buying multiple tools in the same category.** Jasper AND Claude AND ChatGPT all do writing. Having all three means nobody masters any of them, and you pay three subscriptions for one capability. Pick one per category. Master it. Switch if it genuinely does not work — but give it 30 days first.

  ## Make the call.

  Five scenario-based questions. For each one, apply the five factors and recommend the right strategy. These are real decisions — not edge cases.


### Quiz

**Q1: A 10-person marketing agency wants to use AI to generate first drafts of blog posts and social content. Their budget is $200/month and they need to start this week. What is the right call?**
    A. Build — agencies need custom models for brand consistency
  ✓ B. Buy — this is a common problem with excellent SaaS tools that deploy in hours
    C. Hire a consultant — content is too brand-sensitive for off-the-shelf tools
    D. Wait — AI writing tools are still too immature for professional content
  *Common problem, reasonable budget, urgent timeline, no sensitive data. This is a textbook Buy scenario. Tools like Jasper, Claude, or Copy.ai handle this well at $20-$100/month and can be up and running same day.*

**Q2: A wealth management firm wants AI to analyze client portfolios and suggest rebalancing strategies. Client financial data is involved. Budget: $50,000. What is the right call?**
    A. Buy — plenty of fintech SaaS tools handle portfolio analysis
    B. Wait — financial AI is too risky for wealth management
  ✓ C. Build or hire a specialist — financial data sensitivity and regulatory requirements rule out generic SaaS
    D. Hire a general AI consultant for a quick setup
  *Client financial data (PII + regulated) and the high-stakes nature of portfolio recommendations require a solution built with compliance in mind. A generic SaaS tool cannot guarantee the data privacy or regulatory controls required. Build or hire a specialist with fintech AI experience.*

**Q3: A restaurant owner wants to use AI to respond to Google reviews and social media comments. She has no technical staff. Budget: $30/month. What is the right call?**
    A. Build — review responses need to sound like her specifically
  ✓ B. Buy — SaaS tools like Podium or a general AI assistant handle this well
    C. Hire a consultant to build a custom review response system
    D. Wait — AI social media tools are not ready for restaurant use
  *Classic Buy scenario. The problem is common, the budget fits, there is no sensitive data, and she needs it to work without technical maintenance. Tools like Podium, Birdeye, or even Claude with a simple prompt template solve this for under $30/month.*

**Q4: A logistics company wants to predict delivery delays using 5 years of their proprietary shipment data, weather patterns, and carrier performance records. Budget: $80,000. They have two developers. What is the right call?**
    A. Buy — logistics SaaS tools can handle this without custom development
    B. Wait — predictive AI is too expensive for most logistics businesses
  ✓ C. Build — the problem is unique, data is proprietary, team has technical capacity, and budget supports it
    D. Hire a consultant for a 2-week setup
  *Unique problem (proprietary 5-year dataset), significant budget, technical staff available, and the competitive advantage of a custom model justifies the investment. This is one of the rare scenarios where Build is the right answer.*

**Q5: A small nonprofit wants AI to help draft grant applications. Budget: $0 (no AI spend authorized). Timeline: 3 months before next grant cycle. What is the right call?**
    A. Hire a consultant — grant writing is too important to risk on free tools
    B. Build a custom model on volunteer developer time
  ✓ C. Use free tiers of existing AI tools — Claude free, ChatGPT free, or similar
    D. Wait until the board approves a budget
  *Zero budget does not mean zero options. Claude, ChatGPT, and Gemini all have free tiers that handle grant writing drafts well. Start there, demonstrate value, then make the case for a small paid subscription. Do not wait — use what is available now.*


  Section 5
  ## Decision priority.

  When you sit down to make a build-vs-buy call, you should weigh the five factors in a specific order. Some factors eliminate options outright; others just nudge the decision. Put them in order from most to least decisive.

  **Data Sensitivity eliminates first.** If your data is regulated (HIPAA, GDPR, SOC 2 requirements), several options disappear immediately. You cannot use a free-tier SaaS tool for patient health records or financial PII. This factor is binary — either the option meets your data requirements or it does not. No amount of good scores on other factors overcomes a data sensitivity disqualification.

  **Budget eliminates second.** You cannot build a $50,000 custom solution on a $500 budget. Budget removes Build from the table for most small businesses and removes Hire for the smallest ones. Be honest about what you can spend — including hidden costs — before evaluating the remaining options.

  **Technical Capability is the tiebreaker.** When two options both pass the data and budget tests, your team's ability to maintain the solution long-term determines the winner. A bought SaaS tool that the vendor maintains beats a custom build that nobody on your team can update when something breaks.

  **Speed and Uniqueness are nudge factors.** They push you toward one option over another but rarely eliminate an option entirely. Need results fast? Nudge toward Buy. Problem is genuinely unique? Nudge toward Build or Hire. These factors matter, but they matter less than the three eliminators above.

  Here is the decision as a single sentence: **If your data is safe and your budget fits, Buy.** Only move to Hire or Build when you have a genuine reason — not a feeling, but a reason supported by the five-factor scoring matrix — that buying does not solve your specific problem. The default is Buy. Everything else is the exception.

  Write your five-factor scores down before you make a call. Decisions made in meetings with a vendor in the room are influenced by the vendor. Decisions made alone with your scores on paper are influenced by data. One of these leads to better outcomes. It is not the vendor meeting.

  The build-vs-buy decision is one you will make repeatedly as your AI adoption grows. The first time takes the longest because you are learning the framework. By the third time, you will score the five factors in 5 minutes, check the matrix, and have your answer. This efficiency is the point — a framework that you can apply quickly and consistently produces better decisions than ad hoc analysis every time a new tool or vendor appears.
