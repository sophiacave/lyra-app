  # Analytics and Reporting.

  Turn raw data into clear insights. AI reads the spreadsheet so you don't have to.


  ### After this lesson you'll know


    - How to analyze marketing data with AI (even if you hate spreadsheets)

    - Generating executive-ready reports in minutes

    - Extracting actionable insights from customer feedback

    - KPI dashboards and trend analysis with AI




  Data Analysis
  ## Paste your data. Get insights.

  You don't need to know statistics. You don't need pivot tables. Copy your marketing data from any source — Google Analytics, your email platform, social media insights — and ask AI to find the story:


    Here's my marketing data for the past 3 months:

[Paste your data — CSV, table, or just numbers]

Analyze this and tell me:
1. What are the 3 most important trends?
2. What's working best? (highest ROI channels/campaigns)
3. What's underperforming? (where am I wasting money/time?)
4. Any surprising patterns I might not notice?
5. What should I do differently next month?

Present findings as:
- Executive summary (3 sentences)
- Key metrics table
- Top 3 recommendations with specific actions

Be direct. Tell me the hard truths, not just the good news.



  Reports
  ## Generate executive-ready reports in 10 minutes.



    Create a monthly marketing report for my CEO.

Data: [Paste key metrics]
Goals this month: [What you set out to achieve]
Actual results: [What happened]

Format:
1. Executive summary (3 bullet points — wins, challenges, next steps)
2. Key metrics vs goals (table: metric, goal, actual, % change)
3. Channel performance breakdown (what worked where)
4. Top-performing content (which posts/emails/ads won)
5. Recommendations for next month (3 specific actions)

Keep it to 1 page. CEOs don't read long reports.
Use plain language, not marketing jargon.
Lead with results, not activity.



  Customer Insights
  ## Extract gold from customer feedback.

  Got 200 customer reviews, survey responses, or support tickets? AI can find the patterns in minutes:



      → "Categorize these 100 customer reviews by theme. What are the top 5 things people love? Top 5 complaints?"
      → "Analyze these NPS survey comments. What drives promoters vs detractors?"
      → "Find the exact words customers use to describe our product. I want their language, not marketing speak — for use in our ads."
      → "What features are customers requesting most? Rank by frequency and urgency."


  **Privacy note:** Remember to anonymize customer data before pasting into consumer AI tools. Strip names and emails. (See our AI Ethics course for the full privacy playbook.)


  Trend Spotting
  ## Spot trends before your competitors do.

  Use AI to monitor and analyze trends:

    - Paste industry news articles and ask: "What are the 3 biggest trends these articles point to?"

    - Share your website analytics over time and ask: "What content topics are growing in traffic?"

    - Paste social media comments and ask: "What questions keep coming up? What content should I create to answer them?"

    - Share competitor updates and ask: "What strategic moves are my competitors making? What do they suggest about market direction?"


  The marketers who win aren't the ones with the most data — they're the ones who **act on insights fastest.** AI makes you that marketer.


  KPI Dashboard
  ## The 10 marketing KPIs that actually matter.

  Most marketers track too many metrics and act on none. Here are the 10 that drive decisions:




        1

          Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC)
          Total marketing spend divided by new customers acquired. The number that tells you if your marketing is sustainable.



        2

          Conversion Rate
          Percentage of visitors who take the desired action. Track by channel, by campaign, by landing page.



        3

          Email Open Rate
          Industry average is 20-25%. Below that? Your subject lines need work. Way above? Your list might be too small to be meaningful.



        4

          Email Click-Through Rate (CTR)
          Industry average is 2-3%. This tells you if your email body copy and CTA are compelling enough to drive action.



        5

          Return on Ad Spend (ROAS)
          Revenue generated per dollar spent on ads. A ROAS of 4:1 means every $1 in ads produces $4 in revenue.



        6

          Organic Traffic Growth
          Month-over-month growth in search traffic. The compound interest of content marketing — slow to start, powerful over time.



        7

          Social Engagement Rate
          Interactions divided by impressions. A post with 1,000 impressions and 50 interactions has a 5% engagement rate — excellent.



        8

          Lead-to-Customer Rate
          What percentage of leads actually become paying customers? This tells you if your nurture sequence is working.



        9

          Customer Lifetime Value (LTV)
          How much revenue a customer generates over their entire relationship with you. LTV must exceed CAC or the business model breaks.



        10

          Net Promoter Score (NPS)
          Would your customers recommend you? Promoters (9-10) minus Detractors (0-6). Above 50 is excellent. Above 70 is world-class.






  Report Template
  ## The weekly marketing report prompt.

  Use this prompt every Friday to create a quick weekly snapshot. Paste it alongside your week's data:


    Create a weekly marketing snapshot report.

This week's data: [Paste numbers from all channels]
Last week's numbers: [For comparison]
Monthly goals: [What you are tracking toward]

Format:
1. Traffic light summary (green/yellow/red for each channel)
2. Week-over-week changes (what went up, what went down)
3. Top-performing piece of content this week (and why)
4. Worst-performing piece of content this week (and why)
5. One action item for next week based on this data

Keep it under 300 words. This is a snapshot, not a novel.
If something is declining for 3+ weeks, flag it as a trend.

  **Pro tip:** Save your weekly reports in a running document. After 4 weeks, ask AI to analyze all four weekly reports together and produce a monthly trends analysis. Patterns invisible in a single week become obvious across a month.


  Review
  ## Review the executive report structure.


### AI Marketing Report Structure

**Card 1:**
Front: Section 1: Executive Summary
Back: 3 bullet points — wins, challenges, next steps. Keep it to 3 sentences max. CEOs do not read long reports.

**Card 2:**
Front: Section 2: Key Metrics vs Goals
Back: A table with: Metric, Goal, Actual, and Percent Change. Make wins and misses visible at a glance.

**Card 3:**
Front: Section 3: Channel Performance
Back: What worked on which channel? Break down performance by email, social, paid, organic, etc.

**Card 4:**
Front: Section 4: Top-Performing Content
Back: Which specific posts, emails, or ads won? Celebrate and understand what drove performance.

**Card 5:**
Front: Section 5: Recommendations
Back: 3 specific actions for next month. Lead with results, not activity. Plain language, not marketing jargon.
