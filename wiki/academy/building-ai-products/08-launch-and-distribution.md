  # Launch and Distribution

  Nobody cares about your product until you make them care.
  Building is 30% of the work. Getting it in front of the right people is the other 70%. AI products have unique distribution advantages — if you know how to use them.


  ### What you'll learn


    - The three-wave launch strategy for AI products

    - Why "show the output" is the best marketing strategy

    - Channels that work for AI product distribution

    - Building in public as a distribution engine




  Strategy
  ## The Three-Wave Launch

  **Wave 1 — Inner circle (Week -2):** Give your product to 20-50 people you know. Friends, colleagues, Twitter mutuals. Ask them to break it. This isn't about growth — it's about catching embarrassing failures before strangers see them.
  **Wave 2 — Community seeding (Week 0):** Post in the communities where your target users already gather. Product Hunt, Hacker News, relevant subreddits, Discord servers, LinkedIn groups. Don't post "check out my AI tool." Post the output. Show the magic trick.
  **Wave 3 — Content flywheel (Week 1+):** Turn your product's outputs into content. Screenshots of results, before/after comparisons, user testimonials, use case breakdowns. Every piece of content demonstrates the product without being an ad.


  Tactic
  ## Show the Output, Not the Tool

  AI products have a superpower that traditional SaaS doesn't: the output is inherently shareable. A beautifully generated design, a perfectly structured summary, a surprisingly accurate analysis — these are their own marketing.
  Add a subtle watermark or "made with [your product]" tag on outputs. Make sharing frictionless — one-click export to social media, email-ready formats, embeddable widgets. Every output your user shares is a free ad that demonstrates value instead of claiming it.


  ### Distribution Channels Ranked for AI Products

  **High ROI:** Twitter/X (AI community is huge), Product Hunt, niche subreddits, YouTube demos
  **Medium ROI:** LinkedIn (for B2B), Hacker News, Discord communities, SEO/blog content
  **Low ROI (for now):** Paid ads (CAC too high for early stage), cold email, influencer partnerships


  Method
  ## Building in Public

  Document your journey building the AI product. Share your architecture decisions, your prompt engineering insights, your cost breakdowns. The AI builder community is hungry for real, transparent content. Every post attracts potential users who think "if they're this thoughtful about building it, the product must be good."
  Share numbers. "We hit 100 users this week. Average query cost dropped from $0.08 to $0.03 after prompt optimization. Here's how." This kind of content builds trust, attracts users, and establishes authority simultaneously.

  Reality
  ## Launch Day Is Just Day One

  Most AI products don't go viral on launch day. They grow through consistent demonstration of value over weeks and months. The Product Hunt launch gets you 500 visitors. The Twitter thread that shows a mind-blowing output gets you 5,000. The SEO article that ranks for "best AI tool for [your niche]" gets you 500 visitors every month forever.
  Plan for sustained distribution, not a single moment. The launch is a starting gun, not the finish line.


  Checklist
  ## The Pre-Launch Checklist

  Launching without preparation is like opening a restaurant before the kitchen is ready. Complete these before you announce anything publicly.
  **Product readiness:** The core workflow works reliably on 20+ test cases. Error messages are clear and helpful. Loading states communicate progress. The happy path is smooth and the failure path is graceful.
  **Infrastructure readiness:** Error tracking is active (Sentry or equivalent). Analytics events fire correctly. Cost monitoring has alerts set. Rate limiting protects against abuse. Your database can handle 10x your expected day-one traffic.
  **Content readiness:** Landing page clearly communicates the magic trick. Pricing page is live with working payment links. Three demo outputs are prepared for social sharing. A "how it works" video or GIF is ready (under 60 seconds).
  **Support readiness:** You have a way for users to report issues (email, Discord, or in-app chat). FAQ covers the top 5 questions you anticipate. You have a process for handling the first 24 hours of feedback — even if that process is just "check email every 2 hours."
  **Legal readiness:** Privacy policy exists and covers AI-specific concerns (how you handle user data, whether inputs are used for training). Terms of service are in place. If your AI handles sensitive data, you've reviewed relevant compliance requirements.


  Strategy
  ## Platform-Specific Launch Playbooks

  Each platform has its own culture, rules, and optimal posting strategies. What works on Twitter fails on Reddit. What works on LinkedIn fails on Product Hunt. Adapt your message to the medium.
  **Product Hunt:** Launch on Tuesday, Wednesday, or Thursday (highest traffic days). Have 5-10 supporters ready to upvote and comment at launch time. Your tagline should be outcome-focused, not feature-focused. Create a compelling "maker's comment" that tells the story behind the product. Include a special offer for Product Hunt users.
  **Twitter/X:** Lead with the output, not the announcement. A tweet showing a before/after transformation outperforms "I just launched my AI product" by 10x. Use a thread format: Hook (the problem) → Demo (the magic trick) → Social proof (beta user quote) → CTA (try it free). Post between 9-11am EST for maximum engagement.
  **Reddit:** Never post "check out my product" — it will be removed and you'll be banned. Instead, share a genuine insight about the problem you solve. "I analyzed 500 customer support tickets and found that 60% could be automated. Here's what I learned." Mention your product naturally in the comments, not the post.
  **LinkedIn:** Works best for B2B AI products. Write a personal story about why you built it. "I watched my team spend 3 hours every Friday compiling reports. Last week, I built something that does it in 30 seconds." LinkedIn rewards vulnerability and narrative. Include a screenshot of the output, not a link (LinkedIn suppresses posts with external links).
  **Hacker News:** The audience is technical and skeptical. Don't oversell. A "Show HN" post with an honest description — including limitations — performs far better than marketing language. Be in the comments answering technical questions. The HN audience respects builders who engage candidly.


  Flywheel
  ## The Content Flywheel for AI Products

  AI products have a unique content advantage: the product itself generates content. Every output is a potential marketing asset. Build a system that turns product usage into distribution fuel.
  **User-generated showcases:** Ask users for permission to share their best outputs (anonymized if necessary). "See what our users built this week" is more compelling than any marketing copy you could write. Create a gallery or a weekly showcase newsletter.
  **Tutorial content:** Write "How to use [your product] for [specific use case]" articles. These rank for long-tail SEO queries and demonstrate value simultaneously. Each article is both content marketing and a user guide. Aim for one per week.
  **Comparison content:** "How [your product] compares to doing it manually" with real time-savings data. This content targets users actively searching for solutions and provides the evidence they need to convert. Be honest about limitations — credibility converts better than hype.
  **Behind-the-scenes:** Share your prompt engineering process, your architecture decisions, your cost breakdowns. The AI builder community consumes this content voraciously, and many builders are also potential users or recommenders.


  Growth
  ## Post-Launch Growth Levers

  After the launch spike subsides, sustained growth requires deliberate strategy. These are the growth levers that work specifically for AI products.
  **Output watermarking:** A subtle "Made with [Product Name]" on every shareable output. Canva grew significantly through watermarked free-tier outputs. When users share their AI-generated content, your product gets credited. Make the watermark small enough to be acceptable but visible enough to be noticed.
  **Referral programs:** "Give 20 free queries, get 20 free queries." Referral programs work especially well for AI products because the product is easy to demonstrate — the referrer can show the referred person a specific output and say "look what this did."
  **Integration partnerships:** Build integrations with tools your users already use — Slack, Notion, Google Workspace, Zapier. Each integration puts your product in front of that platform's user base. Zapier alone has 6 million users browsing for AI automations.
  **SEO for AI queries:** People search for "AI [your category] tool" in growing numbers. Create landing pages optimized for these queries. "AI meeting notes tool" gets 12,000 searches/month and growing. A top-3 ranking for your category's search term is a permanent growth engine.


  Measurement
  ## Launch Metrics That Matter

  Track these metrics during your first 30 days to gauge launch health and inform your post-launch strategy.
  **Traffic to signup conversion:** What percentage of landing page visitors create an account? Industry average for AI tools is 3-8%. Below 3% means your messaging isn't compelling. Above 8% means your positioning is excellent.
  **Signup to first-use conversion:** What percentage of signups actually try the product? Target: above 60%. If people sign up but never use it, your onboarding has too much friction. Reduce steps between signup and first AI interaction to an absolute minimum.
  **First-use to second-use conversion:** The most critical metric. What percentage of users who try the product once come back for a second session? Target: above 40%. Below that, either the first experience disappointed them or the output wasn't valuable enough to build a habit.
  **Channel attribution:** Track where your users come from. Which community post drove the most signups? Which tweet converted best? Double down on what works and cut what doesn't. In the first 30 days, concentrate your distribution effort on the 2-3 channels that show the highest conversion.


  Mindset
  ## The Distribution Mindset for Solo Builders

  If you're building and distributing alone, you can't do everything. The key is choosing the right 2-3 channels and being consistent, rather than spreading yourself thin across 10 channels and doing none of them well.
  **Pick your primary channel:** Choose the one platform where your target users are most concentrated and most active. For B2B AI products, that's usually LinkedIn or industry-specific communities. For developer tools, it's Twitter/X or Hacker News. For consumer products, it's TikTok or Instagram. Go deep on one channel before going wide.
  **Create once, repurpose everywhere:** Write one in-depth piece of content per week — a blog post, a thread, or a video. Then slice it into smaller pieces for other channels. The blog post becomes 5 tweets, a LinkedIn post, a Reddit comment, and a newsletter issue. One hour of creation, five hours of distribution value.
  **Consistency beats virality:** One post per week for a year (52 posts) builds a more reliable audience than one viral post followed by silence. The algorithm rewards consistency. Your audience rewards reliability. Show up every week with something useful, and distribution compounds.
  **Batch your distribution work:** Set aside one morning per week for all distribution activities. Write content, schedule posts, respond to comments, engage in communities. Don't let distribution creep into your building time every day — it fragments focus and produces worse results in both areas.


  Reality
  ## Common Launch Mistakes

  **Launching too quietly:** You build something incredible and tell 5 people about it. Then you wonder why nobody signs up. You need to push through the discomfort of self-promotion. Nobody will discover your product by accident.
  **Launching too loudly:** You spend $2,000 on ads before you have a single organic user. Paid acquisition before product-market fit is burning money. Get 100 organic users who love your product first. Then consider paid distribution to accelerate what's already working.
  **Launching once:** You post on Product Hunt, get 200 visitors, and declare the launch a failure. Launches aren't events — they're campaigns. Post on Product Hunt this week. Share a thread next week. Publish a case study the week after. Each piece compounds on the last.
  **Ignoring early feedback:** Your first 50 users are the most valuable focus group you'll ever have. They chose to try your product when it had no reviews, no track record, and no brand. Treat their feedback as gold. Reply to every message. Fix their top complaint within 48 hours. Turn early users into evangelists by making them feel heard.


  Summary
  ## The Distribution Equation

  Launch and distribution success boils down to a simple equation: the right message, to the right audience, at the right time, through the right channel. Miss any one of these and conversions suffer.
  **Right message:** Show the output, not the technology. Lead with the transformation. "This meeting summary was generated in 8 seconds" hits harder than "our AI-powered meeting assistant uses advanced NLP."
  **Right audience:** Ten people who desperately need your product are worth more than 10,000 who might be curious. Focus your distribution on the communities where your ideal user already hangs out and is actively looking for solutions.
  **Right time:** Post when your audience is online. Engage when conversations about your problem space are active. Launch when you have enough product quality to create a strong first impression — not before.
  **Right channel:** Each channel has different norms, different audiences, and different content formats that perform best. Master one channel before expanding to others. Depth beats breadth in early-stage distribution.
  Distribution is not a one-time event. It's a muscle you build over months. The launch gets your first 100 users. Your sustained distribution effort gets the next 10,000. Plan accordingly.
  The skills you build in this lesson — creating compelling demonstrations, choosing the right channels, engaging communities authentically, and measuring what works — compound over the lifetime of your product. A founder who masters distribution is worth ten who only know how to code.
  With your product launched and users flowing in, the real work begins. Lesson 9 covers how to measure what matters, iterate on quality, and use data to continuously improve your AI product's output and user experience.
  Remember: the launch is the starting gun, not the finish line. The real growth happens in the weeks and months after launch, through consistent demonstration of value, genuine community engagement, and relentless iteration on the product itself.


  Tactic
  ## Email Marketing for AI Products

  Email remains the highest-converting channel for SaaS products, including AI. Build your email list from day one — even before you have a product to sell.
  **The waitlist email sequence:** When someone joins your waitlist, don't just confirm and go silent. Send a 3-email sequence over the next week. Email 1: Confirm signup and share your vision (day 0). Email 2: Share a behind-the-scenes look at how the AI works (day 3). Email 3: Ask them about their specific use case — this is market research disguised as engagement (day 7).
  **Onboarding emails:** After signup, send a 5-email sequence that drives users toward their aha moment. Email 1: Welcome + quickstart (immediate). Email 2: Tips for best results (day 1). Email 3: Advanced use case inspiration (day 3). Email 4: Invite to share feedback (day 7). Email 5: Upgrade prompt for free-tier users (day 14).
  **Win-back emails:** When a user hasn't logged in for 14 days, send a re-engagement email showing what they're missing. "We've improved our output quality by 30% since your last visit" or "3 new features launched this month." Include a direct link to the product — eliminate every click between the email and the experience.
  **Feature announcement emails:** Every significant improvement is a re-engagement opportunity. Don't just list features — show before/after outputs. "Our summarization just got 2x more accurate. Here's the same document processed with the old and new versions." Demonstration beats announcement every time.


  Strategy
  ## Strategic Partnerships for Distribution

  Partnerships can accelerate distribution faster than any organic channel. The key is finding partners whose audience overlaps with yours but who aren't competitors.
  **Tool integration partnerships:** If your AI product complements an existing tool (a summarizer that integrates with a note-taking app, an analyzer that connects to a CRM), propose a partnership. The existing tool gets a feature they didn't build. You get access to their user base. Both win.
  **Content creator partnerships:** Find YouTubers, bloggers, or newsletter authors who cover your niche. Offer them free access in exchange for an honest review. One genuine review from a trusted voice in your niche converts better than $1,000 in ads.
  **Agency partnerships:** Marketing agencies, consulting firms, and freelancers who serve your target market can become your best distribution channel. If they recommend your tool to every client, one agency partnership equals 50 individual sales. Offer them a referral commission or a white-label option.
  **Marketplace listings:** List your product on every relevant marketplace and directory. AI tool directories (There's an AI for That, Futurepedia), platform marketplaces (Zapier, Slack App Directory), and industry-specific directories. Each listing is a permanent, free distribution channel.
